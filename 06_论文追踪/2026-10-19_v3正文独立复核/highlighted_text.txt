ABSTRACTTwo system-agnostic relations in MST-wi, a Web-system metamorphic testing framework, gate
their verdict on aGUI-reachability model—a crawl-time snapshot of what the interface permits—so what
they establish about object-level authorization depends on when the model was read and what the adjudicator
may consult. We study them and tie each limit to its evidence. Source inspection shows the engine records
only that a relation failed: promotion to aproven violationis added policy, stated here explicitly. On a 576-
record constructed study, a legitimate access whose grant lies outside the framework’s record is reported
as proven in 8 of 8 alarms; the same failure appears when the grant is made between crawl and request, in
the temporal experiments below. Once a readable, sound oracle is supplied, the relation adds no observed
adjudication benefit over the direct consumer of the same oracle and outcome (the paired methods disagree
on 3 of 216 true-violation records, each time favouring the plain request); and the published specificity of
99.81% is an input-level figure whose denominator cannot, on its own, expose a class-uniform failure.
Two experiments carry these claims further. A pre-registered temporal experiment on Gitea 1.22.6 and
GitLab CE 17.11.7 commits 16 authorisation transitions over 712 cross-credential cells, with entitlement
and effect agreeing on 712: 29 stale false proofs appear, and a recrawl with the same credentials removes 29.
A real vulnerable/fixed pair (Gitea 1.20.5 to 1.20.6) shows the independent entitlement/effect gate reporting
1 true positive on the vulnerable build, 0 false proofs, and no alarm on the fixed build—a check of the gate,
not a measure of detection effectiveness.

I. INTRODUCTION
B
ROKEN object-level authorization (BOLA), first in the
OW ASP API Security Top 10 [1], is the failure to en-
force, per object, that the authenticated subject is entitled to
the object touched. Detecting it is anoracleproblem: vulner-
able and correct systems answer with the same success status.
Metamorphic testing is an established approach to that oracle
problem, and MST-wi [2] is a substantial system-agnostic in-
stance for Web systems: 76 system-agnostic relations (MRs)
over 55 Web-specific predicates, reported to detect 85% of
vulnerabilities with specificity 99.81%.
This paper asks what happens to that claim when the
asserted property is object-level authorization. The predi-
cates do not express entitlement; they express what their
authors call reachability—what ‘‘the functionality exposed by
the user interface’’ permits—with the role-to-resource map-
ping ‘‘not always available’’ [2]. We take that sentence as
premise, not discovery. Delegating adjudication to a reacha-
bility model makes the moment the model was read part of the
specification, so we instantiate the predicatesas defined—
over a model recorded at crawl time—and drive the real
systems through three phases: crawl, change authorization out
of band, execute. The middle phase is the one the original
evaluation does not exercise. The constructed study isolates
the same defect without time: it seeds a share outside the
framework’s record and asks whether a gate that cannot see it
still calls the access legal.
We ran a 576-record scenario/configuration study on a
constructed system and a controlled study on Gitea 1.22.6,
extended, with a methodological variation, to Gogs 0.14.3 and
GitLab 17.11.7. We propose no mechanism. What survives is
three findings, one per research question (Section VI).

producesdeterministic false proofs—a legitimate access the

The error is a property of thereading, not of the system.
(2)Oracle (RQ2).When a readable, sound authorization

The published labels are manually verified; the limit is the

II. BACKGROUND: THE PREDICATES IN QUESTION
For authorization there is no intrinsic oracle: vulnerable
and correct executions both answer with success. Meta-
morphic testing replaces the absent oracle with a rela-
tion over multiple executions—and the relation is only as
sound as the assumptions it needs, which for authorization
are exactly the facts the oracle was supposed to supply.
MST-wi [2], building on Mai et al. [3], crawls a per-user
model of reachable URLs and contents, then checks MRs
against that recorded model. In the artifact we inspected,
the predicates guarding an authorization MR read either
the recorded model directly (cannotReachThroughGUI,
userCanRetrieveContent) or static subject properties;
we call a predicateGUI-derivedwhen its value is a function
of the crawl.
Why the false proof is a design consequence, not a bug
Suppose a subject can reach an object at crawl time, so the
record stores it as reachable. Afterwards an administrator
grants the same subject access to afurtherobject through
a channel—an administrative endpoint or a share link—that
a crawl of the subject’s own interface never traverses. At
execution the guard still reads the frozen record, finds the
new object absent, and concludes the subject cannot reach
it; the relation then treats the successful response as a vio-
lation. Every step is exactly as specified, so no guard is mis-
written: the predicate’s two representations—‘‘reachable as
recorded’’ and ‘‘not reachable as recorded’’—cannot express
‘‘reachable, but only after the record was taken’’. Recrawling
thesameinterface does not remove the false proof, because
the grant need not be visible through the subject’s interface
at all; only a recrawl that can observe the granting channel
removes it, which is what the temporal experiment does when
a privileged recrawl repairs all 29 stale cells. The reported
specificity does not register the failure either, because it is a
property of the adjudication’sreading momentrather than of
any single generated input. This is the mechanism the rest of
the paper measures, and the reason we speak of a boundary of
the specification rather than a defect of an implementation.
Two census figures frame what follows. Over the 76 cat-
alog MRs, 38 (50.0%) gate on a GUI-derived or identity
predicate, and only 14 (18.42%) are authorization-relevant by
name or action—an upper bound, reported as a caveat; a full-
text search for predicates reading thestate of an authorization
artifact(ownership, permission record, share) finds 0 occur-
rences. Within the examined vocabulary, then, the authoriza-
tion MRs have no authorization-artifact state observable to
decide on, and the reading moment enters the specification:

III. PROTOCOL: A FAITHFUL THREE-PHASE
INSTANTIATION
Two instruments carry distinct claims. The three-phase in-
stantiation below carries the quantitative results of Sec-
tions IV and V; a direct execution of the library’s own
relations—source unmodified, with the build configuration
and a replay harness added—carries one qualitative claim,
that the published code produces the false proof we pre-
dict (Section VII-C). Neither instrument is a benchmark: the
standing scope and non-claims are collected in Section VIII,
and neither yields a claim of detector accuracy.
Three phases
(A)Crawl: record, per subject and object, whether the subject
can reach the object through the interface, and freeze that
record—the model the predicates will consult. (B)Change
authorization out of band: alter a (subject, object) entitlement
through a channel outside the crawled interface; the frozen
model is not updated, because the architecture has no mecha-
nism to update it. (C)Execute the MRagainst the current state
while the predicates consult the frozen model. Phase B is what
the original evaluation does not exercise, and it is where the
reachability stand-in can be wrong (Fig. 1).
Adjudicator variants and internal controls
A raw alarm becomes a verdict through an exception pol-
icy, and different policies are different methods:raw(the
alarm itself, with no exception handling at all—this is the
baseline any filter must be read against);v1(anexistence
filter: any sharing record on the object downgrades the alarm,
regardless of whom it names or what it permits);v2(ex-
istence plus operation coverage: the record must cover the
operation the MR triggered);v3(state-calibrated—consults
a readable authorization introspection surface, abstains when
none is readable);degrade_all(the floor: abstain on
every alarm);direct_only(authorization surface only,
access outcome ignored—a deliberately unfair control); and
direct_observe(a succeeding plain request checked
against the same readable surface). The last consumes the
same observable quantity as the MR family and decides the
negative result.
Ground truth
Every quantitative result names its ground-truth source, and
it is never the detector under test: construction state for
the constructed system and the scenario/configuration study;
system-specific live re-probes of effective authorization (the
per-system validation procedure is described in Section V) for
Gitea, Gogs and GitLab; the pinned route tables for the grant
census; and the seed list for the seeded target.
Unknown and failure handling
Each predicate can fail to yield a bit, and the handling is fixed
by the pre-registered protocol before any cell is observed,
not negotiated per result. The three failure modes are distinct
and are stated as implemented.Transport-level retries:an
HTTP request that fails to connect (status 0) is re-issued
up to two more times; this retry exists for the network, not
for the transaction.E undefined:an entitlement-changing
transaction that is refused or otherwise does not commit is
notretried at the transaction level—the runner issues it once,
and the cell is markedfailed(reasonmutation_failed)
and excluded from every denominator, with the exclusion
count reported alongside the result.Y undefined:an execution
error or timeout leaves the cell unscored, never coerced to
allowordeny—in the native engine run, 11 relations were
attempted, 10 executed and 1 errored, and the errored one
is reported rather than silently dropped from the attempted
count.O undefined:the introspection surface may return no
definite answer; the state-calibrated method abstains on those
cells by construction, and the channel table reports them in
an explicitunansweredcolumn (56 Gitea and 72 GitLab
cells for the anonymous reader, 0 on Gogs’ authenticated
reader) instead of absorbing them intodeny. On those cells
EandYremain scoreable—an unanswered oracle removes
the oracle-based adjudicators, not the cell. The twelve-row
truth table is defined overE∈ {allow,deny}; anE-undefined
cell would fall outside its rows and be reported as excluded,
not reassigned.Eitself isunknownwhen an object is ab-
sent from the ledger model. For a modelled object with no
recorded granting channel, the pre-registered policy promised
unknown(‘‘the ledger models no channel for the triple’’),
while the shipped implementation returns a genuinedenyfor
that case, reservingunknownfor the absent-object case and
for the one channel the protocol singles out as unmodellable
(deploy keys, which would have to surface as unknown). We
disclose this protocol-to-implementation divergence rather
than reconcile it silently. In the reported runs the two readings
coincide in effect: the channel space of every modelled object
is fixed by the setup ledger, no deploy-key-reachable cell
exists in the topology, and the 203 Gitea and 338 GitLab
deny cells under the default-deny stratum are exactly the cells
whose grant rows are absent—so no cell’s verdict depends on
which reading is taken. Were an unmodelled channel to arise,
the contract marks the cellunknownrather than guessing. In
the reported runs the failure-exclusion exits were never taken:
all 712 temporal cells and both Gogs readings committed,
E=Ythroughout, and zero cells were excluded as failed; the
unanswered-oracleexits, by contrast, were taken, and their
counts are the ones given above. The contract would bind any
rerun on a degraded deployment. Missing or modified input
records fail the pipeline outright (SHA-256 manifests over
inputs and outputs are shipped with the artefact); the pipeline
never substitutes a default value for an absent record.

A. WHAT THE DESIGN VARIES, AND WHAT IT CAN
IDENTIFY
Because the exception policy and the reachability reading
are thrown per record rather than held per configuration,
every record has a partner that differs in that switch alone.
Table 2 uses that: it pairs records on configuration, scenario,
relation and the other switch, and reports the discordant pairs.
A contrast carried by some other factor would show up as
movement in the paired cells; a contrast that is not there shows
up as a zero.
Three results come out of it, and they are stronger than the
aggregate rates they explain. First, the reachability reading
moves the outcome in one direction only: of 288 blind–
aware pairs, 12 lose a false proof when the reading is aware
and 0 gain one, while the same switch costs 3 detections
and gains none. The aware reading is therefore a trade in
a fixed direction, not noise around a rate. It is a statement
about what the gate can see, not about when authorization
changed: the constructed study seeds the out-of-band share
before the run and never mutates entitlement inside it, so
the temporal reading of this contrast belongs to Section V,
not here. Second, the exception ladder hasnodiscordant pair
at either step: raw and e1 differ on no individual record,
and neither do e1 and e1e2. That is a stronger statement
than the aggregate rate not moving—20 records carry a false
proof under all three policies—and it says the two exception
predicates are, on this system, inert rather than merely weak.
Third, oracle readability acts only where there is something to
resolve: in the violating configuration 54 of 78 paired records
change fromviolation-proventoindeterminate
when the surface is switched off, none the other way; in the
correct configuration 78 pairs change not at all.
What the design cannot identify follows from the two
nested dimensions, and we prefer to name it. The interaction
between channel visibility and readability is not estimable,
because the listed-and-unreadable cell was not run; the read-
ability contrast is identified within unlisted only, and we make
no claim that readability moderates the listed channel. The
legitimate-sharing mechanism is not identified under S6–S7,
where it is undefined by construction, so the out-of-band es-
timates are for the e3 configurations alone. The two relations
are not orthogonal to scenario—S5 carries MR-002 only—so
we never compare relations with each other. What remains
is balanced: every (exception policy, reachability reading)
slice holds 52 readable and 44 unreadable records, so the
readability contrast cannot be carried by either thrown switch.

IV. SYNTHETIC STUDY: WHERE THE ADJUDICATION
GOES
This study supplies the evidence for Findings 1 and 3, and the
ablation behind Finding 2; the findings themselves are stated
in Section VI.
A. DEFINITIONS
Confusions are defined over theverdict: afalse
proofis the strict subset of false alarms promoted to
violation-proven—an error the tool asserts as
established. We also report thedetection-keep rate(share of
fired true violations still alarmed) and count only abstentions
justified by an unreadable oracle.
B. WHAT THE CONSTRUCTED SYSTEM SHOWS
Attribution is clean
On the anchored configuration the false-proof rate is 2/2.
With that denominator the rate is unstable by construction,
so we attach to it no interval—nor a percentage, which would
travel out of context. With the legitimate-sharing exception
absent the rate is 0.00 (0/2), and with the channel listed it
is 0.00 (0/2). The phenomenon is therefore attributable to a
legitimate sharing exception the MR cannot know about, not
to execution itself. A2×2grid over channel visibility and
enforcement correctness tells the same story from the other
side: on unlisted cells the false-proof rate is 44.44% with
no false negatives; on listed cells it drops to 0.0% but one
of the five violating records in that cell is a false negative
(4 detected, 1 missed). Avoiding false proofs and keeping
detection are controlled by one switch, and the switch cannot
be thrown both ways at once.
Filtering exceptions does not help
The exception ladder—raw, one exception level, two—leaves
the anchored rate at 1.00, 1.00, 1.00: it does not move. The
exception levels we can express are themselves functions of
the same reachability model, so they inherit its blind spot.
The framework cannot see the channel that matters
When a legitimate share was created through the framework’s
facility, its log holds 12 records and v2 produces 0 false
proofs (rate 0.00); when the same share was created out of
band, the log holds 0 records—no evidence the share exists—
and v2’s false proofs rise to 8 of 8 alarms (rate 1.00), every
one promoted toviolation-proven. The MR cannot
distinguish ‘‘this share does not exist’’ from ‘‘this share exists
but was not crawled’’; its only representation of the first is the
second. On S7, where a genuine violationdoessit behind the
out-of-band share, the same blindness makes the alarm right
for the wrong reason (6 of 6 reached)—and that coincidence
of alarms with violations is what an input-level metric cannot
unpack. The same tension appears within a single MR: for S3
(must not alarm) vs S5 (must alarm), no configuration in the
family is correct on both—the choice of switch is a choice of
whicherror to make (Fig. 2).
Stratification, not aggregation
Aggregated, v3 looks best (recovery 1.00 against
degrade_all’s keep rate 0.00); the reading is wrong,
because v3 changes regime with oracle readability. Stratified
over the 36-record readable grid and its 18-record absent
grid (a representative grid; Section IV -F re-runs the same
comparison over all 576 records): on the readable grid it
removes v2’s false proofs (0 vs. 4) while still alarming on all
9 fired true violations; on the absent grid (18) it alarms on
none of the 5 true violations v2 still alarms on, abstaining
13 times. Pooling converts honest abstention into apparent
performance, so v3’s numbers may appear only stratified.
C. FAIR ABLATION: DOES THE METAMORPHIC STEP ADD
ADJUDICATION INFORMATION?
Our earlier iteration proposed v3 as a mechanism, resting on
the claim that the metamorphic step was necessary. We tested
that claim by removing the step and substituting the most
naive consumer of the same observable. The distinction mat-
ters:direct_only—oracle yes, access outcome ignored—
produces 108 false proofs against v3’s 0 (precision 0.50), and
rejecting it proves nothing because it discards half the evi-
dence. The fair control isdirect_observe: a succeeding
ordinary request plus the same readable surface, consuming
exactly what the MR-based adjudicator consumes.
Table 3 concludes the ablation. The first three rows are a
ladder, and they must be read in order: with no exception
handling at all,rawyields 120 false proofs; the existence
filter v1 cuts them to 60, but pays for it by abstaining on
true violations (FN rises to 18); adding operation coverage
(v2) restores the true violations (FN 3) at the same false-
proof count. Neither filter is therefore a free improvement,
and v3’s zero in the FP column is bought with the FN column.
direct_observehas 0 false proofs against v3’s 0—a tie
at zero, not a win. The comparison is paired, so we report the
discordance rather than an inferential interval: over the 216

The abstentions are not all of one kind (Table 4, panel b).
156 are structural—the surface returns nothing, and there is

E. A PUBLISHED METRIC’S RESOLUTION: WHAT A
POOLED SPECIFICITY CANNOT REGISTER
The published figure is a specificity, not a false-alarm share
of all inputs. MST-wi defines it asTN/(TN + FP)over
the follow-up inputsF, and states that each reported MT
failure was manually verified [2]; the labels are therefore not
the crawler’s own answer. WithFN = 0throughout that
evaluation,TN =F−TP−FPand the figure equals
1−FP/(F−TP): the denominator is thenegativefollow-up
inputs, not every generated input. We use that form below, and
we do not attribute the published number to a circular label.
What such a figure cannot do is register a failure confined
to a class, and the two reasons are arithmetic and coverage
rather than labelling. First, the figure is pooled: a class con-
tributing a sharewof the negative inputs can move it by at
mostw, so a real class-uniform failure is diluted by unaf-
fected inputs. Second, the follow-up inputs are those the crawl
could enumerate; a class of legitimate access the crawl never
reaches contributes no negative inputs and therefore cannot
enter the ratio at all. The second point is the measurement
face of Finding 1, and it is a statement about the coverage of
the input set, not about the correctness of the human labels.
We cannot reproduce the engine’s exact input count, so we
substitute no value for the published figure; the argument
below is about the form of the denominator only.
A bounded formal remark on the denominator
WriteS= 1−FP/N −, whereN − = TN + FPis the
number of negative follow-up inputs the run generated, and
defineS scenario = 1−N failed/Nscenarios, whereN failed is the
number of scenarios in which the failure is uniform.S scenario
is an analytical construct for characterising resolution; it is
not proposed as a replacement metric. The remark concerns
resolution, not estimator consistency or power.

a fixed numbera≥1of false-alarm inputs, ands≥1

Proof. N − =fa+smandFP=fa; substitution gives the
first expression, which increases monotonically inmto1,
whereas the scenario-level figure depends only on the fixed

Corollary 1.No fixed input-level specificity thresholdt<1

abovetwhile theffailing scenarios remain failing.
The finding is therefore about what the published figure
can be asked to show, not about its arithmetic: it is pooled over
classes, and it is computed on the inputs the crawl reached.
Neither point disputes the reported value or its labels; both
limit what a high value certifies about a class the run did not
exercise.
Implementation robustness
An independently written implementation of the same pred-
icates was compared on a subset fixed in advance; it bounds
implementation accident only, and is reported with the con-

A. EIGHT COMMITTED TRANSITIONS WITH A FROZEN
MODEL, ON TWO PRODUCTS
The two-snapshot comparison above cannot separate two
explanations: the model went stale, or the crawl never listed
the channel in the first place. The transition experiment
separates them by construction. We ran eight authorisation
transitions on one fresh Gitea 1.22.6 instance and repeated
the same eight, under the same pre-registered protocol (hash
d09d904707aa) and the same ledger rules, on an indepen-
dent GitLab CE 17.11.7 instance. The mechanisms differ per
product—attaching a repository to a team becomes sharing
a project with a group; removing a team member becomes
removing a group member—so the two runs share a protocol,
not a topology, and we report them side by side rather than
pooled.
Each transition is a closed loop. We crawl every subject,
serialise the per-subject reachable set, freeze it asM 0 and
record its digest; commit one authorisation transaction and
increment the harness versionhvonly if the transaction re-
turns 2xx; re-crawl with thesamecredentials to obtainM 1;

C. FINDING 3 (RQ3): A POOLED SPECIFICITY CANNOT
REGISTER A CLASS IT DID NOT EXERCISE
The reported 99.81% is a specificity over follow-up inputs,
TN/(TN + FP), not a false-alarm share of all generated
inputs; in that evaluation the MT failures were manually ver-
ified, so the labels are not the crawler’s own. Its denominator
cannot, on its own, provide scenario-level resolution for a
failure confined to a class, and the inputs it counts are the
ones the crawl could enumerate.Both halves are consistent
with the reported value. The denominator: withFN = 0, the
figure is1−FP/(F−TP), so a failure uniform across a
class contributes only as many false positives as that class
emits negative inputs and is diluted by the rest of the run
(Proposition 1; Corollary 1: no fixed threshold can guarantee
that such a class forces the metric below it). The coverage: if
a class of legitimate access is never reached by the crawl, it
contributes no negative inputs, so the ratio is silent about it
even though the adjudication that consumes the crawl is not.
Nor does the report fix the weights: if a failing class supplies a
sharewof the negative inputs, the figure moves only between
1and1−w, so telling a small-share failure apart from no
failure requires an estimate ofwthat the published number
does not carry.
We are not saying the figure is false, that its labels are
wrong, or that the false positives were misdiagnosed: the
manual verification is exactly what makes the labels credible.
The claim is narrower—that a pooled figure computed on the
crawled input set does not by itself certify that a class outside
that set was exercised, and that a class-uniform failure can be
held above any fixed threshold by unaffected inputs.

E. ADMISSIBILITY, AND FALSIFICATION
Three properties must be kept apart. A surface isreadable
when its answer can be obtained from the vantage in question;
soundwhen its answer equals the true authorization state; and
admissible, for promotion toviolation-proven, when
(1) it is readableand(2) one can give a reason that it is not
of the same kind as the defect under test—not itself a reach-
ability artifact whose blind spot correlates with the blindness
tested. Readability is what makes soundness usable, but it nei-
ther implies soundness nor confers admissibility: the owner-
vantage authorization-introspection surface is readable on
every cell in each system (48 Gitea, 48 Gogs, 48 GitLab),
yet sound on Gitea alone—it agrees with effective access on
48, 31 and 30 of those cells respectively. The cross-vendor
systems are therefore not cases of ‘‘no oracle’’: a readable
membership surface exists on both, but it is unsound for the
authorization channels exercised here, so it fails clause (2).
Clause (2) is a per-system judgement and not automatable
from readability alone; the cross-vendor results make it con-
crete (Gogs and GitLab fail it; their adjudicators convert the
blind spots into 8 and 5 false proofs). Where the condition
holds, the honest method consumes the oracle directly and
drops the relation; where it does not, the honest method
abstains—as v3 does on the unreadable stratum, where the
aggregate table would have ranked it best, a ranking that
reflects abstention rather than improved detection.

VIII. THREATS TO VALIDITY
Source versus execution
We read the predicates at source (built under JDK 8 and
Maven; Section VII-B) and then executed the relations, un-
modified apart from the build configuration and our harness
(Section VII-C). The harness substitutes an HTTP observa-
tion channel—exact for the reachability predicate, not exer-
cised for the content predicate, whose invocation count we
report rather than claim cleanliness we did not demonstrate.
The run converts one class of claim: that a false proof is
produced by thepublishedrelations on a real system under
the promotion policyPof Section VII-C; it does not convert
the rest: the scenario/configuration and cell studies remain
statements about our instantiation, our exception vocabulary
belongs to the same predicate family, and we report no sensi-
tivity or specificity attributable to MST-wi.
Metric and statistics
Records within a design cell are not independent (Section III,
statistical treatment) and the records are a fixed enumeration,
so we report counts and rates rather than inferential intervals,
and no result rests on statistical significance. The RQ2 abla-
tion is paired and is reported as a discordance count. Finding 3
is a statement about what the published denominator pools
over, not about the value of any specificity figure: we do not
restate the predecessor’s specificity, and no conclusion in this
paper depends on it.
Two MRs, one family
We instantiated 2 MRs of one family—relations gated on the
absence of evidence. The native run lifts the count to 10 of
11 catalog authorization relations but not the family: only the
reachability-gated ones alarm in our run. This is the paper’s
single most important limitation; raising it requires relations
gated otherwise, a different experiment.
Real systems
Three systems (one lineage pair, one independent), chosen for
accessibility, none vulnerable on the tested surface—so only
the false-proof side is measured, within one domain, the git
forge. Channel proportions are topology design parameters,
not field prevalence. Constructed scenarios with construction-
defined ground truth are why the real-system section exists;
three systems tested once each are still not a population.
Stratification and the references used in it
The split of Section IV -F is on whether the introspection
surface returned a reading for the record, not on the config-
uration switch: 108 records sit in configurations that enable
introspection but whose probe returns nothing, so the switch
would misclassify them. The two oracle-free references there
are deliberately weak—one unconditional, one guessing from
a single request—because the point is to size the metamorphic
step against methods that need nothing, not to identify the
best oracle-free detector; a differential prober across accounts
consumes more than those references do and is instantiated
instead in the separate controlled study of Section VI-D.
Both references share an observable with the labelling rule,
asdirect_observedoes, so only the differences against
them—not their absolute rates—are meant to be read.
Our own earlier claims
An earlier iteration proposed v3 as a mechanism on the
strength of its precision (1.00), which was bought with 111
false negatives, and a control (direct_only, precision
0.50) that discarded the access result. Replacing it with a fair
control produced the negative result. We record this as the
condition any similar claim must meet: a control consuming
the same observable quantity as the ground truth.
IX. RELATED WORK
Metamorphic security testing
Metamorphic testing was brought to web security by Mai et
al. [3], who cast security test patterns—authentication, au-
thorization, session—as metamorphic relations so that faults
can be exposed without an oracle. The line matured into
MST-wi [2], whose 76 system-agnostic relations over a Web-

specific domain language are the object of our study. We
take the technique’s own admission that the role-to-resource
mapping is ‘‘not always available’’ at face value and follow
it to its consequence for what the adjudication can decide.
A relation whose guard reads a crawl-time model is sound
only while the model equals the authorization state, and that
equality is exactly what the technique cannot check for itself.
Detecting broken access control
A large and active literature detects access-control flaws
directly, and it is worth separating the oracles it relies on.
Black-box probing drives requests with and without the
rights of another subject and looks for a missing refusal [4],
[5], a line that runs back to AUTHSCOPE [6] and that
has been extended to deployed systems under ethical con-
straints [7]; where an API description is available, the oracle
itself can be synthesised, for instance by transforming an Ope-
nAPI specification into a colored Petri net that predicts per-
object access [8]. A complementary, white-box line recovers
theenforcedpolicy from the implementation—by reverse-
engineering RBAC policies [9], by statically extracting au-
thorization policies [10] or database access-control rules [11],
or by locating the permission checks themselves [12]—and
then flags violations against that recovered model: BOLA
in database-backed applications [13], missing-owner-check
and function-level flaws in Java web applications [14], [15],
function- and object-level access-control flaws across Web
applications [16], and privilege escalation in polyglot mi-
croservices [17]. Specification-driven API fuzzers generate
the requests well but inherit the same oracle difficulty [18]–
[20]; adding explicit access-policy violation checks [21]
moves them toward the detection problem but does not re-
move the need for a ground truth, and a recent turn dele-
gates generation or probing to language models [22]–[24].
A concurrent line reframes BOLA under a zero-trust model,
in which each request must carry an explicit authorization
decision rather than one inferred from endpoint reachabil-
ity [25]; our question sits at that decision level. Each of these
papers reports detections against an oracle it holds privately—
a policy, a seeded defect, a differential response—and none
of them is our comparison class, because we do not measure
detection rates. What this paper adds is three claims about
one published adjudication, each with a falsifier. Two of the
ingredients are already known—that a crawl-time model can
go stale, and that an authorization oracle can be unsound; the
claims below differ in that the first failure is deterministic and
attributable to a representation choice, the second is bounded
by an explicit equivalence rather than argued case by case, and
the third is a property of the published metric’s denominator.
First, a gate that reads a crawl-time model misreports a grant
the record does not contain, deterministically rather than
intermittently (Finding 1; the constructed enumeration). Re-
reading the surface repairs that misreport only when the grant
is one the surface would have shown on a second reading:
across the two products a same-credential recrawl removes
29 of 29 stale false proofs, leaving 0 live ones. When the
grant arrived through a channel the surface never exposes,
no re-reading of that surface repairs it—on Gogs 13 of 19
mismatching cells and 5 of 8 surface-gated false proofs persist
under live reading, all on public-visibility or team-derived
grants that never enter a subject’s personal crawl. Staleness
is repairable and channel blindness is not, and the two call
for different remedies. Second, once a readable, sound oracle
is supplied, the promoted verdict is a subset of direct adju-
dication on the evaluated domain, so the metamorphic step
adds no confirmation there (Proposition 2(b)); the falsifier is
a record the relation confirms and the direct consumer does
not. Third, the published specificity’s denominator is pooled
over the inputs a crawl could enumerate, so a failure confined
to a class outside that set does not move it (Proposition 1). We
claim no new detector, no new oracle, and no recall advantage
over the access-control line; what we claim is a statement
about what an oracle-substituting technique can establish, and
where that statement stops.
Direct authorization oracles, and what is at stake here
Comparing responses across principals or privilege states is
an established way to obtain an authorization oracle when
the ground-truth relation is available, and the black-box and
white-box lines above are instances of it. We propose no new
direct oracle, and we do not claim that direct authorization
comparison is novel. Our subject is a different question: what
an existing oracle-substituting techniquedecideswhen it sub-
stitutes reachability for entitlement. The direct oracle enters
our design in exactly one role—as the fair, same-observable
control that a necessity claim about the metamorphic step
must face. Without such a control, a substitution can appear
necessary only because the alternative it is compared against
was not allowed to consume the same decision-relevant ob-
servables.
LLMs and the oracle problem
LLMs are used for vulnerability detection and specifica-
tion inference [26]–[29], including lightweight models over
hybrid code features [30] and federated, explainable detec-
tors [31]; we use none in the detection loop. The transferable
condition this paper exposes is that any claim of necessity
must be tested against a fair substitute that consumes the same
observable quantity as the ground truth.
Measurement and evaluation
Our contribution is finally a measurement, and it connects to
work on the quality of the artifacts security studies are built
on—for instance the observation that vulnerability datasets
are frequently mislabeled, duplicated or otherwise unfit for
the claims drawn from them [32]. The artifact we report is
of the same family but inside a study rather than a dataset:
a specificity figure whosedenominatoris pooled over the
inputs a crawl could enumerate, so it cannot register a failure
confined to a class that crawl never reached, while the adjudi-
cation that consumes the same crawl can. We therefore read
a testing technique’s own reported metric as a measurement

X. CONCLUSION
For object-level authorization, MST-wi’s predicates do not
know whether the acting subject is entitled to the object; they
know what the interface, as crawled, permitted. Instantiating
them as defined produced three findings (Section VI).
Finding 1 (temporal)
As designed, the adjudication false-proofs on legitimately
granted access, deterministically (1.00 constructed; 0.40
Gitea), because an out-of-band grant fits only the wrong one
of the predicate’s two representations. On the real system the
mismatch appears only under the crawl-time reading (10/48,
vs. 0 live), with an endogenous control holding final visibility
fixed: within this controlled design the only manipulated
explanatory variable is when the model was read, not what
was visible.
Finding 2 (oracle)
The natural repair—a readable, sound authorization oracle
that fully resolves the proposition under test—works, and
in that regime the evaluated metamorphic step provides no
observed incremental adjudication benefit over a direct con-
sumer of the same oracle and outcome: an empirical premise,
formalised as a conditional (Proposition 2), whose empirical
face is that a single ordinary request yields the same false-
proof count (false proofs 0 vs. 0), and that of the 216 true-
violation records the paired comparison disagrees on only
3, each time in favor of the plain request; no margin was
pre-specified, so no inferential claim attaches. On Gitea the
comparison is limited to false-proof behavior, because no
vulnerability was present on the tested surface.
Finding 3 (measurement)
The reported specificity of 99.81% is diagnosed as a specific
published figure: it isTN/(TN + FP)over the follow-up
inputs the crawl could enumerate, with manually verified
labels, and that denominator cannot register a failure confined
to a class the run did not exercise.
Support evidence: reading the implementation confirmed
the semantics and added the mechanism—the crawl model
is frozen by a cache never invalidated, and a missing crawl
defaults to ‘‘unreachable’’—and the positive control is a san-
ity check that closes the loop: on a seeded vulnerable target,
for one fault pattern, the surface-gated adjudicator detects all
planted instances with zero false positives.
What the two added experiments carry
The temporal finding is not confined to one product. Under
the same pre-registered protocol, frozen before the second
run was observed, GitLab CE 17.11.7 commits 8 transitions
over 432 cells, with entitlement and effect agreeing on 432
of them: 16 stale false proofs, all 16 removed by a recrawl
with the same credentials, 0 live false proofs and 0 residual
misses. Pooled with Gitea this is 712 cells, 712 agreements,
29 stale false proofs and 29 fixes. The replication is of a
protocol, not of a topology: GitLab’s project-membership
surface answers 200 or 404, so it errs only by omission—in
53 cells it disagrees with the ledger, every one of them a false
deny, concentrated in the public (44) and site-administration
(9) channels—and both properties are read from the same
run rather than one being derived from the other. The enti-
tlement/effect gate is in turn checked against a real defect:
on a publicly fixed object-versus-path ownership flaw, Gitea
1.20.5 yields 1 true positive with 0 false proofs, and the fixed
1.20.6 yields 0 alarms over 10 control probes. That is a check
of the gate on one defect we located and supplied; it is not a
detection rate.
What we do not claim, and how far the claims reach
We do not claim that MST-wi is wrong or say anything about
its accuracy as a detector; the native run establishes a quali-
tative outcome, for one gate family, on one real system that
on the tested endpoints and transitions enforces the expected
decisions. Three ceilings stack and bound everything above.
First, our two relations are two of a catalogue of 76, and
both belong to one reachability-gated family. Second, even
the execution of the published relations on the native engine
reaches only that family: 2 of the 11 authorization relations
fire (10 executed; OTG_AUTHZ_002, OTG_AUTHZ_002c),
and the content predicateuserCanRetrieveContentis
never invoked (0 calls), so that evidence is bounded the same
way. Third, the real-system study exercises thefalse-proof
face on a single constructed fault pattern, not the detection
face against a population of real flaws, and the Gitea channel
proportions are topology design parameters, not field preva-
lence. Together these fix the ceiling on what a practitioner
may transfer.
What a practitioner should do differently
Three operational consequences follow, each bounded by
what was measured. Do not read a reachability-gated re-
lation’s alarm as proof: because the reading time is part
of the specification, an alarm raised against a crawl-time
snapshot—rather than against live state—cannot be promoted
toviolation-provenon the relation’s word alone. If a
readable, sound authorization oracle is in fact available to
the analyst, consult it directly and drop the relation: for the
relations studied it adds no adjudication information once
that antecedent holds (Proposition 2, Section IV -F). And treat
a reported input-level specificity as a corpus statistic rather
than a guarantee of scenario-level resolution: it can sit near
one while a whole class of legitimate access is misreported
(Finding 3). These are not a claim that metamorphic testing is
worthless; they locate its contribution where no oracle exists,
and bound it by what the reachability reading can see.
What a stronger claim would require
Moving from characterization to something stronger needs,
first, a population of real systems thatcontainthe flaw, so
detection can be measured—the single defect of Section V -D
checks the gate but cannot supply that population; and sec-
ond, relations whose gate is not the absence of evidence—
