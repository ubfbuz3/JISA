ABSTRACTBroken object-level authorization (BOLA) is the top-ranked API security risk, yet detecting it
is an oracle problem: vulnerable and correct systems answer identically. Metamorphic testing is a leading
technique for that problem in Web systems; for the relations we study, the predicates delegate the verdict to a
GUI-reachability model—a crawl-time snapshot of what the interface permits. We instantiate the published
relations as defined and drive constructed and real systems through crawl, out-of-band change, and execution.
Three findings follow. (1) Access granted out of band after the crawl is reported as aprovenviolation—afalse
proof—in 8 of 8 constructed alarms, because an out-of-band grant fits only the wrong one of the predicate’s
two representations. (2) Repairing this with a readable, sound oracle that fully resolves the proposition leaves
the metamorphic step adding no observed adjudication benefit over the direct consumer of the same oracle
and outcome; the paired methods disagree on only 3 of 216 true-violation records, all favouring the plain
request. (3) The reported specificity of 99.81% is not conditioned on a check that the reachability model
equals the authorization state, and its denominator cannot register a scenario-uniform failure.
The findings come from a 576-record scenario/configuration study on a constructed system, together with a
controlled cell study on Gitea 1.22.6, extended, with a methodological variation, to Gogs 0.14.3 and GitLab
17.11.7; an endogenous control holds final visibility fixed, and the published relations reproduce the false
proof within the gated subset that fires.

I. INTRODUCTION
B
ROKEN object-level authorization (BOLA), first in the
OW ASP API Security Top 10 [1], is the failure to en-
force, per object, that the authenticated subject is entitled to
the object touched. Detecting it is anoracleproblem: vulner-
able and correct systems answer with the same success status.
Metamorphic testing is an established approach to that oracle
problem, and MST-wi [2] is a substantial system-agnostic in-
stance for Web systems: 76 system-agnostic relations (MRs)
over 55 Web-specific predicates, reported to detect 85% of
vulnerabilities with specificity 99.81%.
This paper asks what happens to that claim when the
asserted property is object-level authorization. The predi-
cates do not express entitlement; they express what their
authors call reachability—what ‘‘the functionality exposed
by the user interface’’ permits—with the role-to-resource
mapping ‘‘not always available’’ [2]. We take that sen-
tence as premise, not discovery. Delegating adjudication to
a reachability model makes the moment the model was read
part of the specification, so we instantiate the predicatesas
defined—over a model recorded at crawl time—and drive the
system through three phases: crawl, change authorization out
of band, execute. The middle phase is the one the original
evaluation does not exercise.
We ran a 576-record scenario/configuration study on a
constructed system and a controlled study on Gitea 1.22.6,
extended, with a methodological variation, to Gogs 0.14.3 and
GitLab 17.11.7. We propose no mechanism. What survives is
three findings, one per research question (Section VI).

II. BACKGROUND: THE PREDICATES IN QUESTION
For authorization there is no intrinsic oracle: vulnerable
and correct executions both answer with success. Meta-
morphic testing replaces the absent oracle with a rela-
tion over multiple executions—and the relation is only as
sound as the assumptions it needs, which for authorization
are exactly the facts the oracle was supposed to supply.
MST-wi [2], building on Mai et al. [3], crawls a per-user
model of reachable URLs and contents, then checks MRs
against that recorded model. In the artifact we inspected,
the predicates guarding an authorization MR read either
the recorded model directly (cannotReachThroughGUI,
userCanRetrieveContent) or static subject properties;
we call a predicateGUI-derivedwhen its value is a function
of the crawl.
Why the false proof is a design consequence, not a bug
Suppose a subject can reach an object at crawl time, so the
record stores it as reachable. Afterwards an administrator
grants the same subject access to afurtherobject through
a channel—an administrative endpoint or a share link—that
a crawl of the subject’s own interface never traverses. At
execution the guard still reads the frozen record, finds the
new object absent, and concludes the subject cannot reach
it; the relation then treats the successful response as a vi-
olation. Every step is exactly as specified, so no guard is
miswritten: the predicate’s two representations—‘‘reachable
as recorded’’ and ‘‘not reachable as recorded’’—cannot ex-
press ‘‘reachable, but only after the record was taken’’. More
crawling does not remove the false proof, because the grant
need not be visible through the subject’s interface at all, and
the reported specificity never registers it, because the error is
a property of the adjudication’sreading momentrather than
of any single generated input. This is the mechanism the rest
of the paper measures, and the reason we speak of a boundary
of the specification rather than a defect of an implementation.
Two census figures frame what follows. Over the 76 cat-
alog MRs, 38 (50.0%) gate on a GUI-derived or identity
predicate, and only 14 (18.42%) are authorization-relevant by
name or action—an upper bound, reported as a caveat; a full-
text search for predicates reading thestate of an authorization
artifact(ownership, permission record, share) finds 0 occur-
rences. Within the examined vocabulary, then, the authoriza-
tion MRs have no authorization-artifact state observable to
decide on, and the reading moment enters the specification:
if an entitlement changes after the crawl, the MR evaluates
reachability against a world that no longer exists. This is a
statement about what the relationsmean; our experiments are
built so that the crawl-time and live readings can be told apart.
III. PROTOCOL: A FAITHFUL THREE-PHASE
INSTANTIATION
Two instruments carry distinct claims. The three-phase in-
stantiation below carries the quantitative results of Sec-
tions IV and V; a direct execution of the library’s own
relations—source unmodified, with the build configuration

and a replay harness added—carries one qualitative claim,
that the published code produces the false proof we pre-
dict (Section VII-B). Neither instrument is a benchmark: the
standing scope and non-claims are collected in Section VIII,
and neither yields a claim of detector accuracy.
Three phases
(A)Crawl: record, per subject and object, whether the subject
can reach the object through the interface, and freeze that
record—the model the predicates will consult. (B)Change
authorization out of band: alter a (subject, object) entitlement
through a channel outside the crawled interface; the frozen
model is not updated, because the architecture has no mecha-
nism to update it. (C)Execute the MRagainst the current state
while the predicates consult the frozen model. Phase B is what
the original evaluation does not exercise, and it is where the
reachability stand-in can be wrong (Fig. 1).
Adjudicator variants and internal controls
A raw alarm becomes a verdict through an exception policy,
and different policies are different methods:v1(raw alarms
proven);v2(legitimate-sharing exceptions filtered);v3(state-
calibrated—consults a readable authorization introspection
surface, abstains when none is readable);degrade_all
(the floor: abstain on every alarm);direct_only(autho-
rization surface only, access outcome ignored—a deliberately
unfair control); anddirect_observe(a succeeding plain
request checked against the same readable surface). The last
consumes the same observable quantity as the MR family and
decides the negative result.
Ground truth
Every quantitative result names its ground-truth source, and
it is never the detector under test: construction state for
the constructed system and the scenario/configuration study;
system-specific live re-probes of effective authorization (the
per-system validation procedure is described in Section V) for
Gitea, Gogs and GitLab; the pinned route tables for the grant
census; and the seed list for the seeded target.
Scenarios and design cells
Seven scenarios separate the reasons an MR can misfire:
no shared object (S1, S2); an in-band legitimate share vis-
ible in the framework log (S3); an unshared plain viola-
tion (S4); a read-only share plus a write violation (S5); an
out-of-band share invisible to the framework log (S6); an
out-of-band share plus a genuine violation (S7). They are
crossed with 8 configurations defined by instantiation mode,
enforcement correctness, channel visibility and introspection
availability. The nominal cross is7×8combinations, but
four are inadmissible: the two no-exception configurations
(V-noE3-vuln0,V-noE3-vuln1) are undefined under
the two out-of-band scenarios (S6, S7), where the legitimate-
sharing exception does not apply. This leaves 52 admissible
design cells.
Statistical treatment
The 576 records are not independent observations. Each ad-
missible cell is expanded deterministically by the factors that
are actually varied: 2 metamorphic relations (MR-002, MR-
004), 3 exception-policy variants (raw, one level, two levels—
the first deliberately thrown switch), and 2 reachability read-
ings (the frozen crawl-time snapshot and the live execution-
time state—the second deliberately thrown switch). The two
relations do not both apply to every cell: only MR-002 applies
under scenario S5, so 8 S5 cells carry one relation while the
remaining 44 carry two. The record count is therefore
576 = 44·2·3·2 + 8·3·2,
a deliberately unbalanced design—the per-cell multiplier dif-
fers by scenario. The records are therefore a fixed enumer-
ation, not a sample: we report counts, rates and design-cell
denominators, and we attach no inferential interval to any
of them. Where a headline count has a small denominator
we say so explicitly (the anchored rate, 2/2, is unstable by
construction) and lead with the larger-denominator count of
the same phenomenon. No conclusion rests on statistical
significance. Where a comparison is paired—the ablation of
Section IV -C—we report discordant-pair counts rather than
an interval over a difference. The replication ceiling is ex-
plicit: one constructed system, 52 cells, 2 MRs, three real
systems.
Tooling and code provenance
Development of the analysis scripts was assisted by an AI
coding assistant (declared in the Declarations). Every script
that assistant touched was reviewed, executed, and its output
checked by the authors against the raw records; the released
pipeline recomputes every reported scalar from those records,
every table is generated by the pipeline, and the macro files
and the two auto-generated tables share a single source of
truth. No AI tool participated in the detection loop or in any
adjudication.

C. FAIR ABLATION: DOES THE METAMORPHIC STEP ADD
ADJUDICATION INFORMATION?
Our earlier iteration proposed v3 as a mechanism, resting on
the claim that the metamorphic step was necessary. We tested
that claim by removing the step and substituting the most
naive consumer of the same observable. The distinction mat-
ters:direct_only—oracle yes, access outcome ignored—
produces 108 false proofs against v3’s 0 (precision 0.50), and
rejecting it proves nothing because it discards half the evi-
dence. The fair control isdirect_observe: a succeeding
ordinary request plus the same readable surface, consuming
exactly what the MR-based adjudicator consumes.
Table 1 concludes the ablation.direct_observehas
0 false proofs against v3’s 0—a tie at zero, not a win. The
comparison is paired, so we report the discordance rather than
an inferential interval: over the 216 true-violation records
the two methods disagree on 3, and on every one of them
direct_observeis right—it detects a violation that v3
misses or abstains on, its reachability precondition blocking
a detection the plain request makes. The aggregate recalls
0.50 and 0.49 restate that asymmetry rather than measure a
difference, and no margin was pre-specified, so no formal
hypothesis-test claim attaches. We found no incremental ad-
judication advantage of the metamorphic step over direct ob-
servation in these oracle-available scenarios, and that absence
provides no support for an incremental adjudication benefit in
these scenarios: the step would have to be better at something
the plain request cannot do. Selective presentation would have
hidden the cost: v3’s precision of 1.00 was bought with 111
false negatives (F1 0.65), an exchange of 60 false alarms for
missed violations dressed as an improvement.

E. WHAT POOLING HID: THE ABLATION SPLIT BY ORACLE
READABILITY
Table 1 pools two populations in which the oracle-consuming
methods behave in opposite ways, and the pool is misleading
in both directions. Section IV reports the comparison on a
representative grid; this subsection, summarised in Table 2,
repeats it over all 576 records. The records differ in one
respect that decides whether those methods can act at all:
whether the introspection surface returns a reading for the
record. We therefore repeat the same comparison inside each
stratum, with the same methods and the same decision rule,
and we add two references that need no oracle against which
the metamorphic family can be scored where no oracle exists.
Stratification variable
We stratify on theeffectivereadability of the surface—the
value the probe returns for the record—not on the configu-
ration switch. The distinction is not cosmetic: 108 records
sit in configurations flaggedintrospection=onwhose
surface nevertheless returns nothing (the two no-exception
configurations), so the configuration flag is not a faithful
proxy for whether an oracle was available to the adjudicator,
and using it would misclassify those records. The two strata
partition all 576 records: 312 readable, 264 unreadable. Both
are fixed enumerations, so as elsewhere we report counts and
rates and attach no inferential interval.
The readable stratum strengthens Finding 2
Splitting does not rescue the metamorphic step; it removes
the comparison in which it looked best. On the 312 readable
recordsdirect_observereaches recall 1.00 with 0 false
proofs, against v2’s recall 0.97 with 36 false proofs, and
against v3’s recall 0.97 with 0. The ordering is therefore
not an artifact of the metric—the plain request is ahead
on false proofs and not behind on detections—whereas the
pooled table had let the family appear competitive on re-
call. We do not claim domination: the comparison rests on
the constructed-observable argument of Proposition 2, and
direct_observeis not an independent method here,
since it is built from the same two quantities that define the
reference (its second input, the access outcome, is part of
how the reference labels a violation). What the split shows
is the scope of the necessity claim: where an oracle resolves
the proposition, there is nothing left for the relation to add,
and the pooled numbers were flattering to the relation only
because they mixed in records where the oracle-bearing meth-
ods could not run.
The unreadable stratum is the relation’s own regime, and it is
not empty
The 264 unreadable records contain 108 true violations
and 156 correct cases, a violation base rate of 40.91%.
Every oracle-bearing method—v3,direct_observe,
direct_only—detects 0 of them, not because the relations
are weak but because those methods have nothing to con-
sult and, by their own definition, abstain. The metamorphic
family does not abstain: v2 reaches all 108 true violations
(recall 1.00) with 24 false proofs, precision 0.82, a lift of
2.000×over the base rate. Against the two no-oracle refer-
ences:always_alarmscores precision 0.41, which is the
base rate itself—no information at all—andobs_success,
which alarms on the observed success of a plain request,
reaches thesamerecall at precision 0.69, lift 1.692×. The
net difference between the metamorphic family and that ref-
erence is therefore not in detections (0 additional true vio-
lations) but in false alarms: 24 fewer for the metamorphic
family.
What the saving consists of
The attribution is one-sided and mechanical. Over the seven
scenarios, every record of the 24-alarm saving falls on S3,
the legitimate share createdthrough the framework’s own
facilityand hence recorded in its log: 24 false alarms for
obs_successagainst 0 for v2 on the 48 records of that
scenario. On S6, the same share created out of band, the two
agree exactly (24 vs. 24 false alarms on 24 records), and
both for the reason developed in Section VI-A—the predicate
has no representation for a grant the crawl did not see. So
the incremental detection information the metamorphic step
carries where no oracle exists is exactly the information the
crawl-derived reachability model can see: it suppresses what
was logged, and it reproduces Finding 1 on what was not.
This bounds Finding 2 rather than overturning it. Finding 2’s
antecedent—a readable, sound oracle that fully resolves the
proposition—fails on this stratum by construction, and where
it fails the relation is not worthless but worth precisely the
reachability model it reads: above a trivial floor, above a
single uninterpreted request, and below any method that can
consult the entitlement.
Limits of the comparison
Two references are deliberately weak, and we do not present
them as the frontier of oracle-free detection:obs_success
uses one request and no model, and a differential prober
that compares several accounts against one another—the
shape taken by the black-box line reviewed in Section IX—
consumes more thanobs_successdoes and was not in-
stantiated here. The lift we measure is therefore an estimate
against weak references, not a claim about metamorphic
testing versus the best oracle-free detector, and we make

B. A SEEDED POSITIVE CONTROL
On the tested endpoints and transitions the three real systems
enforce the expected decisions, so the question remains:when
a real object-level flaw is present, does the same detector
find it?We answer with a positive control. Acontrolled au-
thorization targetfaithfully emulates the Gitea API contract
the engine consumes, with a correct internal authorization
model and 3 seeded vulnerable instances—all instantiating
one fault pattern (a none-entitlement subject served the ob-
ject atGET /api/v1/repos/{owner}/{repo}), dif-
fering in subject and object, not in fault location; Table 8
is therefore evidence about this fault pattern, not three in-
dependent defect discoveries. Two design points. The target
deliberately exposes its authorization state to the subject-
side harness (mirroring the admissibility condition), which
production Gitea does not—there the oracle is readable from
the owner/admin vantage (56/56) but not from an arbitrary
subject’s (0/40)—and its introspection surface reports the
correctnonepermission, so the oracle stays sound and the
defect is visible only as a discrepancy between oracle and
request. We drove it with the same topology, mutation, crawl,
introspection and adjudication code as above, differing only
in the system under test; ground truth comes from the seed
list, not from the detector.
Table 8 is consistent with the negative result of the ablation
in this controlled fault pattern:direct_observeandv3

direct_only, which ignores the request outcome, raises

succeeds—alarms our strict definition does not promote. The
control thus narrows the claim to one fault pattern: when a
surface that fully resolves the authorization question is avail-

It does not generalise beyond that pattern, and it also delimits

sanity check on the detector’s logic, not a validation of detec-
tion effectiveness on a population, and not a statement about
Gitea’s CVE posture. Its source, seed list and run script are

B. FINDING 2 (RQ2): THE METAMORPHIC STEP ADDS NO
OBSERVED ADJUDICATION BENEFIT
If the false proofs of Finding 1 are removed by consulting a
readable introspection surface, it is the oracle that carries
the work: for the authorization relations and scenarios eval-
uated here, once the oracle fully resolves the authorization
proposition under test, replacing the entire metamorphic step
with a single ordinary request—one that consumes the same
readable oracle and access outcome—provides no observed
incremental adjudication benefit.We state the reason rather
than present it as a discovered effect: its burden is an empir-
ical premise about the evaluated relations, not an algebraic
identity.
Proposition 2(adjudication of the evaluated relations is or-
acle-determined).LetObe an authorization oracle that is
readable(its answer can be obtained from the vantage in
question) andsound(its answer equals the true permission),
and letRbe the observed outcome of an ordinary request.
For the authorization relations and scenarios evaluated here—
whose predicates read the crawl-time reachability modelM
and the response, and nothing else—onceOis readable,
sound and complete for the proposition under test,Msupplies
no decision-relevant authorization information that(O,R)
does not already determine: agreement with the true state
is redundant, and disagreement makes the verdict unsound
(Finding 1). The verdict of the metamorphic family is there-
fore a functionf(O,R)ofOandRalone, and any adjudicator
of that form is extensionally equal to the direct consumer
D:=f(O,R)—the two are the same function applied to the
same arguments. A metamorphic step can beshownto be
necessary only by exhibiting an observable it consumes that
the direct consumer does not.
Proof.The first clause is the empirical premise, established
for these relations by source inspection (Section VII-A) and
by the ablation (Section IV -C); the middle clause then follows
becauseMenters the verdict only through(O,R), and the
remainder is the observation that a function agrees pointwise
with itself, the last sentence being its contrapositive.
The proposition concernsadjudicationinformation only. It
does not imply that a metamorphic relation cannot improve
input generation, search-space reduction, or execution effi-
ciency: a relation may guide which requests are issued, or
which gated subset is exercised, while carrying no verdict
information beyond(O,R). It is a requirement onclaims, not
a verdict on metamorphic testing: it says only that a verdict
computable from(O,R)cannot be shown to beat the direct
consumer of(O,R).
Scope, quantified
The comparison above is pooled. Section IV -E re-runs it

over a trivial floor and over a single uninterpreted request

is exactly coextensive with what the crawl-derived model can
see. Finding 2 is a claim about the regime its antecedent
describes; it does not say the relations are worthless outside

C. FINDING 3 (RQ3): THE REPORTED SPECIFICITY IS NOT
CONDITIONED ON THE CORRESPONDENCE IT ASSUMES
The reported 99.81% is a statement about a corpus of gen-

the reachability model coincides with the true authorization
state, and its input-level denominator cannot provide sta-

The omission: adjudication is a function of the reachability
model, so where model and true state diverge—the out-of-
band case of Finding 1—the figure measures agreement with
the tool’s own crawler; the authors concede the mapping
is ‘‘not always available’’ [2], but the number is reported

D. ADMISSIBILITY, AND FALSIFICATION
Three properties must be kept apart. A surface isreadable
when its answer can be obtained from the vantage in question;
soundwhen its answer equals the true authorization state; and
admissible, for promotion toviolation-proven, when
(1) it is readableand(2) one can give a reason that it is not
of the same kind as the defect under test—not itself a reach-
ability artifact whose blind spot correlates with the blindness
tested. Readability is what makes soundness usable, but it nei-
ther implies soundness nor confers admissibility: the owner-
vantage authorization-introspection surface is readable on
every cell in each system (48 Gitea, 48 Gogs, 48 GitLab),
yet sound on Gitea alone—it agrees with effective access on
48, 31 and 30 of those cells respectively. The cross-vendor
systems are therefore not cases of ‘‘no oracle’’: a readable
membership surface exists on both, but it is unsound for the
authorization channels exercised here, so it fails clause (2).
Clause (2) is a per-system judgement and not automatable
from readability alone; the cross-vendor results make it con-
crete (Gogs and GitLab fail it; their adjudicators convert the
blind spots into 8 and 5 false proofs). Where the condition
holds, the honest method consumes the oracle directly and
drops the relation; where it does not, the honest method
abstains—as v3 does on the unreadable stratum, where the
aggregate table would have ranked it best, a ranking that
reflects abstention rather than improved detection.
Read together, the two findings bound what the technique’s
adjudication can yield for the authorization proposition stud-
ied. If the reachability stand-in is the only warrant and it is
wrong, a legitimate access becomes a false proof (Finding 1).
If a readable, sound oracle is instead available, the relation
adds no adjudication over the direct consumer of that oracle
and the outcome (Finding 2). And where no readable, admis-
sible oracle exists—the black-box case—there is nothing for
a sound method to consume, so it abstains rather than proves.
Across the statuses the oracle can take, the outcomes the
evaluated relations can yield are thus a false proof, a result no
better than the plain request (and, where the oracle is readable,
worse), or an abstention; adjudication of a genuinely illegal
access is not among them for these relations.
Each finding would fall as follows: Finding 1, if the predi-
cates were defined over live rather than recorded reachability;
Finding 2, by an authorization MR needing an observable that
a succeeding request plus a readable oracle cannot produce;
Finding 3, by a specificity in scenario units, or with the
condition explicit, that still fails to separate the configurations
studied here.
VII. SUPPORT EVIDENCE
Nothing here is a further finding. This section holds what the
three findings rest on: the semantics of the two governing
predicates read at source (Section VII-A), an execution of
the published relations on a real system (Section VII-B),
and the robustness of our numbers to a second, independent
implementation of the same predicates (Section VII-C); the
seeded positive control (Section V -B) and the cross-vendor

A. CONFIRMATION AT SOURCE
Definitions can be read generously, so we checked the predi-
cates at source in the public library [2]. It ships 23 relations,
11 authorization relations, 8 gating on the two predicates
analyzed. Four implementation-level properties not made ex-

scenarios on which a second reading is defined, holding
the exception-policy variant at the representative level and
the reachability reading at the one each relation uses (MR-
002 blind, MR-004 aware). This yields 30 (configuration,
scenario) points; MR-002 is defined on 30 of the resulting
relation comparisons and MR-004 on the remaining 24, giv-

set is a fixed enumeration, so no interval is attached. Both
were written from the same reading of the semantics, so this
bounds implementation accident only; it does not bound a
shared blind spot, and we do not present it as corroboration.
VIII. THREATS TO VALIDITY
Source versus execution
We read the predicates at source (built under JDK 8 and
Maven; Section VII-A) and then executed the relations, un-
modified apart from the build configuration and our harness
(Section VII-B). The harness substitutes an HTTP observa-
tion channel—exact for the reachability predicate, not exer-
cised for the content predicate, whose invocation count we
report rather than claim cleanliness we did not demonstrate.
The run converts one class of claim (the false proof is pro-
duced by thepublishedrelations on a real system); it does not
convert the rest: the scenario/configuration and cell studies
remain statements about our instantiation, our exception vo-
cabulary belongs to the same predicate family, and we report
no sensitivity or specificity attributable to MST-wi.
Metric and statistics
The specificity approximation uses two follow-up inputs per
case—not the engine’s count; the conclusion about the de-
nominator’s resolution does not depend on the exact value.
Records within a design cell are not independent (Section III,
statistical treatment) and the records are a fixed enumeration,
so we report counts and rates rather than inferential intervals,
and no result rests on statistical significance. The RQ2 abla-
tion is paired and is reported as a discordance count.
Two MRs, one family
We instantiated 2 MRs of one family—relations gated on the
absence of evidence. The native run lifts the count to 10 of
11 catalog authorization relations but not the family: only the
reachability-gated ones alarm in our run. This is the paper’s
single most important limitation; raising it requires relations
gated otherwise, a different experiment.
Real systems
Three systems (one lineage pair, one independent), chosen for
accessibility, none vulnerable on the tested surface—so only
the false-proof side is measured, within one domain, the git
forge. Channel proportions are topology design parameters,
not field prevalence. Constructed scenarios with construction-
defined ground truth are why the real-system section exists;
three systems tested once each are still not a population.
Stratification and the references used in it
The split of Section IV -E is on whether the introspection
surface returned a reading for the record, not on the config-
uration switch: 108 records sit in configurations that enable
introspection but whose probe returns nothing, so the switch
would misclassify them. The two oracle-free references there
are deliberately weak—one unconditional, one guessing from
a single request—because the point is to size the metamorphic
step against methods that need nothing, not to identify the best
oracle-free detector; a differential prober across accounts con-
sumes more than those references do and was not instantiated.
Both references share an observable with the labelling rule,
asdirect_observedoes, so only the differences against
them—not their absolute rates—are meant to be read.

An earlier iteration proposed v3 as a mechanism on the
strength of its precision (1.00), which was bought with 111
false negatives, and a control (direct_only, precision
0.50) that discarded the access result. Replacing it with a fair

IX. RELATED WORK
Metamorphic security testing
Metamorphic testing was brought to web security by Mai et
al. [3], who cast security test patterns—authentication, au-
thorization, session—as metamorphic relations so that faults
can be exposed without an oracle. The line matured into
MST-wi [2], whose 76 system-agnostic relations over a Web-
specific domain language are the object of our study. We
take the technique’s own admission that the role-to-resource
mapping is ‘‘not always available’’ at face value and follow
it to its consequence for what the adjudication can decide.
A relation whose guard reads a crawl-time model is sound
only while the model equals the authorization state, and that
equality is exactly what the technique cannot check for itself.
Detecting broken access control
A large and active literature detects access-control flaws
directly, and it is worth separating the oracles it relies on.
Black-box probing drives requests with and without the
rights of another subject and looks for a missing refusal [4],
[5], a line that runs back to AUTHSCOPE [6] and that
has been extended to deployed systems under ethical con-
straints [7]; where an API description is available, the oracle
itself can be synthesised, for instance by transforming an Ope-
nAPI specification into a colored Petri net that predicts per-
object access [8]. A complementary, white-box line recovers
theenforcedpolicy from the implementation—by reverse-
engineering RBAC policies [9], by statically extracting au-
thorization policies [10] or database access-control rules [11],
or by locating the permission checks themselves [12]—and
then flags violations against that recovered model: BOLA
in database-backed applications [13], missing-owner-check
and function-level flaws in Java web applications [14], [15],
function- and object-level access-control flaws across Web
applications [16], and privilege escalation in polyglot mi-
croservices [17]. Specification-driven API fuzzers generate
the requests well but inherit the same oracle difficulty [18]–
[20]; adding explicit access-policy violation checks [21]
moves them toward the detection problem but does not re-
move the need for a ground truth, and a recent turn dele-
gates generation or probing to language models [22]–[24]. A
concurrent line reframes BOLA under a zero-trust model, in
which each request must carry an explicit authorization deci-
sion rather than one inferred from endpoint reachability [25];
our question sits at that decision level, asking what an oracle-
substituting technique can establish about it. Our question is
orthogonal to all of these. Each reports detections against an
oracle it holds privately—a policy, a seeded defect, a differ-
ential response; we ask what an existing oracle-substituting
techniquedecideswhen the entitlement information it needs
is not among its observables, and we make that a measurable
property of the technique rather than of a system.
Direct authorization oracles, and what is at stake here
Comparing responses across principals or privilege states is
an established way to obtain an authorization oracle when
the ground-truth relation is available, and the black-box and
white-box lines above are instances of it. We propose no new
direct oracle, and we do not claim that direct authorization
comparison is novel. Our subject is a different question: what
an existing oracle-substituting techniquedecideswhen it sub-
stitutes reachability for entitlement. The direct oracle enters
our design in exactly one role—as the fair, same-observable
control that a necessity claim about the metamorphic step
must face. Without such a control, a substitution can appear
necessary only because the alternative it is compared against
was not allowed to consume the same decision-relevant ob-
servables.
LLMs and the oracle problem
LLMs are used for vulnerability detection and specifica-
tion inference [26]–[29], including lightweight models over
hybrid code features [30] and federated, explainable detec-
tors [31]; we use none in the detection loop. The transferable
condition this paper exposes is that any claim of necessity
must be tested against a fair substitute that consumes the same
observable quantity as the ground truth.

Measurement and evaluation
Our contribution is finally a measurement, and it connects to
work on the quality of the artifacts security studies are built
on—for instance the observation that vulnerability datasets
are frequently mislabeled, duplicated or otherwise unfit for
the claims drawn from them [32]. The artifact we report is
of the same family but inside a study rather than a dataset:
a specificity figure whosedenominatoris structurally unable
to register a whole class of failure, and whose operating point
silently assumes a reachability model equals the authorization
state. We therefore read a testing technique’s own reported
metric as a measurement instrument, stating explicitly what
its denominator can and cannot resolve.
X. CONCLUSION
For object-level authorization, MST-wi’s predicates do not
know whether the acting subject is entitled to the object; they
know what the interface, as crawled, permitted. Instantiating
them as defined produced three findings (Section VI).
Finding 1 (temporal)
As designed, the adjudication false-proofs on legitimately
granted access, deterministically (1.00 constructed; 0.40
Gitea), because an out-of-band grant fits only the wrong one
of the predicate’s two representations. On the real system the
mismatch appears only under the crawl-time reading (10/48,
vs. 0 live), with an endogenous control holding final visibility
fixed: within this controlled design the only manipulated
explanatory variable is when the model was read, not what
was visible.
Finding 2 (oracle)
The natural repair—a readable, sound authorization oracle
that fully resolves the proposition under test—works, and
in that regime the evaluated metamorphic step provides no
observed incremental adjudication benefit over a direct con-
sumer of the same oracle and outcome: an empirical premise,
formalised as a conditional (Proposition 2), whose empirical
face is that a single ordinary request yields the same false-
proof count (false proofs 0 vs. 0), and that of the 216 true-
violation records the paired comparison disagrees on only
3, each time in favor of the plain request; no margin was
pre-specified, so no inferential claim attaches. On Gitea the
comparison is limited to false-proof behavior, because no
vulnerability was present on the tested surface.
Finding 3 (measurement)
The reported specificity of 99.81% is diagnosed as a specific
published figure: it is not conditioned on an explicit check
that the reachability model equals the authorization state, and
its input-level denominator cannot register a class-uniform
failure.
Support evidence: reading the implementation confirmed
the semantics and added the mechanism—the crawl model
is frozen by a cache never invalidated, and a missing crawl
defaults to ‘‘unreachable’’—and the positive control is a san-
ity check that closes the loop: on a seeded vulnerable target,
for one fault pattern, the surface-gated adjudicator detects all
planted instances with zero false positives.
What we do not claim, and how far the claims reach
We do not claim that MST-wi is wrong or say anything about
its accuracy as a detector; the native run establishes a quali-
tative outcome, for one gate family, on one real system that
on the tested endpoints and transitions enforces the expected
decisions. Three ceilings stack and bound everything above.
First, our two relations are two of a catalogue of 76, and
both belong to one reachability-gated family. Second, even
the execution of the published relations on the native engine
reaches only that family: 2 of the 11 authorization relations
fire (10 executed; OTG_AUTHZ_002, OTG_AUTHZ_002c),
and the content predicateuserCanRetrieveContentis
never invoked (0 calls), so that evidence is bounded the same
way. Third, the real-system study exercises thefalse-proof
face on a single constructed fault pattern, not the detection
face against a population of real flaws, and the Gitea channel
proportions are topology design parameters, not field preva-
lence. Together these fix the ceiling on what a practitioner
may transfer.
What a practitioner should do differently
Three operational consequences follow, each bounded by
what was measured. Do not read a reachability-gated re-
lation’s alarm as proof: because the reading time is part
of the specification, an alarm raised against a crawl-time
snapshot—rather than against live state—cannot be promoted
toviolation-provenon the relation’s word alone. If a
readable, sound authorization oracle is in fact available to
the analyst, consult it directly and drop the relation: for the
relations studied it adds no adjudication information once
that antecedent holds (Proposition 2, Section IV -E). And treat
a reported input-level specificity as a corpus statistic rather
than a guarantee of scenario-level resolution: it can sit near
one while a whole class of legitimate access is misreported
(Finding 3). These are not a claim that metamorphic testing is
worthless; they locate its contribution where no oracle exists,
and bound it by what the reachability reading can see.
What a stronger claim would require
Moving from characterization to something stronger needs,
first, a real system thatcontainsthe flaw, so detection can
be measured; and second, relations whose gate is not the ab-
sence of evidence—checkable against a subject’s entitlements
without pretending that entitlements are reachable facts. The
transferable condition—we call it thesame-observable con-
dition—is the one that killed our own mechanism: a com-
ponent’s necessity must be tested against a substitute that
consumes thesameobservable quantity as the ground truth;
a substitute that consumes less does not establish incremental
value. The MST-wi case study is an instance of that condition,
not its scope: a component’s necessity is not established un-
less a fair control that lacks the component but consumes the
same decision-relevant observables is empirically or formally
shown to be weaker.
