## 1. Question the rule answers

For a target system $S$, a defect class $c$, and a candidate oracle surface $O$, the
rule decides whether an answer from $O$ may promote a metamorphic-relation alarm to
`violation-proven` on the proposition "subject $u$ is not authorized to reach object
$o$ through channel $ch$".

It is the operational form of clause (2) of the admissibility condition: the oracle
must not be "of the same kind as the defect under test". Clause (1), readability, is
decided separately and is not this rule's business.

## 2. Defect classes in scope (fixed)

The classes are fixed here so that the study cannot be re-scoped after seeing which
classes are easy to detect.

| id | class | what the defect breaks | channel exercised |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | read enforcement (BOLA-read) | object-level read authorization | `GET` on the object and its content |
| C2 | modify enforcement (BOLA-write) | object-level write authorization | `PATCH`/`PUT`/`DELETE` on the object |
| C3 | inheritance | authorization inherited through org/team/fork edges | `GET`/write after a grant that should not propagate |
| C4 | privilege escalation | grant path itself (collaborator / team / membership) | `PUT`/`POST` that widens another subject's access |

C1 and C2 are the classes the original submission studied. C3 and C4 are added by this
protocol; they are *not* claimed to have been studied before 2026-10-19.

## 3. Evidence levels

Every decision must name the level at which it was taken.

- **L0 — documentation.** Product documentation states which component answers.
  Insufficient for any decision other than `unknown`.
- **L1 — observable API surface.** The endpoints that answer $y$ and $o$ are
  identified by probing a running instance, and the processes and permission models
  behind them are inferred from version banners, error surfaces and route structure.
  No source is read.
- **L2 — source inspection at a pinned version.** The function or guard that produces
  each answer is located in the pinned source of $S$; the call graph between the two
  paths is read.
- **L3 — differential intervention.** A defect is injected or selected such that it
  changes the answer on one path and, if the paths were shared, must change the other.
  Observed non-propagation is positive evidence of independence; propagation is
  positive evidence of sharing.

## 4. Decision procedure (fixed)

Apply in order; the first rule that fires decides. The procedure is deliberately
one-sided: it prefers `reject` and `unknown` over `admit`.

1. **Unusable oracle.** If the oracle route cannot be completed at the level available
   (endpoint absent, required parameter unsupported, answer not expressible for the
   channel), record `reject` with reason `oracle-unusable`. Rationale: clause (1)
   already fails, so independence is moot.
2. **Shared decision point.** If $P_y$ and $P_o$ contain a common decision point, or a
   common component whose failure could co-occur with the tested defect, record
   `reject` with reason `shared`.
3. **Same process and same permission model at L1 only.** If both paths are answered by
   one process applying one permission model on the tested channel, and no source-level
   evidence to the contrary exists, record `reject` with reason `conservative-L1`.
   This is the reading used for the archived systems; it is a refusal to promote, not a
   claim that the paths are provably shared.
4. **Independence at L2.** If source inspection shows the two paths neither call each
   other nor consult a common authorization decision on the tested channel, record
   `admit` at L2.
5. **Independence at L3.** If a differential intervention changes $P_y$ and not $P_o$,
   record `admit` at L3 regardless of whether L2 was reached; if it changes both,
   record `reject` with reason `shared`.
6. **Otherwise** record `unknown` with the reason the enumeration stopped.

Ties and conflicts: a lower-numbered rule wins over a higher-numbered one. `admit` is
never inferred from behaviour alone at L1.

## 5. What each status licenses

| status | promotion to `violation-proven` | what the paper may say |
|---|---|---|
| `admit` (L2/L3) | permitted | the oracle is treated as an independent entitlement source for this class |
| `reject` | refused | the oracle reading is diagnostic only; alarms are reported as gate outcomes, not as confirmed violations |
| `unknown` | refused | independence is not established; the same refusal as `reject`, with the reason stated |

Every promoted verdict in the paper must carry its status and level. A number computed
under `reject` or `unknown` may still be reported; it may not be described as a
confirmed violation.

Filled under this rule. Rows marked `pending` are the new results this protocol exists
to govern; they were empty when the protocol was frozen.

| system | class | oracle surface | level | status | reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gitea 1.20.5 | C1/C2 | `/repos/{o}/{n}/collaborators/{u}/permission` | L1 | `reject` | `conservative-L1` |

| Gogs 0.13 | C1/C2 | per-object collaborator route | L1 | `reject` | `oracle-unusable` |
| seeded target | C1 | emulator permission route | L2 | `pending` | — |
| seeded target | C2 | emulator permission route | L2 | `pending` | — |
| seeded target | C3 | emulator permission route | L2 | `pending` | — |
| seeded target | C4 | emulator membership route | L2 | `pending` | — |

The seeded target is the only system in which L2 is available at all, because its
source is ours and the two paths can be read. If C3 or C4 turns out to share the
decision point with the content path in that emulator, the correct outcome under this
rule is `reject`, and the study must report that rather than a detection number.

## 7. Falsifiers

- A `reject` at L1 is falsified by L2 evidence that the two paths consult different
  authorization decisions; the register row is then re-decided at L2 under rule 4.
- An `admit` at L2 is falsified by an L3 intervention that propagates; the row moves to
  `reject` with reason `shared`.
- A `pending` row may not be resolved by anything other than an L2 or L3 observation.

## 8. Change control

- Amendments are appended as `v2`, `v3`, … with date, diff and reason.
- No amendment may be made after the outcome of the affected run is known, except to
  record a falsification under §7; such an amendment must quote the observation.
- The digest of this file is recorded in `SHA256SUMS` next to it and in the commit that
  introduces it.

## 9. What this protocol does not do

- It does not establish independence for any product whose source we did not read.
- It does not convert the archived `reject` rows into `admit`; those rows stay `reject`.
- It does not decide whether the emulator's defects are *realistic*; that is a separate
  threat-to-validity question answered in the paper's limitations.
- It does not license any claim that a positive detection in the new study is a
  previously unknown vulnerability of a production system.
