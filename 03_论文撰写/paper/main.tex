\begin{abstract}
Metamorphic testing substitutes for the missing Web-security oracle by relating a
system's own responses to one another instead of to a ground truth. For the
object-level-authorization relations we study, the predicates delegate the
verdict to a \emph{GUI-reachability model}---what the interface, as crawled,
permits---a \emph{crawl-time snapshot}. We instantiate the published relations as
defined and drive constructed and real systems through crawl, out-of-band change,
and execution. Three findings follow. (1)~Access granted out of band after the
crawl is reported as a \emph{proven} violation---a \emph{false proof}---in
\ResObsOobVtwoFalseProof{} of \ResObsOobFalseAlarm{} constructed alarms, because
an out-of-band grant fits only the wrong one of the predicate's two
representations. (2)~Repairing this with a readable, sound oracle that fully
resolves the proposition leaves the metamorphic step adding no observed
adjudication benefit over the direct consumer of the same oracle and outcome; the
paired methods disagree on only \ResAbDisagreementN{} of \ResAbNBola{}
true-violation records, all favouring the plain request. (3)~The reported
specificity of \ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\% is not conditioned on a check that the
reachability model equals the authorization state, and its denominator cannot
register a scenario-uniform failure.

The findings come from a \ResSynRecords{}-record scenario/configuration study and
a Gitea \ResRsVersion{} study extended to Gogs \GgVersion{} and GitLab
\GlVersion{}. On Gitea the mismatch tracks the reading time, not visibility,
appearing under the crawl-time reading and not live, with an endogenous control
holding final visibility fixed; executing the published relations reproduces the
false proof within the gated subset that fires.
\end{abstract}
