\begin{abstract}
Metamorphic testing is the standard answer to the oracle problem in Web security
testing: \mbox{MST-wi} defines \ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic metamorphic
relations (MRs) over a Web-specific domain language, and reports a specificity of
\ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\%. We show that, for object-level authorization, these
relations delegate adjudication to a \emph{GUI-reachability model} that is a
\emph{crawl-time snapshot}, and that this delegation fails along a deterministic
direction rather than as noise.

From a three-phase instantiation of the predicates as originally defined---crawl,
out-of-band authorization change, execute---a \ResSynRecords{}-instance factorial
study and a controlled study on Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, replicated on Gogs
\GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}, we characterise the failure
as three boundaries. (i) An access legitimately granted out of band after the
crawl is reported as a \emph{proven} violation---a \emph{false proof}---because
the predicate has only two representations and one is unavailable. (ii) Repairing
this with a readable authorization oracle makes the MR step unnecessary in that
oracle-available regime: replacing
it with a single ordinary request changes nothing measurable, a descriptive,
point-estimate comparison (not a formal non-inferiority test), and not
domination. (iii) The reported specificity therefore rests
on an undeclared premise---that the reachability model coincides with the true
authorization state---and on an input-level denominator blind to scenario-level
failure.

On Gitea the mismatch appears only under a crawl-time reading and never live,
locating the mechanism in \emph{time} rather than visibility; and the oracle that
would discharge the premise is readable from an owner's vantage but never from a
black-box subject's own. We confirm the predicates' semantics at source and
execute the published relations---their source unmodified, with the build
configuration and a replay harness added---reproducing the false proof on a real
system. We contribute a characterisation, a measurement caveat, and one negative
result.
\end{abstract}
