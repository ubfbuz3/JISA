\begin{abstract}
Metamorphic testing is an established approach to the Web-security oracle
problem: \mbox{MST-wi} defines \ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic
relations (MRs) over a Web-specific domain language, reporting
\ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\% specificity. For the reachability-gated authorization
relations examined here, the predicates delegate adjudication to a
\emph{GUI-reachability model} that is a \emph{crawl-time snapshot}, and that
delegation fails deterministically, in one direction.

We obtain three findings from a three-phase instantiation of the predicates as
defined---crawl,
out-of-band authorization change, execute---a \ResSynRecords{}-record
scenario/configuration study, and a Gitea \ResRsVersion{} study replicated on
Gogs \GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}. (i) Access legitimately granted out of band after the crawl is reported as a \emph{proven}
violation---a \emph{false proof}---because one of its two representations is
unavailable. (ii) Repairing this with a readable, sound oracle that fully
resolves the proposition leaves the MR step adding no adjudication
information: a
necessary condition, since a direct consumer of the same oracle and outcome
reproduces the verdict, and an ordinary request changes nothing
measurable (a descriptive point estimate, not a
non-inferiority test, and not domination). (iii) The reported specificity is not
conditioned on a reachability--authorization check, and its input-level
denominator cannot resolve a scenario-level failure. The study instantiates
\ResDesignMrs{} reachability-gated relations; the wider catalogue is support
evidence.

On Gitea the mismatch appears only under the crawl-time reading, never live: it
tracks the reading time, not visibility. The oracle that would discharge
the condition is readable from an owner's vantage but never a black-box
subject's, and executing the published relations reproduces the false proof
within the gated subset that fires.
\end{abstract}
