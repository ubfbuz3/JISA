\section{Background: the predicates in question}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{The oracle problem and metamorphic relations}

An oracle decides whether an execution has failed. For memory-safety or
injection faults the oracle can often be a crash, a sanitizer, or a schema. For
authorization there is no such intrinsic signal: the vulnerable and the correct
execution are both successful HTTP responses. Metamorphic testing replaces the
absent oracle with a relation over multiple executions: execute the same
operation under a transformed configuration, and require that the two observed
outcomes stay in a specified relation. The relation becomes the oracle.

The price is a new dependency. The relation is only as sound as the assumptions
under which it is expected to hold, and those assumptions are, for authorization,
exactly the facts that the absent oracle was supposed to supply.

\subsection{How \mbox{MST-wi} decides an authorization MR}

\mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic} builds on the earlier
metamorphic security testing design of Mai et al.~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}. It
introduces a Security Metamorphic Relation Language (SMRL) with
\ResLitSmrlFunctions{} Web-specific functions, and a catalog of
\ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic MRs. Its architecture has two stages: a
crawler builds a model of the application as reached through the user interface
and records, per user, which URLs and contents that user could reach; then a
generator emits follow-up inputs derived from a seed input and the MRs are
checked against the recorded model.

The predicates that guard an authorization MR belong, in the artifact we
inspected, to a small family. Two of them read the recorded reachability model
directly---a ``cannot reach through the GUI'' predicate and a ``user can
retrieve content'' predicate---and two read static properties of the acting
subject, namely whether the subject is a supervisor of another or a site
administrator. We say that such a predicate is \emph{GUI-derived} when its value
is a function of the crawl.

\subsection{Two facts about the catalog, both counted mechanically}

We parsed the authors' public artifact rather than reasoning about the language
in the abstract. Two counts matter for what follows.

\paragraph{The gate is GUI-derived, and the catalog is mostly not about
authorization} Over the \ResCatalogMrTotal{} MRs in the catalog,
\ResCatalogGuiDerived{} (\ResCatalogGuiDerivedPct{}\%) gate on a GUI-derived
predicate, and \ResCatalogAnyPred{} gate on a GUI-derived or
identity-property predicate. Only \ResCatalogAuthzName{} MRs are named
\texttt{OTG\_AUTHZ} and only \ResCatalogChangeCred{} call the credential-change
action; their union is \ResCatalogUnion{} (\ResCatalogUnionPct{}\%), of which
\ResCatalogUnionGated{} are gated on a GUI-derived or identity predicate. The
honest reading of these numbers is a caveat, not a scandal: depending on the
count, the authorization-relevant share of the catalog is between
\ResCatalogAuthzName{}/\ResCatalogMrTotal{} and
\ResCatalogUnion{}/\ResCatalogMrTotal{}, and the union is an \emph{upper bound}
on authorization relevance, not a measurement. We report all counts together
for exactly this reason, and we do not claim that authorization coverage is
thin: the authors' own accounting shows that they address \ResLitAuthzAddressed{}
of \ResLitAuthzWeakAll{} weaknesses under the \emph{Authorize Actors} design
principle, i.e.\ \ResLitAuthzAddressedPct{}\%, which ranks
\ResLitAuthzRank{}-rd of the principles they cover.

\paragraph{The vocabulary contains no authorization-artifact-state
predicate} We searched the full text of the original paper for predicates that
read the \emph{state of an authorization artifact}---ownership, an access
predicate, a permission record, a share. Words such as \code{isOwner},
\code{canAccess}, \code{ownerOf}, \code{hasPermission}, \code{ownership} and
\code{shares\_of} occur \ResCatalogAuthzStateHits{} times in total. This is not
a discovery; it is the necessary consequence of the vocabulary being the one
listed in the paper, and we treat it as a premise throughout. The predicates
that \emph{are} present occur with the counts
\ResVocabisSupervisorOf{}/\ResVocabcannotReachThroughGUI{}/\ResVocabuserCanRetrieveContent{}/\ResVocabisAdmin{}
respectively, which is what makes the delegation to reachability unavoidable.

\subsection{Why this makes the reading moment part of the specification}

Suppose a subject's entitlement to an object changes after the crawl: the owner
grants it out of band, or the object is made public, or an administrative action
grants access. Nothing in the two-stage architecture observes that change,
because the model the MR consults was frozen at crawl time. The MR will then
evaluate reachability against a world that no longer exists.

This is not a bug report about an implementation. It is a statement about what
the relations \emph{mean}: because they are defined over a recorded reachability
model, their verdict is a statement about that model. Our experiments are
constructed so that the two readings---the crawl-time one, and a live one---can
be told apart, so that we can report which one is responsible for an observed
disagreement.
