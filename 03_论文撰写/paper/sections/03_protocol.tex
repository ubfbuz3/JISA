\section{Protocol: a faithful three-phase instantiation}
\label{sec:protocol}

Two instruments carry distinct claims. The three-phase instantiation below
carries the quantitative results of Sections~\ref{sec:synthetic}
and~\ref{sec:realsystem}; a direct execution of the library's own
relations---source unmodified, with the build configuration and a replay harness
added---carries one qualitative claim, that the published code produces the
false proof we predict (Section~\ref{sec:native-run}). Neither instrument is a
benchmark: the standing scope and non-claims are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:threats}, and neither yields a claim of detector accuracy.

\paragraph{Three phases}
(A)~\emph{Crawl}: record, per subject and object, whether the subject can reach
the object through the interface, and freeze that record---the model the
predicates will consult. (B)~\emph{Change authorization out of band}: alter a
(subject, object) entitlement through a channel outside the crawled interface;
the frozen model is not updated, because the architecture has no mechanism to
update it. (C)~\emph{Execute the MR} against the current state while the
predicates consult the frozen model. Phase~B is what the original evaluation
does not exercise, and it is where the reachability stand-in can be wrong
(Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}).

\paragraph{The protocol is frozen, and it was amended twice}
\label{sec:frozen}
We describe the protocol as \emph{frozen} rather than as pre-registered, because
the two words make different promises and only the first one is true here. The
base protocol was pinned by digest (hash \texttt{\TmpProtocol}) before the first
cell of the first run was observed, and the runs carry that digest in their
records; but the rules a run followed are not all rules that were written before
any observation. Two amendments were made. Amendment~A1 demoted the secondary
calibration vantage from a pass/fail gate to a recorded diagnostic, on the ground
that a credential which cannot read the surface cannot distinguish ``no grant''
from ``no visibility'', so gating on that column would confound a product's
choice of readable endpoints with a failure of the oracle; it scopes to the second
product only, and the first product's record keeps the original gate and the
original base digest. Amendment~A2 records a non-local side effect of an
out-of-band mutation found in an archived pilot---removing a user's group
membership also removes that user's direct membership in the group's
projects---and it is the reason the ledger is \emph{not} patched with a rule
learned from observation: encoding it would make $E$ depend on a behaviour we
learned from $Y$, which is the failure this design exists to avoid. The pilot is
archived, and no cell of it enters any denominator.
Which rules a given run followed is recoverable from that run's own
\texttt{amendment\_id} and \texttt{amendment\_sha256} fields rather than from a
timestamp, and we make no claim that A2 predates the first transition of the
reported run: the digest is bound by the run record, whereas A2's self-reported
write time falls after that run's last probe. We do not correct the field in
place, because doing so would change the digest the run binds, and we state the
inconsistency rather than paraphrase it away.
What no amendment touched is fixed as written: the ground-truth source, the
abstention exits, the \emph{unknown} policy, the denominator conventions, and the
prohibition on letting $Y$ or $O$ reach $E$. The two rules the frozen text leaves
genuinely open---how an \emph{unknown} entitlement is scored, and how an alarm
becomes a claim---are treated below and in
Section~\ref{sec:stages} as explicit choices, each with its sensitivity reported
(Tables~\ref{tab:caliber} and~\ref{tab:promotion}).

\paragraph{Alarm, claim, and truth}
\label{sec:stages}
Three objects are kept apart throughout, and no result mixes them. The
\emph{alarm} $A$ is what the technique itself returns: for the relations studied,
$A=y\wedge\neg m$, the conjunction of a successful request with the absence of
the subject from the frozen reachability model. It asserts that a metamorphic
relation failed; it asserts nothing about authorization, and it has no ledger, no
expected decision and no verdict behind it. A \emph{claim} $H$ is the promotion of
an alarm to ``violation-proven'' by a policy $P$ that we state rather than
inherit---the catalogue's purpose invites that reading, but its code does not
perform it. The \emph{truth} $T$ is computed independently, from the ledger's
entitlement $E$ and the content-level effect $Y$. Promotion is therefore an
adjudication policy layered on the technique's native output, not part of it, and
the reported false-proof count is a property of the pair $(A,P)$, not of $A$
alone. Section~\ref{sec:policy} quantifies how far the choice of $P$ moves the
published conclusion, and Section~\ref{sec:native-run} applies the same
separation to the unmodified library.

\paragraph{Adjudicator variants and internal controls}
A raw alarm becomes a verdict through an exception policy, and different policies
are different methods: \texttt{raw} (the alarm itself, with no exception handling
at all---this is the baseline any filter must be read against); \textbf{v1} (an
\emph{existence} filter: any sharing record on the object downgrades the alarm,
regardless of whom it names or what it permits); \textbf{v2} (existence plus
operation coverage: the record must cover the operation the MR triggered);
\textbf{v3} (state-calibrated---consults
a readable authorization introspection surface, abstains when none is readable);
\texttt{degrade\_all} (the floor: abstain on every alarm);
\texttt{direct\_only} (authorization surface only, access outcome ignored---a
deliberately unfair control); and \texttt{direct\_observe} (a succeeding plain
request checked against the same readable surface). The last consumes the same
observable quantity as the MR family and decides the negative result.

\paragraph{Ground truth}
Every quantitative result names its ground-truth source, and it is never the
detector under test. For the real-system experiments the three quantities come
from different places and are kept apart: $E$ (entitlement) is read from the
runner's own ledger of authorization events, recorded at mutation time and
independent of the interfaces under test; $Y$ (effect) is the content-level
outcome of a probe by the subject's own credential (for writes, owner-side
re-reads and deletion re-reads rather than status codes); $O$ (the
introspection surface) is diagnostic only and never substitutes for either.
That the ledger and the content probes agreed cell by cell is a measured result
of the runs, not a definition, and the oracle's disagreements with the ledger
are reported as findings, not absorbed. Construction state carries the
constructed system and the scenario/configuration study; the pinned route
tables carry the grant census; the seed list carries the seeded target.

\paragraph{Unknown and failure handling}
Each predicate can fail to yield a bit, and the handling is fixed by the frozen
protocol (Section~\ref{sec:frozen}) before any cell is observed, not negotiated per
result. The failure modes are distinct and are stated as implemented.
\emph{Transport-level retries:} an HTTP request that fails to connect (status~0) is
re-issued up to two more times; this retry exists for the network, not for the
transaction. \emph{$E$ undefined:} an entitlement-changing transaction that is
refused or otherwise does not commit is not retried at the transaction level---the
runner issues it once, and the cell is marked \emph{failed} (reason
\texttt{mutation\_failed}) and excluded from every denominator, with the exclusion
count reported alongside the result. \emph{$Y$ undefined:} an execution error or
timeout leaves the cell unscored, never coerced to \emph{allow} or \emph{deny}---in
the native engine run, \NtMrTotal{} relations were attempted, \NtMrExecuted{}
executed and \NtMrErrored{} errored, and the errored one is reported rather than
silently dropped. \emph{$O$ undefined:} the introspection surface may return no
definite answer; the state-calibrated method abstains on those cells by
construction, and the channel table reports them in an explicit \emph{unanswered}
column (\TmpUnansGitea{} Gitea and \TmpUnansGitlab{} GitLab cells for the anonymous
reader, \GgChUnans{} on Gogs' authenticated reader) instead of absorbing them into
\emph{deny}. On those cells $E$ and $Y$ remain scoreable---an unanswered oracle
removes the oracle-based adjudicators, not the cell. The twelve-row truth table is
defined over $E\in\{\text{allow},\text{deny}\}$; an $E$-undefined cell falls outside
its rows and is reported as excluded, not reassigned.

\paragraph{The \emph{unknown} rule is a scoring choice, and both calibers are reported}
$E$ is \texttt{unknown} when an object is absent from the ledger model. For a
modelled object with no recorded granting channel the frozen text promised
\texttt{unknown} (``the ledger models no channel for the triple''), while the
shipped implementation returns a genuine \emph{deny}, reserving \texttt{unknown} for
the absent-object case and for the one channel the protocol singles out as
unmodellable (deploy keys). We disclose this divergence rather than reconcile it
silently, and we do not settle it by keeping the more favourable reading: both are
reported. Closed-world default deny is what the main analysis uses; the literal
reading marks those cells $E$-unknown and excludes them from scoring. Neither
reading touches a single alarm, because every stale false proof falls on an
$E$-allow cell: the \TmpBothStaleFP{} stale false proofs and the \TmpBothLiveFP{}
live ones are identical under both. What the rule moves is the population the
metrics are computed over, and it moves it far enough to change the reported rate
by a factor of about four. Under default deny all \TmpBothCells{} committed cells
are scorable, the \TmpBothStaleFP{} false proofs are a share of \CalImpShare{}\% of
that set, and specificity is \CalImpSpec{}\%; under the literal rule the scorable
set shrinks to the \TmpBothLitKnown{} cells whose entitlement the ledger fixes, the
same false proofs are a share of \CalLitShare{}\% of it, and specificity falls to
\CalLitSpec{}\%. The literal rule is not an improvement: it raises the observed
share only by discarding the \TmpBothLitUnk{} cells on which the adjudication
happened to be right, and it leaves coverage at \CalLitCov{}\% of the committed
cells. Precision is zero in both calibers and recall is undefined in both, because
this grid contains no violation and therefore no positive; specificity and the
false-proof share are reported with their denominators and are not a false
discovery rate. Table~\ref{tab:caliber} gives the two confusion matrices side by
side.

\paragraph{The closed-world assumption is bounded, not verified}
Default deny is sound only if the ledger enumerates every granting channel, and
that assumption cannot be established from inside the run. We therefore state what
it rests on and what it would cost to be wrong. It holds \emph{by construction} for
the frozen topologies used here: every grant written in the setup phase went through
a modelled channel (owner, organisation owner, site administrator, public
visibility, direct collaborator, team or group inheritance), and no other granting
channel was exercised by the setup or mutation phase of these runs. It is also
partially self-checking. An unrecorded grant that the system in fact enforces would
surface as $E\neq Y$ on a probed cell, and $E=Y$ holds on all \TmpBothEeqY{} of
\TmpBothCells{} cells here---a measured result rather than a definition, and not a
tautology, since the oracle disagrees with $E$ on \TmpBothOracleDisagree{} cells in
the same runs. What the check cannot see is an unrecorded grant on a cell nobody
probed, a channel that exists but is never exercised, or a grant the system also
fails to enforce. Deployment reality adds channels this design never instantiates:
directory and SSO group synchronisation, deploy keys (named by the protocol itself
as unmodellable), external identity providers, and grants written straight to the
database.
Table~\ref{tab:ledger} asks a narrower question, and answers it by intervention
rather than by assertion. For each channel in turn its grant rows are deleted from
every cell's recorded basis list and the damaged entitlement is scored, with the
scoring executed rather than re-derived. A cell that carries an alarm is passed to
the same classifier the runs use, so its category is whatever that classifier
returns; a cell that carries no alarm is counted as an \emph{apparent} false
negative when the damaged label reads deny and the probe allowed, which is the
category the classifier would assign to a denial nobody reported. The closed-form
counts are asserted equal to the executed ones, so an analytic slip fails the
build rather than reaching this table. Nothing a subject is
permitted to do changes, because a ledger is a record and not the system: every
cell that moves here remains a legitimate access, and the \PolAlarms{} false proofs
are not repaired by its moving, only re-described. The alarm cannot move either---it
is computed from the crawl snapshot, the probe response and the role, and never
reads the ledger. What the deletion changes is the reference label, and with it the
words used for outcomes that did not change. Omitting public visibility re-labels
\LedPublicDeny{} legitimate cells as deny in the damaged ledger; against that label
\LedPublicTp{} existing alarms score as \emph{apparent} true positives and
\LedPublicFn{} legitimate accesses as \emph{apparent} false negatives, and the
reported false-proof count falls to \LedPublicFp{}. Site administration is the
extreme case, where \LedSiteAdminTp{} of the \PolAlarms{} alarms become apparent
true positives and no access becomes an apparent miss. Cells resting on a second
channel are untouched, which is why the re-labelled counts fall short of the
resting ones: \LedPublicShared{} on public visibility, \LedCollaboratorShared{} on
direct collaborator and \LedTeamShared{} on team inheritance.

Read inside this analysis, the effect is one-directional and it is an undercount.
With the cell sample, the alarms, the probe responses and the promotion decisions
all held fixed, and only legitimate grant rows deleted under default deny, a
damaged label can lower the reported false-proof count but cannot raise it. That,
and only that, is what we claim. It is not a bound for unrecorded authorization
mechanisms in general, it says nothing about channels this design never exercised,
and it does not transfer to a deployment where the ledger may fail in the other
direction. What it does establish is that the false-proof rate is conditioned on
the completeness of the reference record, and that the conditioning is not benign:
incompleteness hides false proofs from the measurement rather than exposing them.
Under the literal rule the same cell instead leaves the denominator, the other horn
of the same dilemma.

One consequence of this must be kept apart from the label argument. A promotion
policy that reads the ledger instead of being fixed in advance does not merely lose
its contradictions---it acts on them. The \LedPublicTp{} cells re-labelled above
are exactly the alarms a ledger-gated policy would promote once it consulted the
damaged ledger, because its rule is to promote where the ledger records a definite
deny. Its zero in Table~\ref{tab:promotion} is a statement about a sound ledger and
not a property that survives a damaged one; the two analyses touch the same cells
from opposite directions and should not be merged.

Missing or modified input records fail the pipeline at entry: the SHA-256 manifests
over inputs and outputs shipped with the artefact are checked before any generator
runs, so a modified input is rejected before it can reach a table, and a missing one
aborts rather than degrades; the pipeline never substitutes a default value for an
absent record. The source-state facts of the native run are generated from frozen
upstream inputs (commit, digests and patch manifest) rather than a live checkout, so
the historical statements are recomputable from the archive alone.

\paragraph{Scenarios and design cells}
Seven scenarios separate the reasons an MR can misfire: no shared object (S1,
S2); an in-band legitimate share visible in the framework log (S3); an unshared
plain violation (S4); a read-only share plus a write violation (S5); an
out-of-band share invisible to the framework log (S6); an out-of-band share plus
a genuine violation (S7). They are crossed with \ResSynConfigs{} configurations
defined by instantiation mode, enforcement correctness, channel visibility and
introspection availability. The nominal cross is
$\ResDesignScenarios{}\times\ResSynConfigs{}$ combinations,
but four are inadmissible: the two no-exception configurations
(\texttt{V-noE3-vuln0}, \texttt{V-noE3-vuln1}) are undefined under the two
out-of-band scenarios (S6, S7), where the legitimate-sharing exception does not
apply. This leaves \ResDesignCells{} admissible design cells.

Table~\ref{tab:design} gives the complete cross, factor by factor: the levels of
each, how it is varied, the number of records at each level, and what it is
crossed with. Table~\ref{tab:design-census} in Section~\ref{sec:support} gives
the cell-by-cell census---eight configurations by seven scenarios, with a dash
wherever a combination is undefined. Two dimensions are nested rather than
crossed, and we state them here instead of letting the tables imply a factorial
that was not run: channel visibility exists only where the application offers a
sharing facility, so the two no-exception configurations sit outside it; and the
listed level was instantiated only with introspection on, so the combination
\emph{listed and unreadable} is untested rather than shown to be impossible:
a product can list a share and still refuse to answer the per-object
introspection query, and this enumeration would not see it.

\subsection{What the design varies, and what it can identify}
\label{sec:identify}

Because the exception policy and the reachability reading are thrown per record
rather than held per configuration, every record has a partner that differs in
that switch alone. Table~\ref{tab:attribution} uses that: it pairs records on
configuration, scenario, relation and the other switch, and reports the
discordant pairs. A contrast carried by some other factor would show up as
movement in the paired cells; a contrast that is not there shows up as a zero.

\input{attribution_table.tex}

Three results come out of it, and they are stronger than the aggregate rates
they explain. First, the reachability reading moves the outcome in one direction only:
of \DsgGuiPairs{} blind--aware pairs, \DsgGuiFixedFP{} lose a false proof when
the reading is aware and \DsgGuiNewFP{} gain one, while the same switch costs
\DsgGuiLostTP{} detections and gains none. The aware reading is therefore a
trade in a fixed direction, not noise around a rate. It is a statement about
what the gate can see, not about when authorization changed: the constructed
study seeds the out-of-band share before the run and never mutates entitlement
inside it, so the temporal reading of this contrast belongs to
Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}, not here. Second, the exception ladder has
\emph{no} discordant pair at either step: raw and e1 differ on no individual
record, and neither do e1 and e1e2. That is a stronger statement than the
aggregate rate not moving---\DsgExcKeptFP{} records carry a false proof under all
three policies---and it says the two exception predicates are, on this system,
inert rather than merely weak. Third, oracle readability acts only where there
is something to resolve: in the violating configuration \DsgReadGained{} of
\DsgReadPairs{} paired records change from \texttt{violation-proven} to
\texttt{indeterminate} when the surface is switched off, none the other way; in
the correct configuration \DsgReadNullPairs{} pairs change not at all.

What the design cannot identify follows from the two nested dimensions, and we
prefer to name it. The interaction between channel visibility and readability is
not estimable, because the listed-and-unreadable cell was not run; the
readability contrast is identified within unlisted only, and we make no claim
that readability moderates the listed channel. The legitimate-sharing mechanism
is not identified under S6--S7, where it is undefined by construction, so the
out-of-band estimates are for the e3 configurations alone. The two relations are
not orthogonal to scenario---S5 carries MR-002 only---so we never compare
relations with each other. What remains is balanced: every
(exception policy, reachability reading) slice holds \DsgBalOn{} readable and
\DsgBalOff{} unreadable records, so the readability contrast cannot be carried by
either thrown switch.

\paragraph{Statistical treatment}
The \ResSynRecords{} records are not independent observations. Each admissible
cell is expanded deterministically by the factors that are actually varied:
\ResDesignMrs{} metamorphic relations (MR-002, MR-004), \ResDesignPrecondVariants{}
exception-policy variants (raw, one level, two levels---the first deliberately
thrown switch), and \ResDesignGuiModes{} reachability readings (a constant
unreachable list and the listing current at execution---the second deliberately
thrown switch). The second is a visibility manipulation, not a temporal one. The two relations do not both apply to every cell: only MR-002 applies
under scenario~S5, so \ResDesignCellsOneMr{} S5 cells carry one relation while
the remaining \ResDesignCellsTwoMr{} carry two. The record count is therefore
\[
\ResSynRecords{} = \ResDesignCellsTwoMr{}\cdot\ResDesignMrs{}\cdot\ResDesignPrecondVariants{}\cdot\ResDesignGuiModes{}
+ \ResDesignCellsOneMr{}\cdot\ResDesignPrecondVariants{}\cdot\ResDesignGuiModes{},
\]
a deliberately unbalanced design---the per-cell multiplier differs by
scenario. The records are therefore a fixed enumeration, not a sample: we report
counts, rates and design-cell denominators, and we attach no inferential
interval to any of them. Where a headline count has a small denominator we say
so explicitly (the anchored rate, \ResFpEthreeHits{}/\ResFpEthreeTotal{}, is
unstable by construction) and lead with the larger-denominator count of the same
phenomenon. No conclusion rests on statistical significance. Where a comparison
is paired---the ablation of Section~\ref{sec:synthetic-ablation}---we report
discordant-pair counts rather than an interval over a difference. The
replication ceiling is
explicit: one constructed system, \ResDesignCells{} cells, \ResDesignMrs{} MRs,
three real systems.

\paragraph{Tooling and code provenance}
Development of the analysis scripts was assisted by an AI coding assistant
(declared in the Declarations). Every script that assistant touched was
reviewed, executed, and its output checked by the authors against the raw
records; the released pipeline recomputes every reported scalar from those
records, every table is generated by the pipeline, and the macro files and the
two auto-generated tables share a single source of truth. No AI tool
participated in the detection loop or in any adjudication.
