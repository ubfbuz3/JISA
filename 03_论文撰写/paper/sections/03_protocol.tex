\section{Protocol: a faithful three-phase instantiation}
\label{sec:protocol}

Two instruments carry distinct claims. The three-phase instantiation below
carries the quantitative results of Sections~\ref{sec:synthetic}
and~\ref{sec:realsystem}; a direct execution of the library's own
relations---source unmodified, with the build configuration and a replay harness
added---carries one qualitative claim, that the published code produces the
false proof we predict (Section~\ref{sec:native-run}). Neither instrument is a
benchmark or yields a claim of detector accuracy; the standing scope and
non-claims are collected in Section~\ref{sec:threats}.

\paragraph{Three phases}
(A)~\emph{Crawl}: record, per subject and object, whether the subject can reach
the object through the interface, and freeze that record---the model the
predicates will consult. (B)~\emph{Change authorization out of band}: alter a
(subject, object) entitlement through a channel outside the crawled interface;
the frozen model is not updated, because the architecture has no mechanism to
update it. (C)~\emph{Execute the MR} against the current state while the
predicates consult the frozen model. Phase~B is what the original evaluation
does not exercise, and where the reachability stand-in can be wrong
(Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}).

\paragraph{The protocol is frozen, and it was amended twice}
\label{sec:frozen}
We describe the protocol as \emph{frozen} rather than as pre-registered, because
only the first word is true here. The base protocol was pinned by digest (hash
\texttt{\TmpProtocol}) before the first cell of the first run was observed, and
the runs carry that digest in their records; but the rules a run followed are not
all rules that were written before any observation. Amendment~A1 demoted the
secondary calibration vantage from a pass/fail gate to a recorded diagnostic, and
scopes to the second product only. Amendment~A2 records a non-local side effect
found in an archived pilot---removing a user's group membership also removes that
user's direct membership in the group's projects---and it is why the ledger is
\emph{not} patched with a rule learned from observation: encoding it would make
$E$ depend on a behaviour learned from $Y$. The pilot is archived and no cell of
it enters any denominator. Which rules a run followed is recoverable from its
\texttt{amendment\_id} and \texttt{amendment\_sha256} fields rather than from a
timestamp, and A2's self-reported write time falls after the reported run's last
probe; we retain the field as recorded rather than correct it in place, because
doing so would change the digest the run binds. What no amendment touched is
fixed as written: the ground-truth source, the abstention exits, the
\emph{unknown} policy, the denominator conventions, and the prohibition on
letting $Y$ or $O$ reach $E$. The two rules left genuinely open---how an
\emph{unknown} entitlement is scored, and how an alarm becomes a claim---are
treated as explicit choices with their sensitivity reported
(Tables~\ref{tab:caliber} and~\ref{tab:promotion}).

\paragraph{Alarm, claim, and truth}
\label{sec:stages}
Three objects are kept apart throughout. The \emph{alarm} $A$ is what the
technique returns: $A=y\wedge\neg m$, the conjunction of a successful request with
the absence of the subject from the frozen reachability model. It asserts that a
metamorphic relation failed; it asserts nothing about authorization, and it has no
ledger, no expected decision and no verdict behind it. A \emph{claim} $H$ is the
promotion of an alarm to ``violation-proven'' by a policy $P$ that we state rather
than inherit---the catalogue's purpose invites that reading, but its code does not
perform it. The \emph{truth} $T$ is computed independently, from the ledger's
entitlement $E$ and the content-level effect $Y$. Promotion is therefore an
adjudication policy layered on the technique's native output, not part of it, and
the reported false-proof count is a property of the pair $(A,P)$.
Section~\ref{sec:policy} quantifies how far the choice of $P$ moves the published
conclusion, and Section~\ref{sec:native-run} applies the same separation to the
unmodified library.

\paragraph{Adjudicator variants and internal controls}
A raw alarm becomes a verdict through an exception policy, and different policies
are different methods: \texttt{raw} (no exception handling---the baseline any filter
must be read against); \textbf{v1} (an \emph{existence} filter: any sharing record on
the object downgrades the alarm); \textbf{v2} (existence plus operation coverage);
\textbf{v3} (state-calibrated---consults a readable authorization introspection
surface, abstains when none is readable); \texttt{degrade\_all} (the floor: abstain
on every alarm); \texttt{direct\_only} (authorization surface only, access outcome
ignored---a deliberately unfair control); and \texttt{direct\_observe} (a succeeding
plain request checked against the same readable surface), which decides the negative
result.

\paragraph{Ground truth}
Every quantitative result names its ground-truth source, and it is never the detector
under test. $E$ (entitlement) is read from the runner's own ledger of authorization
events, recorded at mutation time and independent of the interfaces under test; $Y$
(effect) is the content-level outcome of a probe by the subject's own credential (for
writes, owner-side re-reads rather than status codes); $O$ (the introspection surface)
is diagnostic only and never substitutes for either. That ledger and content probes
agreed cell by cell is a measured result, and the oracle's disagreements with the
ledger are reported as findings, not absorbed.

\paragraph{Unknown and failure handling}
The failure modes are distinct and stated as implemented. \emph{Transport-level
retries:} a request that fails to connect (status~0) is re-issued up to two more times.
\emph{$E$ undefined:} an entitlement-changing transaction that is refused or does not
commit is issued once, marked \emph{failed} (\texttt{mutation\_failed}) and excluded
from every denominator, with the exclusion count reported---none in the runs of
Section~\ref{sec:temporal}. \emph{$Y$ undefined:} an
execution error or timeout leaves the cell unscored, never coerced to \emph{allow} or
\emph{deny}---in the native engine run, \NtMrTotal{} relations were attempted,
\NtMrExecuted{} executed and \NtMrErrored{} errored. \emph{$O$ undefined:} the
state-calibrated method abstains on those cells by construction, and the channel table
reports them in an explicit \emph{unanswered} column (\TmpUnansGitea{} Gitea and
\TmpUnansGitlab{} GitLab cells for the anonymous reader, \GgChUnans{} on Gogs'
authenticated reader) instead of absorbing them into \emph{deny}.

\paragraph{The \emph{unknown} rule is a scoring choice, and both calibers are reported}
$E$ is \texttt{unknown} when an object is absent from the ledger model. For a
modelled object with no recorded granting channel the frozen text promised
\texttt{unknown}, while the shipped implementation returns a genuine \emph{deny},
reserving \texttt{unknown} for the absent-object case and for deploy keys. We disclose
this divergence rather than reconcile it silently, and report both readings. Closed-world
default deny is what the main analysis uses; the literal reading marks those cells
$E$-unknown and excludes them from scoring. Neither reading touches a single alarm,
because every stale false proof falls on an $E$-allow cell: the \TmpBothStaleFP{}
stale false proofs and the \TmpBothLiveFP{} live ones are identical under both.
What the rule moves is the population the metrics are computed over, far enough to
change the reported rate by a factor of about four. Under default deny all
\TmpBothCells{} committed cells are scorable, the \TmpBothStaleFP{} false proofs are
a share of \CalImpShare{}\% of that set, and specificity is \CalImpSpec{}\%; under
the literal rule the scorable set shrinks to the \TmpBothLitKnown{} cells whose
entitlement the ledger fixes (\CalLitCov{}\% of the committed cells), the same false
proofs are a share of \CalLitShare{}\%
of it, and specificity falls to \CalLitSpec{}\%. The literal rule is not an
improvement: it raises the observed share only by discarding the \TmpBothLitUnk{}
cells on which the adjudication happened to be right. Precision is zero in both
calibers and recall is undefined in both, because this grid contains no violation.

\paragraph{The closed-world assumption is bounded, not verified}
Default deny is sound only if the ledger enumerates every granting channel, and that
assumption cannot be established from inside the run. It holds \emph{by construction} for the
frozen topologies used here: every grant written in the setup phase went through a modelled
channel (owner, organisation owner, site administrator, public visibility, direct
collaborator, team or group inheritance). It is also partially self-checking: an unrecorded
grant the system in fact enforces would surface as $E\neq Y$ on a probed cell, and $E=Y$ holds
on all \TmpBothEeqY{} of \TmpBothCells{} cells---a measured result rather than a definition,
since the oracle disagrees with $E$ on \TmpBothOracleDisagree{} cells in the same runs. What
the check cannot see is an unrecorded grant on a cell nobody probed, a channel that exists but
is never exercised, or a grant the system also fails to enforce; deployment adds directory and
SSO group synchronisation, deploy keys, external identity providers, and grants written
straight to the database.

Table~\ref{tab:ledger} answers a narrower question by intervention. For each channel in turn
its grant rows are deleted from every cell's recorded basis list and the damaged entitlement is
scored, with the scoring executed rather than re-derived: an alarmed cell is passed to the same
classifier the runs use, and an unalarmed cell counts as an \emph{apparent} false negative when
the damaged entitlement is deny and the observed effect is allow. The closed-form counts are
asserted equal to the executed ones. Nothing a subject is permitted to do changes, because a
ledger is a record and not the system: every cell that moves here remains a legitimate access,
and the \PolAlarms{} false proofs are not repaired by its moving, only re-described. The alarm
cannot move either---it is computed from the crawl snapshot, the probe response and the role,
and never reads the ledger. The intervention adds one premise: deleting one channel's rows does
not cascade into another channel's entitlement, and assertions on the \LedMultiBasis{}
multi-channel cells verify only that the implementation preserves an alternative recorded basis.
Omitting public visibility re-labels \LedPublicDeny{} legitimate cells as deny; against that
label \LedPublicTp{} existing alarms score as \emph{apparent} true positives and \LedPublicFn{}
legitimate accesses as \emph{apparent} false negatives, and the reported false-proof count falls
to \LedPublicFp{}. Site administration is the extreme case, where \LedSiteAdminTp{} of the
\PolAlarms{} alarms become apparent true positives and no access becomes an apparent miss.
Cells resting on a second channel are untouched: \LedPublicShared{} on public visibility,
\LedCollaboratorShared{} on direct collaborator and \LedTeamShared{} on team inheritance.

With the sample, the alarms, the probe responses and the promotion decisions all held fixed, and
only legitimate grant rows deleted under default deny, a damaged label can lower the reported
false-proof count but cannot raise it: incompleteness hides false proofs from the measurement
rather than exposing them. A promotion policy that reads the ledger rather than being fixed in
advance acts on those contradictions, so the \LedPublicTp{} re-labelled cells are exactly the
alarms a ledger-gated policy would promote, and its zero in Table~\ref{tab:promotion} is a
statement about a sound ledger rather than a property that survives a damaged one.

\paragraph{Scenarios and design cells}
Seven scenarios separate the reasons an MR can misfire: no shared object (S1,
S2); an in-band legitimate share visible in the framework log (S3); an unshared
plain violation (S4); a read-only share plus a write violation (S5); an
out-of-band share invisible to the framework log (S6); an out-of-band share plus a
genuine violation (S7). They are crossed with \ResSynConfigs{} configurations
defined by instantiation mode, enforcement correctness, channel visibility and
introspection availability. The nominal cross is
$\ResDesignScenarios{}\times\ResSynConfigs{}$ combinations, but four are
inadmissible: the two no-exception configurations (\texttt{V-noE3-vuln0},
\texttt{V-noE3-vuln1}) are undefined under S6 and S7, where the
legitimate-sharing exception does not apply. This leaves \ResDesignCells{}
admissible design cells.

Table~\ref{tab:design} gives the complete cross factor by factor; the cell-by-cell census
ships with the artifact. Two dimensions are
nested rather than crossed: channel visibility exists only where the application
offers a sharing facility, so the two no-exception configurations sit outside it;
and the listed level was instantiated only with introspection on, so \emph{listed
and unreadable} is untested rather than shown to be impossible.

\subsection{What the design varies, and what it can identify}
\label{sec:identify}

Because the exception policy and the reachability reading are thrown per record
rather than held per configuration, every record has a partner that differs in that
switch alone. Table~\ref{tab:attribution} pairs records on configuration, scenario,
relation and the other switch, and reports the discordant pairs.

\input{attribution_table.tex}

Three results come out of it. First, the reachability reading moves the outcome in
one direction only: of \DsgGuiPairs{} blind--aware pairs, \DsgGuiFixedFP{} lose a
false proof when the reading is aware and \DsgGuiNewFP{} gain one, while the same
switch costs \DsgGuiLostTP{} detections and gains none. It is a statement about what
the gate can see, not about when authorization changed: the constructed study seeds
the out-of-band share before the run and never mutates entitlement inside it, so the
temporal reading belongs to Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}. Second, the exception
ladder has \emph{no} discordant pair at either step: raw and e1 differ on no
individual record, and neither do e1 and e1e2, so the two exception predicates are,
on this system, inert rather than merely weak. Third, oracle readability acts only
where there is something to resolve: in the violating configuration
\DsgReadGained{} of \DsgReadPairs{} paired records change from
\texttt{violation-proven} to \texttt{indeterminate} when the surface is switched
off, none the other way; in the correct configuration \DsgReadNullPairs{} pairs
change not at all.

What the design cannot identify follows from the two nested dimensions. The
interaction between channel visibility and readability is not estimable, because the
listed-and-unreadable cell was not run; the readability contrast is identified
within unlisted only. The legitimate-sharing mechanism is not identified under
S6--S7, where it is undefined by construction, so the out-of-band estimates are for
the e3 configurations alone. The two relations are not orthogonal to
scenario---S5 carries MR-002 only---so we never compare relations with each other.
What remains is balanced: every (exception policy, reachability reading) slice holds
\DsgBalOn{} readable and \DsgBalOff{} unreadable records.

\paragraph{Statistical treatment}
The \ResSynRecords{} records are not independent observations: each admissible cell is
expanded deterministically by the factors actually varied---\ResDesignMrs{} metamorphic
relations (MR-002, MR-004), \ResDesignPrecondVariants{} exception-policy variants, and
\ResDesignGuiModes{} reachability readings. Only MR-002 applies under scenario~S5, so
\ResDesignCellsOneMr{} S5 cells carry one relation while the remaining
\ResDesignCellsTwoMr{} carry two, giving
\[
\ResSynRecords{} = \ResDesignCellsTwoMr{}\cdot\ResDesignMrs{}\cdot\ResDesignPrecondVariants{}\cdot\ResDesignGuiModes{}
+ \ResDesignCellsOneMr{}\cdot\ResDesignPrecondVariants{}\cdot\ResDesignGuiModes{}.
\]
The records are a fixed enumeration, not a sample: we report counts, rates and
design-cell denominators and attach no inferential interval to any of them. Where a
headline count has a small denominator we lead with the larger-denominator count of the same
phenomenon (the anchored rate, \ResFpEthreeHits{}/\ResFpEthreeTotal{}, is unstable by
construction). The replication ceiling is one constructed system, \ResDesignCells{} cells,
\ResDesignMrs{} MRs, three real systems.
