\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Metamorphic security testing} The lineage begins with Mai et
al.~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic} and continues with \mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic},
whose own admission that the role-to-resource mapping is ``not always available''
we build on deliberately. We contribute no new relation but a characterisation of
where this family's adjudication stops discriminating.

\paragraph{API testing and access-control detection} Specification-driven
fuzzers generate requests well but inherit the same oracle difficulty~\cite{Atlidakis2019RESTler,Arcuri2024Advanced,Seran2025Handling};
a recent line targets access control directly by black-box probing, policy
checks, or LLM-driven generation~\cite{Liu2025BACScan,Sahin2026Enhancing,Uddin2026Automated,Schlaubitz2025A},
and policy-inference approaches recover the enforced policy from the
implementation~\cite{Huang2024Detecting,Le2021Automated}. BOLA is further
examined as an attack surface and empirically in deployed
systems~\cite{Filho2025Automated,Wu2026Rethinking,Chehade2025Forbidden}. Our
question is orthogonal to all of these: not a better detector, but what an
existing oracle-substituting technique actually decides when the entitlement
information it needs is not among its observables.

\paragraph{LLMs and the oracle problem} LLMs are used for vulnerability detection
and specification inference~\cite{Wang2024A,Wen2024Enchanting,Wang2026Measuring,LeCong2025Can};
we use none in the detection loop---our negative result argues for keeping the
decision in a deterministic component explicit about what it can observe, and an
LLM-driven extension would still face Boundary~1. Studies asking where a
technique's assumptions break, rather than proposing improvements, are rarer than
they should be; the transferable condition this paper exposes is that any claim
of necessity must be tested against a fair substitute.
