\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Metamorphic security testing} The lineage this paper interrogates
begins with metamorphic security testing for Web systems~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic},
which introduced system-agnostic relations comparing the behaviour of authorized
and unauthorized users, and continues with the \mbox{MST-wi}
design~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}, which generalises it into
\ResLitMrCatalog{} catalogued relations over a \ResLitSmrlFunctions{}-predicate
domain language. Our contribution is not a new relation but a characterisation of
where this family's adjudication stops discriminating, and of what the reported
metric can and cannot see. We are explicitly building on the design's own
admission that the role-to-resource mapping is ``not always
available''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}.

\paragraph{Black-box and specification-driven API testing} RESTler derives
producer--consumer dependencies from an API specification and prunes
sequences with response feedback~\cite{Atlidakis2019RESTler}. Search-based
approaches extend white-box heuristics to REST APIs and to service
interactions~\cite{Arcuri2024Advanced,Seran2025Handling}. These systems generate
requests well but inherit the same oracle difficulty: a successful response
cannot by itself distinguish an authorized from an unauthorized access. Our
Boundary~1 describes what happens when the missing oracle is supplied by a
reachability model rather than by the system.

\paragraph{Dedicated broken-access-control detection} A recent line targets
access control directly, using black-box probing~\cite{Liu2025BACScan},
access-policy-violation checks inside fuzzers~\cite{Sahin2026Enhancing},
LLM-driven test generation for microservice
authorization~\cite{Uddin2026Automated}, automated detection of function- and
object-level access-control flaws~\cite{Schlaubitz2025A}, and Java-specific
access-control vulnerability detection
\cite{Shi2025Facilitating,Feng2025JAuthGuard}. Database-level and
policy-inference approaches recover the enforced access-control policy from the
implementation, either to find missing owner checks~\cite{Huang2024Detecting,Liu2025MOCGuard}
or to extract the policy itself~\cite{Le2021Automated,Berger2020Static,Zhang2024Extracting,Zuo2017AUTHSCOPE}.
Our question is orthogonal to all of these: we do not propose a better detector,
we ask what an existing oracle-substituting technique actually decides when the
entitlement information it needs is not among its observables.

\paragraph{Automated BOLA attack detection and the shape of the problem} Closely
related work constructs the object-level attack and detects
it~\cite{Filho2025Automated}, re-examines BOLA under a zero-trust
framing~\cite{Wu2026Rethinking}, or evaluates broken access control empirically
in deployed systems~\cite{Chehade2025Forbidden}. These establish that the problem
matters and that it is empirically present; our contribution is a
methodological measurement of one class of oracle substitution, not a new
prevalence estimate.

\paragraph{LLM-based vulnerability detection and specification inference} Large
language models are used broadly for vulnerability
detection~\cite{Guo2022HyVulDect,Yang2024DLAP,Liu2024Enhancing}, and there are
efforts to extract privilege-related facts or program specifications with their
help~\cite{Wang2024A,Wen2024Enchanting}, including
work measuring the reasoning boundaries of LLMs on implicit
specifications~\cite{Wang2026Measuring,LeCong2025Can}. We do not use an LLM in
the detection loop: our
negative result is an argument for keeping the \emph{decision} in a
deterministic component and being explicit about what that component can observe.
An LLM-driven extension of this work would still face Boundary~1, because the
missing observable is missing regardless of who reasons about it.

\paragraph{The oracle problem as a first-class object} Finally, we note the
methodological framing this paper belongs to. Studies that ask where a testing
technique's assumptions break---rather than proposing an
improvement---are rarer than they should be, and in our own case the boundary was
found by a control rather than by a hypothesis: the negative result of
Section~\ref{sec:synthetic-ablation} was produced by replacing our own mechanism
with the most naive consumer of the same observable. We regard the condition this
exposes---that any claim of necessity must be tested against a fair
substitute---as the transferable part of this work.
