\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Broken object-level authorization (BOLA), ranked first in the OWASP API Security
Top~10~\cite{OWASP2023API1}, is the failure to enforce, per object, that the
authenticated subject is entitled to the object being touched. It is attractive
to attackers precisely because the request is well formed and the endpoint is
reachable: only the \emph{entitlement} is wrong.

Detecting it is an \emph{oracle} problem. A test that fires a request and reads
the response cannot, by itself, tell a legitimate access from an illegitimate
one, because a vulnerable system answers both with the same success status. The
standard answer in the literature is \emph{metamorphic testing}: instead of
asserting an expected output, assert a relation that must hold across several
executions. \mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic} is the most
developed instance of this idea for Web systems. It contributes a catalog of
\ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic metamorphic relations (MRs) written in a
domain-specific language whose vocabulary contains
\ResLitSmrlFunctions{} Web-specific predicates, and it reports that it
``automatically detected \ResLitReportedDetection{}\% of their vulnerabilities''
with a specificity of \ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\%.

This paper is about what happens to that claim when the object of the assertion
is \emph{object-level authorization}. Our starting observation is not a
measurement but a reading of the relations themselves. To decide
object-level authorization, an MR must know whether the acting subject is
entitled to the object. \mbox{MST-wi}'s predicates do not express entitlement;
they express what its own authors describe as reachability---what ``the
functionality exposed by the user interface'' permits. The authors are explicit
that this is a stand-in, and that the information it stands in for can be
missing: to determine the test outcome, ``we need a mapping between roles and
resources, \textbf{which is not always available}''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}.
We take that sentence as our premise rather than our finding.

If the adjudication of an MR is delegated to a \emph{reachability} model, then
the model's reading of the system---and in particular the moment at which it was
read---becomes part of the specification. We therefore instantiate the
predicates \emph{as they are defined}, i.e.\ relative to a reachability model
recorded at crawl time, and we drive the system through three phases: crawl,
change authorization out of band, then execute the MR. The middle phase is the
one the original design does not exercise, because in its evaluation the
authorization state is stable across the crawl and the run.

Under that faithful instantiation we run a
\ResSynRecords{}-instance factorial
study over a constructed system, and a controlled study on Gitea
\ResRsVersion{}. We do not propose a new mechanism; an earlier iteration of this
work did, and our own ablation killed it (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic},
Block~9). What survives is a characterisation of where the MR family stops
being informative, stated as three boundaries: two that squeeze the technique
from either side, and a third in the metric that would otherwise have reported
it.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The adjudication as designed produces deterministic false proofs.}
      Taken as published---raw alarms, or alarms filtered by bookkeeping
      exceptions---it reports a legitimate access granted out of band after the
      crawl as an already-established violation: a \emph{false proof} at rate
      \ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} on the out-of-band scenario
      (\ResObsOobVtwoFalseProof{} of \ResObsOobFalseAlarm{} alarms) and at rate
      \ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{} on Gitea
      (\ResRsMrBookkeepingFp{} of \ResRsAuthorizedCells{} authorized cells). The
      direction is deterministic rather than noisy, because the predicate has
      only two representations available and one of them cannot describe an
      out-of-band grant.
\item \textbf{When a sound, readable authorization oracle is available, the
      metamorphic step is not empirically necessary in our evaluated scenarios.}
      Repairing (i) by consulting a readable introspection surface
      works, but it is the \emph{oracle} that does the work, not the relation:
      replacing the metamorphic step with a single ordinary request changes
      nothing measurable---false proofs \ResAbDirectObserveFP{} against
      \ResAbVThreeFP{}, recall \ResAbDirectObserveRec{} against
      \ResAbVThreeRec{}.       We report a descriptive, point-estimate
      comparison---not a formal hypothesis test (no margin was fixed in
      advance)---and not domination: the difference of \ResAbRecallDiff{} is not
      distinguishable
      from zero (95\% CI $[\ResAbRecallDiffLo{}, \ResAbRecallDiffHi{}]$). On the
      \ResAbDisagreementN{} records where the two methods disagree it is the
      MR-based method that errs, its reachability precondition blocking a
      detection the plain request makes.
\item \textbf{The reported specificity rests on an undeclared premise and on a
      low-resolution denominator.} The premise is that the reachability model
      coincides with the true authorization state; the denominator is the count
      of generated \emph{inputs}, which is structurally insensitive to a failure
      that is uniform across a whole scenario class.
\end{enumerate}

The boundaries are not a claim that \mbox{MST-wi} is wrong. Two of them are
anticipated in the original literature: the reachability stand-in is announced,
and the predecessor design attributes its own residual false alarms to crawler
coverage of ``all URLs for all users''~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}. Our
contribution is to locate the failure \emph{mechanically}---it is a function of
\emph{when} the reachability model was read, not of \emph{which} objects are
visible---and to attach it to the reported metric.

\paragraph{Research questions}
The characterisation is organised around three research questions, one per
boundary. \textbf{RQ1} (temporal): does a crawl-time GUI-reachability proxy
systematically misclassify authorisation changes that occur after the crawl?
\textbf{RQ2} (oracle): when a sound, readable authorisation oracle is available,
does metamorphic adjudication provide measurable benefit over a direct request
that consumes the same observable? \textbf{RQ3} (measurement): can an
input-level specificity metric reveal failures that are uniform at the scenario
level?

\paragraph{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A three-phase instantiation of \mbox{MST-wi}'s reachability predicates
      that makes the crawl-time reading explicit, together with two internal
      control methods that consume the same observables and therefore bound any
      claim about the MR step (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}).
\item A \ResSynRecords{}-instance factorial study
      (\ResSynErrors{} errors) whose design has \ResDesignCells{} independent
      cells and \ResDesignMrs{} MRs, reported at that granularity
      (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}).
\item A controlled study on Gitea \ResRsVersion{} in which the mismatch appears
      under a crawl-time reading (\ResRsMismatchSnapshot{}/\ResRsMismatchDenom{})
      and never under a live reading (\ResRsMismatchLive{}/\ResRsMismatchDenom{}),
      locating the mechanism in time rather than in visibility; and in which the
      oracle that would discharge the premise is readable from the
      owner/administrator vantage (\ResRsOwnerReadable{}/\ResRsOwnerReadableTotal{})
      but never from a black-box subject's own vantage
      (\ResRsBlackboxSelfReadable{}/\ResRsBlackboxSelfCells{})
      (Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}).
\item A replication of the same protocol on Gogs \GgVersion{} and GitLab
      \GlVersion{}---one lineage-related, one independent---where the only
      readable oracles are readable but unsound, wrong on \GgOracleWrong{}
      (Gogs) and \GlOracleWrong{} (GitLab) of \GgCells{} cells, and the
      oracle-gated adjudicators convert those blind spots into \GgFpVthree{}
      and \GlFpVthree{} (v3) false proofs that the sound oracle of Gitea
      never produced (Section~\ref{sec:gogs}).
\item An execution of the library's own authorization relations---their source
      unmodified, with the build configuration (\texttt{pom.xml}) and a replay
      harness added---against that system: the legitimate entitlement granted
      after the crawl is
      answered ``cannot reach'' \NtKeyCrtgTrue{} times and \NtKeyMrFired{}
      relations raise an alarm on it, so the false proof is attributable to the
      published code rather than to our reading of it
      (Section~\ref{sec:native-run}).
\item One negative result: for this problem the MR step carries no work that a
      plain request does not carry, and we show it with a control that consumes
      the same observable quantity, rather than with a straw man
      (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}, Block~9).
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:boundaries} states the three boundaries and the condition under
which the first of them may be lifted; Section~\ref{sec:threats} lists what our
evidence cannot support.
