\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Broken object-level authorization (BOLA), first in the OWASP API Security
Top~10~\cite{OWASP2023API1}, is the failure to enforce, per object, that the
authenticated subject is entitled to the object touched. Detecting it is an
\emph{oracle} problem: vulnerable and correct systems answer with the same
success status. Metamorphic testing is an established approach to that oracle
problem, and \mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic} is a substantial
system-agnostic instance for Web systems: \ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic
relations (MRs) over \ResLitSmrlFunctions{} Web-specific predicates, reported to
detect \ResLitReportedDetection{}\% of vulnerabilities with specificity
\ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\%.

This paper asks what happens to that claim when the asserted property is
object-level authorization. The predicates do not express entitlement; they
express what their authors call reachability---what ``the functionality exposed
by the user interface'' permits---with the role-to-resource mapping
``\textbf{not always available}''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}. We take
that sentence as premise, not discovery. Delegating adjudication to a reachability
model makes the moment the model was read part of the specification, so we
instantiate the predicates \emph{as defined}---over a model recorded at crawl
time---and drive the system through three phases: crawl, change authorization
out of band, execute. The middle phase is the one the original evaluation does
not exercise.

We ran a \ResSynRecords{}-record scenario/configuration study on a constructed
system and a controlled study on Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, extended, with a
methodological variation, to Gogs
\GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}. We propose no mechanism. What survives is three findings
(Section~\ref{sec:findings}), one per research question:

\emph{Finding~1 (RQ1, temporal).} The adjudication as designed produces
\emph{deterministic false proofs}---a legitimate access granted out of band
after the crawl is reported as a proven violation, at rate
\ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} on the constructed system and \ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{}
on Gitea, because the predicate's two representations cannot describe an
out-of-band grant; the error is a property of the \emph{reading}, not of the
system.

\emph{Finding~2 (RQ2, oracle).} When a sound, readable authorization oracle fully
resolves the proposition under test, the metamorphic step carries no additional
decision-relevant information beyond what the oracle-plus-request pair already
determines---a necessary condition (Proposition~\ref{lem:oracle-suff}). Empirically,
within the evaluated scenarios, a single
ordinary request provides no observed incremental adjudication benefit over the
MR step: the paired methods disagree on only \ResAbDisagreementN{} records, all
favoring \texttt{direct\_observe} (false proofs \ResAbDirectObserveFP{} vs.~\ResAbVThreeFP{}, recall \ResAbDirectObserveRec{} vs.~\ResAbVThreeRec{}; a descriptive, point-estimate comparison, not a formal hypothesis test, no margin pre-specified, not domination).

\emph{Finding~3 (RQ3, measurement).} The reported specificity is not conditioned
on an explicit check that the reachability model coincides with the authorization
state, and is computed in units insensitive to scenario-uniform failure.

The native execution, the seeded target and the second implementation are support
evidence for these findings, not further headline findings
(Section~\ref{sec:support}).

\paragraph{Research questions}
The three findings answer one question each:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{RQ1 (temporal).} Does the crawl-time reachability model remain
sound for authorization decisions after an out-of-band authorization change?
\item \textbf{RQ2 (oracle).} When a readable, sound authorization oracle fully
resolves the proposition under test, does the metamorphic step add incremental
adjudication information beyond the oracle and the request outcome?
\item \textbf{RQ3 (measurement).} Can an input-level specificity metric provide
stable scenario-level resolution for a scenario-uniform failure, or can the
failure be arbitrarily diluted by unaffected inputs?
\end{itemize}
Section~\ref{sec:findings} answers each in turn.

The findings are not a claim that \mbox{MST-wi} is wrong: the reachability
stand-in is announced by its authors, and the predecessor design attributes its
residual false alarms to crawler coverage~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}. Our
contribution is therefore a \emph{methodological characterisation}, not a new
detector, and has three layers: (i)~\emph{mechanistic characterisation}---we
locate the false-proof failure structurally, in \emph{when} the reachability
model was read rather than which objects are visible (Finding~1);
(ii)~\emph{fair-control ablation}---under a readable, sound authorization oracle,
the metamorphic step adds no measurable adjudication information beyond the
oracle and the request outcome, established by a same-observable direct
adjudicator (Proposition~\ref{lem:oracle-suff}, Finding~2); and (iii)~\emph{metric
characterisation}---we identify a specific failure mode in a published
specificity figure: it is not conditioned on the
reachability--authorization correspondence its interpretation assumes, and its
input-level denominator cannot register a class-uniform failure (Finding~3,
Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}). The evidence---the three-phase instantiation
with internal controls (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), the scenario/configuration
study (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}), the Gitea study with an endogenous control
and a seeded-vulnerability positive control (Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}), the
source reading, the native execution of the library's own relations, and the
second implementation (Section~\ref{sec:support})---supports these layers rather
than standing as further headline claims.
