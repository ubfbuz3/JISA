\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Broken object-level authorization (BOLA), first in the OWASP API Security
Top~10~\cite{OWASP2023API1}, is the failure to enforce, per object, that the
authenticated subject is entitled to the object touched. Detecting it is an
\emph{oracle} problem: vulnerable and correct systems answer with the same
success status. The standard answer is metamorphic testing, and
\mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic} is its most developed instance
for Web systems: \ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic relations (MRs) over
\ResLitSmrlFunctions{} Web-specific predicates, reported to detect
\ResLitReportedDetection{}\% of vulnerabilities with specificity
\ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\%.

This paper asks what happens to that claim when the asserted property is
object-level authorization. The predicates do not express entitlement; they
express what their authors call reachability---what ``the functionality exposed
by the user interface'' permits---with the role-to-resource mapping
``\textbf{not always available}''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}. We take
that sentence as premise, not finding. Delegating adjudication to a reachability
model makes the moment the model was read part of the specification, so we
instantiate the predicates \emph{as defined}---over a model recorded at crawl
time---and drive the system through three phases: crawl, change authorization
out of band, execute. The middle phase is the one the original evaluation does
not exercise.

We ran a \ResSynRecords{}-record scenario/configuration study on a constructed
system and a controlled study on Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, replicated on Gogs
\GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}. We propose no mechanism; an earlier
iteration of this work
did, and our own ablation removed it. What survives is a characterisation as
three boundaries (Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}): (i)~the adjudication as
designed produces \emph{deterministic false proofs}---a legitimate access
granted out of band after the crawl is reported as a proven violation, at rate
\ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} on the constructed system and \ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{}
on Gitea, because the predicate's two representations cannot describe an
out-of-band grant; (ii)~when a sound, readable authorization oracle is
available, the metamorphic step is not empirically necessary in our evaluated
scenarios---replacing it with a single ordinary request changes nothing
measurable (false proofs \ResAbDirectObserveFP{} vs.~\ResAbVThreeFP{}, recall
\ResAbDirectObserveRec{} vs.~\ResAbVThreeRec{}; a descriptive, point-estimate
comparison, not a formal hypothesis test, no margin pre-specified, not
domination), and on all \ResAbDisagreementN{} disagreeing records the MR-based
method errs; (iii)~the reported specificity rests on an undeclared premise
(reachability model $=$ authorization state) and an input-level denominator
insensitive to scenario-uniform failure. These are organised as RQ1 (temporal),
RQ2 (oracle), RQ3 (measurement).

The boundaries are not a claim that \mbox{MST-wi} is wrong: the reachability
stand-in is announced by its authors, and the predecessor design attributes its
residual false alarms to crawler coverage~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}. Our
contribution is to locate the failure mechanically---in \emph{when} the model
was read, not \emph{which} objects are visible---and to attach it to the
reported metric: a three-phase instantiation with internal controls
(Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); the scenario/configuration study
(Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}); the Gitea study with an endogenous control, a
seeded-vulnerability positive control, and a native execution of the library's
own relations attributing the false proof to the published code
(Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}); and the boundaries with an admissibility
condition and a formal statement of the metric's resolution
(Section~\ref{sec:boundaries}).
