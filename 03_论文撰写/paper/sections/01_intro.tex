\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\PARstart{B}{roken} object-level authorization (BOLA), first in the OWASP API
Security Top~10~\cite{OWASP2023API1}, is the failure to enforce, per object, that the
authenticated subject is entitled to the object touched. Detecting it is an
\emph{oracle} problem: vulnerable and correct systems answer with the same
success status. Metamorphic testing is an established approach to that oracle
problem, and \mbox{MST-wi}~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic} is a substantial
system-agnostic instance for Web systems: \ResLitMrCatalog{} system-agnostic
relations (MRs) over \ResLitSmrlFunctions{} Web-specific predicates, reported to
detect \ResLitReportedDetection{}\% of vulnerabilities with specificity
\ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\%.

This paper asks what happens to that claim when the asserted property is
object-level authorization. The predicates do not express entitlement; they
express what their authors call reachability---what ``the functionality exposed
by the user interface'' permits---with the role-to-resource mapping
``\textbf{not always available}''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}. We take
that sentence as premise, not discovery. Delegating adjudication to a reachability
model makes the moment the model was read part of the specification, so we
instantiate the predicates \emph{as defined}---over a model recorded at crawl
time---and drive the real systems through three phases: crawl, change
authorization out of band, execute. The middle phase is the one the original
evaluation does not exercise. The constructed study isolates the same defect
without time: it seeds a share outside the framework's record and asks whether
a gate that cannot see it still calls the access legal.

We ran a \ResSynRecords{}-record scenario/configuration study on a constructed
system and a controlled study on Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, extended, with a
methodological variation, to Gogs
\GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}. We propose no mechanism. What survives is
three findings, one per research question (Section~\ref{sec:findings}).

\paragraph{Research questions and findings}
(1)~\emph{Temporal (RQ1).} Does the crawl-time reachability model remain sound
for authorization decisions after an out-of-band authorization change? No: the
adjudication as designed produces \emph{deterministic false proofs}---a legitimate
access the model cannot see is reported as a proven violation once an alarm is
promoted, at rate \ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} in the constructed study, where the share
is seeded outside the framework's record, and at rate
\ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{} on Gitea, where the grant is made between the crawl and
the request. The predicate's two representations cannot describe either grant. The
error is a property of the \emph{reading}, not of the system. The alarm and the
count of proven violations are separable, and we keep them apart: no policy changes
what the relations return, while the number of contradictable claims runs from
none, through \PolPallFalse{} under promote-every-alarm, to \PolPoracleFalse{} under
the oracle-gated variant (Table~\ref{tab:promotion}). Reporting the promotion
policy is therefore part of reporting the finding, not a footnote to it.
(2)~\emph{Oracle (RQ2).} When a readable, sound authorization oracle fully
resolves the proposition under test, does the metamorphic step add incremental
adjudication information beyond the oracle and the request outcome? Not on the
evaluated domain---and there the answer is a construction rather than a
measurement: the direct adjudicator is built from the same entitlement and outcome
quantities that define the reference label, so the two rules \emph{cannot} part
where the oracle agrees with the ledger (Section~\ref{sec:boundary2}). Agreement on
that domain is an identity, and we present it as one rather than as evidence that
direct observation detects better. What is empirical is whether the domain is
reached: the antecedent is an oracle that is readable, sound \emph{and} admissible,
and no system studied here supplies one
(Section~\ref{sec:admissibility}). Where it fails, substituting the direct
adjudicator for the relation makes matters worse rather than better---false proofs
\GgFpDirectObserve{} against \GgFpVthree{} on Gogs and \GlFpDirectObserve{} against
\GlFpVthree{} on GitLab (Table~\ref{tab:gogs-cross})---so the practical advice is
conditional on an admission test, not a blanket replacement.
(3)~\emph{Measurement (RQ3).} Can a pooled specificity over follow-up inputs
provide stable scenario-level resolution for a failure confined to a class? No:
unaffected inputs dilute it, and a class the crawl never reaches contributes no
negative inputs at all, so the figure is silent about that class
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}). The published labels are manually
verified; the limit is the denominator and the input set, not the labels.

The native execution, the seeded target and the second implementation are support
evidence for these findings, not further headline findings
(Section~\ref{sec:support}).

\paragraph{What is new here, and what is not}
Two of the ingredients are already established, and we neither re-derive nor claim
them: that a crawl-time reachability model can go stale or under-enumerate (the
predecessor attributes its residual false alarms to crawler
coverage~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}), and that a reachability predicate is not an
entitlement predicate (its authors say the role-to-resource mapping is ``not always
available''~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}). A third---that a pooled specificity
is a weak instrument for class-level failure---is familiar from the measurement
literature and is not offered as a discovery
either~\cite{Croft2023Data}. Section~\ref{sec:related} sets each claim beside the
prior position it extends and names the check that would count against it, so a
reader can see which sentences carry new content. What is new is the following, and
only this.
(i)~\emph{A representation argument, not a coverage observation.} We show that the
false proof is a deterministic consequence of which of the predicate's two
representations a grant fits, hence fixed in direction, and that the variable that
governs it is \emph{when} the model was read rather than what was visible---a
distinction the coverage explanation does not make, and one that also tells
staleness (repairable by recrawling) from channel blindness (not repairable by the
same recrawl;
Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}). (ii)~\emph{The alarm/policy separation, quantified.}
The technique returns an alarm; treating it as a violation is a policy we state, and
we report what four policies make of the same alarms
(Section~\ref{sec:policy}). (iii)~\emph{A conditional equivalence with a named
antecedent.} Promotion through the relation is a subset of direct adjudication once
an admissible oracle exists, which bounds the technique's added value from above
without asserting that any deployed oracle satisfies the antecedent---and we apply an
explicit admission test that, on the systems tested, refuses it
(Section~\ref{sec:admissibility}). (iv)~\emph{A denominator argument about the
published metric.} A specificity pooled over the inputs a crawl could enumerate
cannot register a failure confined to a class that crawl did not reach
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}).
The scope is a characterization of metamorphic testing for object-level
authorization in Web/API systems, not a new detector. Although the technique is
general, every empirical claim here is scoped to reachability-gated authorization
relations, and we state where the transfer stops. The evidence---the three-phase
instantiation with internal controls (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}), the
scenario/configuration study (Section~\ref{sec:synthetic}), the Gitea study with an
endogenous control and a seeded-vulnerability positive control extended to four
defect classes (Section~\ref{sec:realsystem}), and the source reading, the native
execution of the library's own relations and the second implementation
(Section~\ref{sec:support})---supports these four items rather than standing as
further headline claims.
