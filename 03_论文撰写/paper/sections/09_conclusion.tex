\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Metamorphic testing substitutes for a missing oracle by asserting a relation
across executions. For object-level authorization the relation must know
whether the acting subject is entitled to the object, and \mbox{MST-wi}'s
predicates do not know that: they know what the interface, as crawled,
permitted. We instantiated the predicates as defined---against a reachability
model recorded at crawl time---and drove a constructed system through
\ResSynRecords{} records and a real system, Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, through
\ResRsCells{} cells under two readings.

Three boundaries came out of it. Taken as designed---raw alarms, or alarms
filtered by bookkeeping exceptions---the adjudication reports a legitimate access
granted out of band after the crawl as a proven violation, at rate
\ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} on the constructed system and
\ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{} on Gitea; it does so deterministically, in one
direction, because the predicate has two representations and an out-of-band
grant fits only the wrong one. The natural repair, consulting a readable
authorization oracle, works---and, in that oracle-available regime, makes the
metamorphic step unnecessary in our evaluated scenarios, because
replacing it with a single ordinary request changes nothing measurable (false
proofs \ResAbDirectObserveFP{} against \ResAbVThreeFP{}, a tie at zero; recall
difference \ResAbRecallDiff{}, 95\% CI
$[\ResAbRecallDiffLo{}, \ResAbRecallDiffHi{}]$, not distinguishable from zero),
and on the \ResAbDisagreementN{} records where the two differ it is the MR-based
method that fails. We report a descriptive, point-estimate comparison rather
than superiority; because no margin was fixed in advance, no formal
hypothesis-test claim attaches to it. On the real
system the mismatch appears under the crawl-time reading
(\ResRsMismatchSnapshot{}/\ResRsMismatchDenom{}) and never under a live reading
(\ResRsMismatchLive{}/\ResRsMismatchDenom{}), with an endogenous control that
holds final visibility fixed; the responsible variable is time, not visibility.
And the reported specificity of \ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\% is conditional on a
premise it does not state and is computed in units---generated inputs---that
cannot resolve a scenario-uniform failure.

One methodological result deserves separating from the three boundaries. The
arguments above could have been made---and in an earlier draft were made---from the
published \emph{definitions} of the two predicates, which is weak evidence, since
a definition can be read generously. Reading the implementation instead
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}) confirms the semantics and adds two facts the published
text does not state: the crawl-time model is frozen by a cache that is never
invalidated, and a missing crawl makes the predicate report ``unreachable'', so
the MR's precondition is satisfied by an absence of data rather than by evidence.
That supplies a mechanism for the deterministic direction we report, and it is a
mechanism that a definition cannot supply.

We did not set out to write this paper. Our first version proposed a mechanism,
the state-calibrated adjudicator, and our own ablation removed it: once the
metamorphic step is replaced by the most naive consumer of the same observable,
the mechanism's advantage disappears (\ResAbDirectObserveFP{} false proofs
against \ResAbVThreeFP{}), and its precision had been bought with
\ResAbVThreeFN{} false negatives. What is left is what we can defend: a
characterisation, a measurement caveat, and one negative result.

\paragraph{What we do not claim} We do not claim that \mbox{MST-wi} is wrong,
and we claim nothing about its accuracy as a detector. We read its engine
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}) and we ran it (Section~\ref{sec:native-run}); the run
establishes a qualitative outcome, for one gate family, on one real system
that, on the tested endpoints and transitions, enforces the expected
decisions---not a rate. Its authors
announce the reachability stand-in,
and its predecessor attributes its own residual false alarms to crawler
coverage. We do not claim a field prevalence for out-of-band grants; our channel
proportions are a design parameter. We do not claim that our two MRs represent
the catalog; they are two, of one family, and that is the ceiling on everything
we say. And we do not claim to have found a better detector.

\paragraph{What we would do next, and what it would cost} We have taken the
obvious first step: the library's own relations now execute
(Section~\ref{sec:native-run}), which lifts the false-proof side from our two
relations to \NtMrExecuted{} of the catalog's. That does not lift the ceiling
that matters most. The run cannot measure detection, because the system is
correct; and the relations it executes share one gate family, which is the thing
we set out to characterise, so a broader catalog of the same kind would broaden
the illustration without testing the boundary. Two things would move this from a
characterisation into something stronger, and both remain expensive. The first
is a real system that \emph{contains} an object-level authorization flaw, so that
the detection side can be measured and not only the false-proof side. The second
is relations whose gate is \emph{not} the absence of evidence---checkable
against a subject's entitlements without pretending that entitlements are
reachable facts---which is a construction problem, not a measurement problem.

\paragraph{The transferable part} The condition that killed our mechanism is the
one we would put in front of any similar claim: a component's necessity must be
tested against a substitute that consumes the \emph{same} observable quantity as
the ground truth. A substitute that consumes less does not establish
incremental value: surviving it does not show the component does anything.
