\section{Synthetic study: where the adjudication goes}
\label{sec:synthetic}

This study supplies the evidence for Findings~1 and~3, and the ablation behind
Finding~2; the findings themselves are stated in Section~\ref{sec:findings}.

\subsection{Definitions}
\label{sec:metrics}

Confusions are defined over the \emph{verdict}: a \emph{false proof} is the strict
subset of false alarms promoted to \texttt{violation-proven}---an error the tool
asserts as established. We also report the \emph{detection-keep rate} (share of fired
true violations still alarmed) and count only abstentions justified by an unreadable
oracle.

\subsection{What the constructed system shows}

\paragraph{Attribution is clean} On the anchored configuration the false-proof rate
is \ResFpEthreeHits{}/\ResFpEthreeTotal{}. With that denominator the rate is unstable
by construction, so we attach to it neither an interval nor a percentage. With the
legitimate-sharing exception absent the rate is \ResBaseFpRate{}
(\ResBaseFpHits{}/\ResBaseFpTotal{}), and with the channel listed it is
\ResListedCtlFpRate{} (\ResListedCtlFpHits{}/\ResListedCtlFpTotal{}). The phenomenon
is therefore attributable to a legitimate sharing exception the MR cannot know
about, not to execution itself. A $2{\times}2$ grid over channel visibility and
enforcement correctness tells the same story: on unlisted cells the false-proof rate
is \ResBEThreevulnZerounlistedFpPct{}\% with no false negatives; on listed cells it
drops to \ResBEThreevulnZerolistedFpPct{}\% but one of the five violating records in
that cell is a false negative (\ResBEThreevulnOnelistedTp{} detected,
\ResBEThreevulnOnelistedFn{} missed). Avoiding false proofs and keeping detection are
controlled by one switch, and the switch cannot be thrown both ways at once.

\paragraph{Filtering exceptions does not help}
The exception ladder---raw, one exception level, two---leaves the anchored rate at
\ResLadderrawRate{}, \ResLaddereOneRate{}, \ResLaddereOneeTwoRate{}: it does not
move. The exception levels we can express are themselves functions of the same
reachability model, so they inherit its blind spot.

\paragraph{The framework cannot see the channel that matters}
When a legitimate share was created through the framework's facility, its log holds
\ResObsFwLogRecords{} records and v2 produces \ResObsFwVtwoFalseProof{} false proofs
(rate \ResObsFwVtwoFpRate{}); when the same share was created out of band, the log
holds \ResObsOobLogRecords{} records---no evidence the share exists---and v2's false
proofs rise to \ResObsOobVtwoFalseProof{} of \ResObsOobFalseAlarm{} alarms (rate
\ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{}), every one promoted to \texttt{violation-proven}. The MR
cannot distinguish ``this share does not exist'' from ``this share exists but was not
crawled''; its only representation of the first is the second. On S7, where a genuine
violation \emph{does} sit behind the out-of-band share, the same blindness makes the
alarm right for the wrong reason (\ResObsOobBolaVtwoProvenBola{} of
\ResObsOobBolaBolaTotal{} reached)---the coincidence an input-level metric cannot
unpack. The same tension appears within a single MR: for S3 (must not alarm) vs S5
(must alarm), no configuration in the family is correct on both---the choice of
switch is a choice of \emph{which} error to make (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenarios}).

\paragraph{Stratification, not aggregation}
Aggregated, v3 looks best (recovery \ResVdVThreeRecoveryRate{} against
\texttt{degrade\_all}'s keep rate \ResVdDegradeallKeepRate{}); the reading is wrong,
because v3 changes regime with oracle readability. Stratified over the
\ResIntroOnN{}-record readable grid and its \ResIntroOffN{}-record absent grid (a
representative grid; Section~\ref{sec:stratum} re-runs the comparison over all
\ResSynRecords{} records): on the readable grid it removes v2's false proofs
(\ResIntroOnVthreeFpN{} vs.~\ResIntroOnVtwoFpN{}) while still alarming on all
\ResIntroOnVthreeProvenBola{} fired true violations; on the absent grid
(\ResIntroOffN{}) it alarms on none of the \ResIntroOffVtwoProvenBola{} true
violations v2 still alarms on, abstaining \ResIntroOffAbstain{} times. Pooling
converts honest abstention into apparent performance.

\subsection{Fair ablation: does the metamorphic step add adjudication information?}
\label{sec:synthetic-ablation}

Our earlier iteration proposed v3 as a mechanism, resting on the claim that the
metamorphic step was necessary. We tested that claim by removing the step and
substituting the most naive consumer of the same observable. The distinction
matters: \texttt{direct\_only}---oracle yes, access outcome ignored---produces
\ResAbDirectOnlyFP{} invalid violation claims (binary false positives, none of them
strict false proofs; every one falls on a request the server denied) against v3's
\ResAbVThreeFP{} (precision \ResAbDirectOnlyPrec{}), and rejecting it proves nothing
because it discards half the evidence. The fair control is \texttt{direct\_observe}:
a succeeding ordinary request plus the same readable surface, consuming exactly what
the MR-based adjudicator consumes.

\subsection{Coverage, selective risk, and what a pooled precision does not say}
\label{sec:coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} scores the alarm. A method that abstains is counted as not
having alarmed, so the abstention lands in TN or in FN: right for ``does the tool report a
violation'', wrong for ``does the method decide''. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} rescores the
same \CvgN{} records on the decision. v3 decides \CvgVthreeDecided{} of them---coverage
\CvgVthreeCoveragePct{}\%---abstaining on \CvgVthreeAbstain{} (\CvgVthreeAbstBola{} on true
violations, \CvgVthreeAbstNormal{} on records with none), and its precision of $1.00$ and
selective risk of \CvgVthreeRiskPct{}\% are properties of that coverage. The abstentions are
not all of one kind (panel b): \CvgVthreeUnread{} are structural, and the other
\CvgVthreeCover{} fall on a \emph{readable} surface that shows a covering grant---a
non-decision, not a correct rejection. Of the \CvgVtwoFP{} false proofs v2 promotes, v3
declines \CvgVtwoFPUnread{} because the surface is unreadable and \CvgVtwoFPCover{} because it
shows the grant; only the first are the availability gate the method is named for. With the
\CvgVthreeMissed{} records on which no alarm is raised at all, v3 leaves
\CvgVthreeFNunproven{} of \CvgViolations{} violations unproven; the row that forces a verdict
on an unreadable probe covers every record, detects the same violations and misses
\CvgForcedObsFN{}, at a selective risk of \CvgForcedObsRiskPct{}\% made entirely of that
forcing.

\texttt{direct\_only} asserts a violation whenever the authorization surface reports no
granting channel; it decides \CvgDirOnlyDecided{} records and is right on
\CvgDirOnlyTP{} true violations. Its \CvgDirOnlyFP{} invalid claims are not strict false
proofs, since every one falls on a request the server denied
(\CvgDirOnlyFpNoEffect{} without violation effect, \CvgDirOnlyFpEffect{} on an allowed
request), and its selective risk is \CvgDirOnlyRiskPct\%, not zero. \texttt{direct\_observe}
decides \CvgObsDecided{} records---coverage \CvgObsCoveragePct{}\%---abstaining on
\CvgObsAbstain{} (\CvgObsAbstBola{} on true violations), detects \CvgObsDetected{} and raises
no false proof; on the \CvgJointVthreeDirect{} records both decide, it detects
\CvgDetectGap{} more violations than v3, and the remaining difference in pooled recall is
coverage, not accuracy. Selective risk is not a ranking: \texttt{degrade\_all} alarms on
nothing, has a selective risk of \CvgDegradeRiskPct{}\% at \CvgDegradeCoveragePct{}\% coverage,
and detects no violation. We therefore report precision and selective risk with coverage
attached and never compare them at unequal coverage.

\subsection{A published metric's resolution: what a pooled specificity cannot register}
\label{sec:resolution}

The published figure is a specificity, not a false-alarm share of all inputs.
\mbox{MST-wi} defines it as $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$ over the
follow-up inputs $F$, and states that each reported MT failure was manually
verified~\cite{Chaleshtari2022Metamorphic}; the labels are therefore not the
crawler's own answer. With $\mathrm{FN}=0$ throughout that evaluation, the figure
equals $1-\mathrm{FP}/(F-\mathrm{TP})$: the denominator is the \emph{negative}
follow-up inputs, not every generated input.

What such a figure cannot do is register a failure confined to a class, for two
reasons that are arithmetic and coverage rather than labelling. First, the figure is
pooled: a class contributing a share $w$ of the negative inputs can move it by at
most $w$, so a real class-uniform failure is diluted by unaffected inputs. Second,
the follow-up inputs are those the crawl could enumerate; a class of legitimate
access the crawl never reaches contributes no negative inputs and cannot enter the
ratio at all. The second point is the measurement face of Finding~1, and it is a
statement about the coverage of the input set, not about the correctness of the
human labels. We cannot reproduce the engine's exact input count, so we substitute no
value for the published figure.

\paragraph{A bounded formal remark on the denominator}
Write $S=1-\mathit{FP}/N^{-}$, where $N^{-}=\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP}$ is the number of
negative follow-up inputs the run generated, and define $S_{\mathrm{scenario}} = 1 -
N_{\mathrm{failed}} / N_{\mathrm{scenarios}}$, where $N_{\mathrm{failed}}$ is the
number of scenarios in which the failure is uniform. $S_{\mathrm{scenario}}$ is an
analytical construct for characterising resolution, not a proposed replacement
metric; the remark concerns resolution, not estimator consistency or power.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:resolution}
Fix $f \ge 1$ failed scenarios, each producing a fixed number $a \ge 1$ of
false-alarm inputs, and $s \ge 1$ successful scenarios each contributing $m$
additional alarm-free negative inputs. Then
$S = 1 - (f a) / (f a + s m)$, which tends to $1$ as $m \to \infty$, while
$S_{\mathrm{scenario}} = 1 - f/(f+s)$ is independent of $m$ and strictly less
than $1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$N^{-} = f a + s m$ and $\mathit{FP} = f a$; substitution gives the first
expression, which increases monotonically in $m$ to $1$, whereas the
scenario-level figure depends only on the fixed counts $f$ and $s$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:resolution}
No fixed input-level specificity threshold $t < 1$ can guarantee that a
scenario-uniform failure will push the metric below it: choosing
$m > (f a/s)\cdot t/(1-t)$ drives $S$ above $t$ while the $f$ failing
scenarios remain failing.
\end{corollary}

The finding is therefore about what the published figure can be asked to show: it is
pooled over classes, and computed on the inputs the crawl reached. Neither point
disputes the reported value or its labels.
