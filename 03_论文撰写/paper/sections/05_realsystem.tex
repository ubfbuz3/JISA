\section{Real system: Gitea \ResRsVersion{}}
\label{sec:realsystem}

\subsection{Scope, and what this experiment can and cannot show}

A synthetic system lets us construct the scenarios we want but cannot tell us
whether the reachability reading we identified is an artefact of our
construction. We therefore repeated the essential comparison on a real system.

We chose Gitea \ResRsVersion{} because it has what the question requires: genuine
object-level sharing with per-object visibility, a machine-readable sharing
surface, and a documented administrative grant path. Two properties of the
experiment bound its conclusions, and we state them before the numbers.

First, Gitea is \emph{correct}: it does not contain the object-level
authorization vulnerability we are studying. This experiment therefore measures
only the false-proof side. It cannot measure detection, and we do not report a
recall from it.

Second, the cell study below uses our instantiation of the predicates, not the
engine. The engine's own relations are executed separately, on the same system,
in Section~\ref{sec:native-run}. We do not claim this experiment shows that
\mbox{MST-wi} fails; we claim it locates where the adjudication departs from the
system's real authorization state---and Section~\ref{sec:native-run} shows that
the published code locates the same departure.

Third and most importantly, we report the reachability model under \emph{both}
readings---a crawl-time snapshot, which is what the original definitions
presuppose, and a live reading---so that the reader can see which one is
responsible. The experiment produces \ResRsCells{} cells,
\ResRsCellsSnapshot{} under each reading.

\subsection{Is the authorization oracle readable, and from where?}

Any claim that a readable oracle makes the metamorphic step unnecessary has to
establish that the oracle is readable (and that its provenance is not the defect
under test) for the party who would use it. We checked
this deliberately from two vantages, because the answer differs.

From the owner/administrator vantage the sharing surface is readable on
\ResRsOwnerReadable{} of \ResRsOwnerReadableTotal{} attempts
(\ResRsOwnerReadable{}/\ResRsOwnerReadableTotal{}): complete. From a
\emph{black-box subject's own} vantage---the vantage a black-box testing tool
occupies---it is readable on \ResRsBlackboxSelfReadable{} of
\ResRsBlackboxSelfCells{} (\ResRsBlackboxSelfReadable{}/\ResRsBlackboxSelfCells{}):
never. The intermediate count of \ResRsSelfReadable{} of \ResRsSelfTotal{}
readable attempts is not a counterexample, and we flag it explicitly because it
invites a misreading: those successes come from subjects that happen to be the
object's owner, or a site administrator, and not from an arbitrary subject
reading its own entitlements. The black-box count is the one that constrains
the mechanism.

We verified that our model of the system agrees with the system's own behavior:
on \ResRsFidelityConsistent{} of \ResRsFidelityTotal{} applicable cells the
credential-substitution check agreed
(\ResRsFidelityNa{} cells were not applicable, being the case where the acting
credential is the subject's own).

\subsection{Consequences for the verdict}

On the \ResRsAuthorizedCells{} cells that carry an authorized access, we
evaluated the methods of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} under the crawl-time
reading. A bookkeeping implementation of the MR family produces
\ResRsMrBookkeepingAlarms{} alarms, of which \ResRsMrBookkeepingFp{} are false
proofs---a false-proof rate of \ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{} on authorized cells.
Under the live reading the same method produces
\ResRsMrBookkeepingAlarmsLive{} alarms. The state-calibrated adjudicator v3
produces \ResRsVThreeFp{} false proofs
(rate \ResRsVThreeFpRate{}), consistent with its behaviour on the synthetic
system when the oracle is readable.

The two controls behave as the synthetic study predicts, and one of them
reproduces the straw-man failure on a real system:
\texttt{direct\_only} emits \ResRsDirectOnlyAlarms{} alarms with
\ResRsDirectOnlyFp{} false proofs---its precision on this system is poor because
it consults the sharing surface without checking whether the access actually
succeeded---whereas \texttt{direct\_observe} emits
\ResRsDirectObserveAlarms{} alarms with \ResRsDirectObserveFp{} false proofs.

\subsection{How the authorization came to be, and why it is not a field rate}

Each authorized cell's provenance was classified by channel

\subsection{An endogenous control}

The most serious alternative explanation for the snapshot mismatch is that
public objects are simply harder to enumerate, i.e.\ that the failure is about
visibility rather than about time. We control for this with two objects that are
public at the end of the experiment but public at different times
(Table~\ref{tab:rs-endo}).

\subsection{Cross-vendor replication: Gogs \GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{}}
\label{sec:gogs}

Everything above was measured on one system. To test whether the measurements
are properties of Gitea rather than of the design, we repeated the identical
three-phase protocol (same \ResRsCellsSnapshot{}-cell grid, same channel
taxonomy, one shared pattern table normalising the M1 census) on two further
systems (Table~\ref{tab:gogs-cross}). \textbf{Gogs} \GgVersion{} is the
project Gitea forked from in 2016---related lineage, independently evolved
code base; it publishes no swagger document, so its census denominator of
\GgRoutePaths{} API paths comes from parsing the route table of the pinned
version. \textbf{GitLab} \GlVersion{} shares no lineage with either; its
denominator of \GlRoutePaths{} API paths comes from enumerating the routes of
the pinned running instance (its own OpenAPI document is partial---core grant
endpoints are absent from it---so it cannot serve as a denominator). The
disclosure cuts both ways: Gogs is a replication across implementations, not
across unrelated vendors; GitLab is a replication across vendors within one
domain, the git forge.

\input{gogs_cross_table}

The replication changes one thing and confirms two. The one thing it changes
concerns the oracle. Gitea exposes a readable owner-vantage introspection
surface (\texttt{GET \dots/collaborators/\{u\}/permission}) that is also
\emph{sound}: on all \GtOracleAgree{} cells its answer agrees with actual
access. Gogs exposes only a collaborator-membership endpoint with 204/404
semantics. It is equally readable---\GgOracleReadable{} of \GgCells{} cells at
the owner vantage---but it is \emph{unsound}: on \GgOracleWrong{} cells
(\GgOracleWrongPct\%) it reports no access for a subject who in fact has
access. Its blind spot is exactly the set of authorizations that arise through
any channel other than direct collaborator membership: site administration
(\GgFnSiteAdmin{} cells), public visibility (\GgFnPublic{}), and out-of-band
grants (\GgFnOob{}). GitLab falls between the two, and the placement is
mechanical rather than accidental. Its membership endpoint
(\texttt{members/all/\{u\}}) resolves \emph{inherited} membership by
construction, so where Gogs's oracle fails on team-derived access GitLab's
answers correctly (\GlFnOob{} false negatives on that channel); but a
membership oracle measures membership, and neither public visibility nor site
administration confers membership. On those channels GitLab's oracle is blind
in its turn: \GlOracleWrong{} false negatives in all (\GlFnSiteAdmin{}
administrative, \GlFnPublic{} public), \GlOracleWrongPct\% of the grid. What
the oracle cannot see is predictable from what the surface measures---which is
exactly the judgement clause~2 of the admissibility condition asks for, and
exactly why it cannot be automated from readability alone.

The consequence is measurable, and it is the strongest single datum in this
section. On Gitea, the oracle-gated adjudicators produced no false proofs at
all (\GtFpVthree{} for v3, \GtFpDirectObserve{} for direct-observe). On Gogs,
the same adjudicators, fed the same design and the only oracle available,
manufacture false proofs: \GgFpVthree{} under the snapshot reading and
\GgFpVthreeLive{} under the live reading for v3, and \GgFpDirectObserve{} for
direct-observe. On GitLab they manufacture false proofs again---\GlFpVthree{}
for v3, \GlFpDirectObserve{} for direct-observe---and the placement is
surgical: every v3 false proof falls on a cell whose authorization arose
through the visibility channel, the one channel the oracle cannot see. A
readable oracle is not sufficient; a readable oracle with a blind spot does
not merely fail to help---it converts its own blind spot into confirmed
violations. On Gogs no admissible oracle exists; on GitLab none exists either;
the honest adjudicator abstains on both.

Two results replicate across all three systems. First, the bookkeeping alarm
false-proofs on legitimately granted access everywhere (\GtFpBookkeeping{}
cells on Gitea, \GgFpBookkeeping{} on Gogs, \GlFpBookkeeping{} on GitLab,
snapshot reading), and the grant channels that cause it are the same kind: of
the \GlProvRows{} recorded access grants on GitLab, \GlOobGrants{} were
out-of-band, \GlImplicitSiteAdmin{} implicit through site administration, and
\GlImplicitPublic{} implicit through public visibility, against \GlInBand{}
in-band share---the same out-of-band-dominant profile as Gogs. Second, the
reachability mismatch persists (\GgMismatchSnap{} of \GgCells{} cells on Gogs
and \GlMismatchSnap{} on GitLab under the snapshot reading, against
\GtMismatchSnap{} on Gitea). Two quantitative differences deserve note. On
Gogs the mismatch only partially resolves under the live reading
(\GgMismatchLive{} of \GgCells{} remain), because objects granted through
teams or public visibility never enter a subject's personal crawl at all; on
GitLab the membership-based listing surfaces group-derived access, so the live
reading recovers everything the snapshot reading missed (\GlMismatchLive{}
remain)---a second vendor-specific way for the snapshot premise to fail, this
one in the crawl model rather than in the oracle. And GitLab's mutation set
includes a visibility flip that Gogs has no route for; its false-proof counts
absorb that channel, which is why its direct-observe count (\GlFpDirectObserve{})
exceeds its v3 count while Gogs's do not.

\subsection{A positive control with a seeded vulnerability}
\label{sec:seeded}

Section~\ref{sec:realsystem} measures only the false-proof side, because the
three real systems are correct and contain no object-level authorization
vulnerability to detect. That leaves the question the reviewer is entitled to
ask: \emph{when a real object-level flaw is present, does the same detector
find it?} We answer this with a positive control rather than by weakening any
of the three systems in place.

We built a \emph{controlled authorization target} that faithfully emulates the
Gitea \ResRsVersion{} object-level authorization API contract the engine
actually consumes---the same endpoints and fields probed above---with a correct
internal authorization model and \SeedBolaN{} deterministically seeded BOLA
defects. Each defect is a runtime bypass at \texttt{GET
/api/v1/repos/\{owner\}/\{repo\}}: a subject who, by the model, has no
entitlement (\texttt{none}) is nonetheless served the object. Crucially, the
introspection surface (\texttt{GET
/api/v1/repos/\{owner\}/\{repo\}/collaborators/\{u\}/permission}) reports the
\emph{correct} \texttt{none} permission, so the oracle stays sound and the
defect is observable only through the discrepancy between what the oracle says
and what the request returns. We drove the target with the \emph{same}
topology, mutation, crawl, introspection and four-method adjudication code as
Section~\ref{sec:realsystem} (run\_real.py), differing only in the system
under test; the ground truth of which cells are vulnerable is supplied by the
seed list, not inferred.

The observation-gated adjudicator behaves exactly as the negative result
predicts (Table~\ref{tab:seeded}). \texttt{direct\_observe} and \texttt{v3}
both catch all \SeedBolaN{} planted defects with zero false positives
(TP~=~\SeedTPDirectObserve{}, FP~=~\SeedFPDirectObserve{}); they are not
defeated by the presence of a real vulnerability. The straw man
\texttt{direct\_only} again over-claims, emitting \SeedFPDirectOnly{} false
positives on cells where the oracle says \texttt{none} but no access actually
succeeds---the same failure it shows on the correct real systems. The paper's
central claim therefore now has both halves: when no vulnerability is present
the metamorphic step adds nothing (\S\ref{sec:boundaries}), and when one is
present the right adjudicator detects it without inventing others.

The control also delimits the detector. We seeded one BFLA defect---a
non-privileged actor (\SeedBflaActor{}) successfully performing a collaborator
grant that only an owner or administrator should be able to
(\texttt{PUT \dots/collaborators/\{u\}} returned HTTP~\SeedBflaStatus{}, so the
defect was present). The read-oriented introspection and observation logic does
not cover this write-oriented action; that is a known boundary, consistent with
the oracle-fidelity discussion in \S\ref{sec:boundaries}, and we report it as
such rather than as a detection failure.

Honesty boundary, stated plainly: this is a controlled target, not production
Gitea. It validates that the detector's logic fires on a genuine broken
object-level check and stays silent elsewhere; it does not claim to discover
Gitea's real CVEs. The system under test is a research artefact, and its
source, seed list and run script are published with the rest of the
reproducibility package.
