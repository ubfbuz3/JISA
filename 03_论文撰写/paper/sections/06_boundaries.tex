\section{Findings, admissibility, and falsification}
\label{sec:findings}

Each finding is a boundary on what the published relations can establish about
object-level authorization---\emph{temporal}, \emph{oracle} and
\emph{measurement}---carried by the studies of
Sections~\ref{sec:synthetic}--\ref{sec:realsystem}. The confirmations they rest
on (the predicates read at source, the published relations executed on a real
system, an independent implementation of the same predicates) introduce no
further claim and are collected as support evidence in Section~\ref{sec:support}.

\subsection{Finding 1 (RQ1): deterministic false proofs---the temporal boundary}
\label{sec:boundary1}

\emph{Taken as published---raw alarms, or alarms filtered by bookkeeping
exceptions---the adjudication reports a legitimate access whose grant the crawl
record does not contain as a finding of an established violation under the
promotion policy $P$ of
Section~\ref{sec:native-run}, which we state explicitly for this evaluation
rather than inherit, since the
engine's own return value asserts nothing about authorization---in the direction
forced by the two-valued predicate encoding.} The two studies supply the grant
in different ways: the constructed study seeds it outside the framework's
record, and the temporal study makes it between the crawl and the request. On the constructed system, rate
\ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} (\ResObsOobVtwoFalseProof{} of \ResObsOobFalseAlarm{}
alarms) against \ResObsFwVtwoFalseProof{} for the same share created in band; on
Gitea, \ResRsMrBookkeepingFp{} false proofs on \ResRsAuthorizedCells{} authorized
cells (\ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{}) under the crawl-time reading, against
\ResRsMrBookkeepingAlarmsLive{} alarms live---objects, entitlements and predicates
held fixed, only the reading time changed---so the error is a property of the
\emph{reading}, not of the system; the endogenous control
(Table~\ref{tab:rs-endo}) holds final visibility fixed and isolates that moment.
The direction is forced: the predicate has exactly two representations, and a
grant created outside the crawled interface has no representation but ``cannot
reach''. The predecessor attributes its residual false alarms to crawler
coverage~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}; we add that the failure is a function of
\emph{when} the model was read, which makes the natural oracle-based repair
available while showing no incremental adjudication benefit from the metamorphic
step (Finding~2).

\subsection{Finding 2 (RQ2): the metamorphic step adds no observed adjudication benefit}
\label{sec:boundary2}

\emph{If the false proofs of Finding~1 are removed by consulting a readable
introspection surface, it is the oracle that carries the work: for the
authorization relations and scenarios evaluated here, once the oracle fully
resolves the authorization proposition under test, replacing the entire
metamorphic step with a single ordinary request---one that consumes the same
readable oracle and access outcome---provides no observed incremental
adjudication benefit.} We state the
reason rather than present it as a discovered effect: its burden is an empirical
premise about the evaluated relations, not an algebraic identity.

We separate the two parts, because only one of them can fail. The algebraic part
is a lemma about function composition; the empirical part is a statement about
what these two relations actually read, and it has a falsifier.

\emph{The direct adjudicator.} Fix, before any run, the rule $D$ that issues
\texttt{violation-proven} for $(o,s,a)$ exactly when the oracle $O$ answers
\emph{deny} and the ordinary request $R$ succeeds. $D$ consumes $O$ and $R$ and
nothing else. It is a definition, not a result, and it is the comparator in the
ablation of Section~\ref{sec:synthetic-ablation} (\texttt{direct\_observe}).

\begin{lemma}[absorption]
\label{lem:absorption}
Let a detector issue its verdict through a map $g(A,O,R)$, where $A$ is its alarm
and $O,R$ as above. If, on the domain of cells considered, (i)~$A$ is a function
$a(M,R)$ of the crawl-time model and the response only, and (ii)~$M$ is
$O$-determined for the authorization proposition under test---that is, $M$ carries
no distinction the oracle does not already make---then there is a map $g'$ with
$g(a(M,R),O,R)=g'(O,R)$ on that domain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (i) the first argument of $g$ is a function of $(M,R)$; by (ii) $M$ is a
function of $O$ on the domain; substituting gives $A=a'(O,R)$, so $g$ factors
through $(O,R)$ as $g'(O,R):=g(a'(O,R),O,R)$.
\end{proof}

The lemma is deliberately weak: it says that \emph{if} a detector reads nothing
beyond the model and the response, its verdict cannot carry information the pair
$(O,R)$ lacks---but only when both premises (i) and (ii) hold on the domain. It
does not say that any particular implementation satisfies (i)
or (ii), and it does not compare two programs---it compares two functions on a
domain.

\begin{proposition}[what the relation can add once the oracle is admitted]
\label{lem:oracle-suff}
For the two relations examined here, premise~(i) of
Lemma~\ref{lem:absorption} holds by source inspection: their predicates read the
crawl-time reachability model and the response, and nothing else
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}). Premise~(ii), by contrast, is an empirical condition
on the recorded domain---$M$ would have to be a function of $O$ there---and we
do not assume it; the conclusions below are stated so as not to need it.
Write $y$ for the response bit, $m$ for the model bit
and $o$ for the oracle bit; the shipped alarm is $A=y\wedge\neg m$, the direct
adjudicator is $D=y\wedge\neg o$, and the promoted verdict of the
state-calibrated method is $H=A\wedge\neg o$, which abstains where $o$ is
undefined. On the domain where $o$ is defined:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $A=D$ if and only if $y=0$ or $m=o$; the two agree on every record
whose request fails, and part exactly on the rows with $y=1$ and $m\ne o$;
\item[(b)] $H\Rightarrow D$, because $A\Rightarrow y$ and the two share
$\neg o$. The metamorphic step therefore cannot confirm a record that the
direct consumer of $(y,o)$ does not confirm; it can only abstain or repeat.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Part~(b) is algebra and needs no premise about the model. Part~(a) is algebra
too, but it is informative only where $m=o$, and that equality is a measured
condition rather than a corollary of oracle soundness: the oracle being sound
does not make the crawl-time model sound. Row~7 of Table~\ref{tab:truth} holds
\TvtModelWrong{} cells with
$y=1$, $m=0$ and $o=1$---the oracle answers correctly, the model is stale, and
the relation raises a false proof that $D$ does not. The tempting step from
``the oracle is readable and correct'' to ``$M$ and $O$ agree'' is therefore
invalid, and we do not take it; premise~(ii) of the lemma does not hold on the
recorded domain: rows~7 and~8 share $O=\mathrm{allow}$ but carry different
values of $M$, so $O$ does not determine $M$ there---which is why we do not
present the relation as informationally redundant
in general, only as non-additive in confirmation once a definite oracle exists.
What the proposition establishes is narrower
and sufficient for the argument: once a definite oracle is available, promotion
through the relation is a \emph{subset} of direct adjudication, so the relation
cannot add confirmations. Whether $m=o$ holds is a measured property of each
system, reported in Table~\ref{tab:truth} and in
Section~\ref{sec:stratum}, not a corollary of oracle correctness.

The predicates, explicitly. The two shipped predicates read
\begin{align}
A_{002} &= \neg\mathrm{sup}(B,A)\;\wedge\;\neg m \nonumber\\
        &\quad\wedge\;\neg\mathrm{err}(o_1)\;\wedge\;q(o_1,o_2),\label{eq:a002}\\
A_{004} &= y\;\wedge\;\neg m,\label{eq:a004}
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{err}$ is the error predicate and $q$ normalised response
identity. On the domain evaluated here $\neg\mathrm{sup}=1$,
$\mathrm{err}(o_1)=0$, and $q=y$ in all \TvtMrTwo{} records on which the second
clause was computed, so both collapse to the single map
\begin{equation}
A \;=\; y \;\wedge\; \neg m .
\label{eq:aflat}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:a002}--\eqref{eq:aflat} are not a paraphrase of the code;
they reproduce the recorded alarm of all \TvtRecords{} constructed records and
all \TvtCells{} temporal cells with no disagreement
(\texttt{analysis/ingest\_truth.py}), and the same script checks
part~(a) cell by cell. Two evidence layers must be kept apart here. The
temporal-cell check evaluates the domain-internal simplification of
Equation~\eqref{eq:aflat} against the alarm each cell already recorded; it is a
self-consistency check of the map, not a second, independent execution of the
full MR-002, whose predicate also consults the supervisor relation and the
response pair. The evidence that the full-form predicate fires as written is
the constructed study's \TvtMrTwo{} predicate-level records; the native run of
Section~\ref{sec:native-run} carries the unmodified library's own evaluation,
and neither layer is substituted for the other. The three records on which the state-calibrated method
and \texttt{direct\_observe} still differ are not a counterexample to~(b): all
three are the same S5 write cell under the three exception settings, where the
model lists the object for reading, the alarm stays silent, and the oracle
reports that the write is denied. There the relation misses a violation the
direct adjudicator confirms---a loss, not a gain, and one the reachability
gate cannot express because it collapses read and write reachability.

\paragraph{Reading the table}
Table~\ref{tab:truth} evaluates both verdicts on every combination of the four
variables and reports how many of the \TvtCells{} temporal cells fall in each
row, split by the ledger's entitlement. Three things are visible in it that
prose cannot state as compactly. First, the equivalence is exactly as narrow as
the algebra says: the native alarm $A$ and the direct adjudicator $D$---the two
measured surfaces, not the promoted verdict $H$, which the table lists
separately---agree in \TvtAgree{} of the \TvtReadable{}
cells with a usable oracle and part only in the two rows where $Y=1$ and
$M\ne O$, which hold \TvtDisagree{} cells. Second, the parting is not one
phenomenon but two, and they need different remedies. In the \TvtOracleWrong{}
cells of row~6 the model is right and the membership surface answers \emph{deny}
on a cell that is both entitled and readable, so the direct adjudicator alone
raises a false proof; a better oracle would remove those. In the
\TvtModelWrong{} cells of row~7 the model is stale and the oracle right, so the
relation alone raises a false proof; a recrawl would remove those. Third, row~5
shows why agreement is not a certificate of correctness: in \TvtBothWrong{}
cells both surfaces answer \emph{deny}, the ledger says the subject is entitled,
the request succeeds, and both verdicts are false proofs. The proposition is
about information, so it is silent on row~5---but a reader who took $A=D$ as
evidence that the pair decides correctly would be wrong there, and the table
says so before the reader has to notice it.

Two further readings of the same table belong here, because both bear on how far
the result travels. The rows without an oracle are not idle: \TvtUnreadable{}
cells return no definite answer, $D$ is undefined on all of them, and the
relation still alarms in \TvtUnreadAlarms{}---alarms that nothing in this
experiment can adjudicate, which is the regime the proposition's antecedent
excludes rather than a gap in it. And the model term is not free. Recomputed over
the two model semantics of the constructed study, the constant variant, which
alarms on every successful access, finds \TvtBlindTP{} violations at
\TvtBlindFP{} false proofs; the current-listing variant finds \TvtConTP{} at
\TvtConFP{}---\TvtModelSaveFP{} fewer false proofs for \TvtModelCostTP{} fewer
detections, over \TvtConAware{} records each. A relation with a reachability
gate is therefore neither the alarm-free ideal of Finding~2's antecedent nor a
free improvement on the ungated variant; it trades one error for the other, and
the trade is the reason the gate is worth stating as a variable rather than
folding into the oracle.

\paragraph{Three differences from the ideal predicate}
Equations~\eqref{eq:a002}--\eqref{eq:aflat} describe the predicates as written,
and the shipped code departs from them in three places. None changes the
algebra; each either moves cells out of the domain or corrupts a variable the
two verdicts share, which is why none of them makes the relation beat $D$.
(i)~\emph{The model term is a store, not a query.}
\texttt{cannotReachThroughGUI} and \texttt{userCanRetrieveContent} are set
membership over the crawl inputs: they issue no request and read a store loaded
once. So $m$ is fixed at crawl time while $y$ and $o$ are read at decision time,
and rows~6--7 are precisely the cells on which that interval carries a change.
(ii)~\emph{The error predicate is a status code.} MST-wi asks whether an error
page is rendered; the port asks whether the status is at least 400. A denial
served with 200, or an error page served with 403 on the subject's own object,
makes $y$ wrong---and $y$ enters both $A$ and $D$, so the two stay tied while
both are wrong. This is the sense in which the tie is evidence about information
and not about accuracy. (iii)~\emph{Response identity is not authorization
identity.} The clause $q$ compares the whole normalised response, not the
authorization proposition under test; a per-viewer field would make $q$ false
while access had in fact been granted, and the relation would stay silent on a
violation. On the evaluated domain this does not bite ($q=y$ throughout), but it
is the one of the three that produces a miss rather than a false proof, and it
would grow if the relation were extended to responses carrying viewer-specific
fields.

The proposition is falsifiable in one specific way: exhibit an observable $Z$,
\emph{other than the model and the response}, that a relation consumes and that
is decision-relevant. Then premise (i) fails, the lemma does not apply, and the
relation could in principle beat $D$. The model itself is not such a $Z$: a
stale model also fails to be $O$-determined, but that breaks premise (ii), and
the direction of the break is visible in Table~\ref{tab:truth}---row~7 is a
false proof, not an advantage. We know of no such observable for these two
relations, and the ablation is consistent with its absence; that is the whole of
the claim. We do not assert that no such observable could exist for other
relations in the catalogue, and we do not assert that the relations are useless.

The proposition concerns \emph{adjudication} information only. It does not imply
that a metamorphic relation cannot improve input generation, search-space
reduction, or execution efficiency: a relation may guide which requests are
issued, or which gated subset is exercised, while carrying no verdict information
beyond $(O,R)$. It is a requirement on \emph{claims}, not a verdict on
metamorphic testing: it says only that a verdict computable from $(O,R)$ cannot be
shown to beat the direct consumer of $(O,R)$.

\paragraph{Scope, quantified}
The comparison above is pooled. Section~\ref{sec:stratum} re-runs it inside each
stratum of effective oracle readability (Table~\ref{tab:stratum}) and thereby
bounds the claim rather than
widening it. Where the oracle is readable (\MiiNOn{} records) the conclusion is
stronger than ``no observed benefit'': the plain request carries fewer false proofs
(\MiiOnDirectObserveFP{} vs.~\MiiOnVTwoFP{}) and no fewer detections. Where it is
not (\MiiNOff{} records) the antecedent fails---there is no oracle to consult, and
every oracle-bearing method detects nothing by construction---so the metamorphic
family is compared against oracle-free references instead: it retains an
advantage over a trivial floor and over a single uninterpreted request
(\MiiLiftVTwo{}$\times$ vs.~\MiiLiftObsSuccess{}$\times$ over the base rate), and
that advantage is exactly coextensive with what the crawl-derived model can see.
Finding~2 is a claim about the regime its antecedent describes; it does not say
the relations are worthless outside it.

Evidence: Table~\ref{tab:ablation}---\ResAbDirectObserveFP{} false proofs
vs.~\ResAbVThreeFP{} (a tie at zero, not a win) over the \ResAbNBola{}
true-violation records. The comparison is paired, so we report the discordance
rather than an inferential interval: the two methods disagree on
\ResAbDisagreementN{} records, and on every one \texttt{direct\_observe} is
right---the aggregate recalls \ResAbDirectObserveRec{} and \ResAbVThreeRec{}
restate that asymmetry rather than measure a difference, and no margin was fixed
in advance. Gitea independently reproduces the zero-false-proof result
(\ResRsDirectObserveFp{} vs.~\ResRsVThreeFp{}), but not the same comparison,
because the tested surface contains no seeded vulnerability; the cross-vendor
systems (Section~\ref{sec:gogs}) and the seeded control (Section~\ref{sec:seeded})
complete the evidence. One qualification is part of the claim: the control is
\emph{constructed}, not discovered---\texttt{direct\_observe} instantiates the
direct-consumer construction $D$ of Proposition~\ref{lem:oracle-suff}, so the tie
is the empirical face of the necessary condition, a statement about the
definitions rather than a coincidental measurement.

\subsection{Finding 3 (RQ3): a pooled specificity cannot register a class it did not exercise}

\emph{The reported \ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\% is a specificity over follow-up
inputs, $\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$, not a false-alarm share of all
generated inputs; in that evaluation the MT failures were manually verified, so
the labels are not the crawler's own. Its denominator cannot, on its own,
provide scenario-level resolution for a failure confined to a class, and the
inputs it counts are the ones the crawl could enumerate.} Both halves are
consistent with the reported value. The denominator: with
$\mathrm{FN}=0$, the figure is $1-\mathrm{FP}/(F-\mathrm{TP})$, so a failure
uniform across a class contributes only as many false positives as that class
emits negative inputs and is diluted by the rest of the run
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}; Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution}: no fixed
threshold can guarantee that such a class forces the metric below it). The
coverage: if a class of legitimate access is never reached by the crawl, it
contributes no negative inputs, so the ratio is silent about it even though the
adjudication that consumes the crawl is not. Nor does the report fix the
weights: if a failing class supplies a share $w$ of the negative inputs, the
figure moves only between $1$ and $1-w$, so telling a small-share failure apart
from no failure requires an estimate of $w$ that the published number does not
carry.

We are not saying the figure is false, that its labels are wrong, or that the
false positives were misdiagnosed: the manual verification is exactly what makes
the labels credible. The claim is narrower---that a pooled figure computed on
the crawled input set does not by itself certify that a class outside that set
was exercised, and that a class-uniform failure can be held above any fixed
threshold by unaffected inputs.

\paragraph{What an endogenous count certifies, conditionally}
Fix a run $\omega$ and a method $D$; let $n_i(D,\omega)$ be the number of
negative inputs $D$ generates for scenario $i$ in that run and $f_i(D,\omega)$
the false proofs among them. The endogenous score is
$S(D,\omega)=1-\sum_i f_i(D,\omega)\big/\sum_i n_i(D,\omega)$ when the
denominator is positive. Three conditional statements, and no more. (i)~For
each fixed $\omega$, the identity holds by counting: it is a definition
evaluated on that run's records, so it needs no distributional assumption. (ii)
Correlation among records (a design cell shares its fixture) affects what
confidence one may attach to $S$ if it were re-estimated on a fresh run; it does
not move the count itself, which is why we report counts and rates and no
interval. (iii)~For a family of runs $\omega_k$ in which the negative-input
total stays positive, $\;S_k\to 1\;$ if and only if
$\sum_i f_i(D,\omega_k)\big/\sum_i n_i(D,\omega_k)\to 0$; a finite-sample
$S<1$ by itself says nothing about the limit. Two arithmetic examples fix the
 distinction. If the false-proof count is held fixed at one while the
negative-input total grows with $k$, then $S_k=1-1/k\to 1$: the failure never
disappears, only its weight in the ratio does. If instead the false proofs grow
proportionally---$f=\alpha k$ with $0<\alpha<1$ and $k$ chosen so the counts
are integral---then $S_k=1-\alpha$ for every $k$: input growth produces no
dilution toward~1 at all. Both mechanisms coexist with an unexercised failing
class, which contributes no negative inputs and leaves every $S_k$ unchanged
(the same blind spot as Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}). These are
statements about one run's arithmetic, conditional on its scenario coverage;
they are not a theorem that any run detects every failure, and we do not use
them as one.

\subsection{Competing baselines: what a succeeding request cannot separate}
\label{sec:baselines}

The comparison that matters is not against our own ablations but against what a
conventional differential prober can do with the same two observations. We
implemented three such rules and ran them on the same \BlCells{} cells (six
configurations crossed with S1--S7), each consuming one request as the owner and
one as the actor. \emph{B1 status-only} alarms whenever the replayed request
returns a non-error status---the coarsest rule a replay scanner can use.
\emph{B2 equality replay} alarms only when the owner's request succeeded and the
actor received byte-identical output, which is the differential rule with no
authorization model. \emph{B3} adds the strongest information a tester can hold
without reading the target: it suppresses every cell covered by the tester's own
share log, so it knows each grant it created itself. The fourth row is MR-002
with the v3 adjudication.

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} reports the outcome. The cells contain
\BlBolaCells{} true violations, \BlLegitCells{} legitimate accesses through a
grant, and \BlDeniedCells{} denials. B1 and B2 claim on all 27 allowed cells:
\BlBOneTP{} true positives and \BlBOneFP{} false proofs, a precision of
\BlBTwoPrecision{}\%. On these cells the equality test adds nothing to the status
test, because every allowed access returns the owner's bytes. B3 removes the six
in-band grants and still issues \BlBThreeFP{} false proofs, all on S6, where the
grant was seeded out of band. Ours issues \BlOursTP{} true positives and no false
proof (precision \BlOursPrecision{}\%), at the cost of \BlOursAbstain{}
abstentions: the twelve legitimate grants plus five violations in the two
configurations whose authorization surface is unreadable.

Two readings of this table would be wrong. It is not a detection gain: the
baselines find all fifteen violations (\BlBaselineRecallAll{}\% total recall),
whereas ours confirms ten---exactly those
whose surface is readable---and declines the other five. The two recall figures
for ours must be read together and are not interchangeable: on the cells where
ours issues a verdict at all, recall is \BlOursRecallDecided{}\%, because an
abstention is neither a hit nor a miss there; over all \BlBolaCells{} violation
cells, including the \BlOursAbstainViol{} abstained ones, total recall is
\BlOursRecallAll{}\%. Nor is it a free
improvement: coverage falls to \BlOursCoverage{}\%. What the table isolates is the
only difference the claim depends on. A succeeding request is compatible with
both a grant and a defect, so a rule that treats it as proof must either ignore
grants (B1, B2) or see only the grants it created (B3). The out-of-band grant is
the residual, and it is not a corner case in the lab: on the Gitea snapshot the
equality rule fires on all \BlRsBTwoClaims{} legitimately allowed cells, and the
in-band log still leaves \BlRsBThreeClaims{}, because the framework log does not
observe \ResRsChannelNotObservableRate{} of the effective access there. That
figure is a property of the constructed topology, not an estimate of field
prevalence. The budget is matched at the level of the probe pair and only there:
\BlProbeReq{} requests per cell for every method, one as the owner and one as the
actor, \BlSharedProbes{} shared probes in all, plus one additional read of the
authorization surface for ours on the \BlOursExtraCells{} cells where a relation
alarms and none where it does not---\BlOursExtraAvg{} per cell on average,
\BlOursExtraTotal{} reads in total, so ours issues \BlOursRequestsTotal{} requests
against the baselines' \BlSharedProbes{}. Total request count therefore differs
across methods in this table, which is why it is read as a comparison of
adjudication trade-offs at different information costs; the matched-total-budget
comparison is reported separately below, and it does not reverse that reading. The
whole comparison, both probes and the additional reads included, ran in
\BlRunSeconds{}~s of wall-clock harness time covering setup, probing, scoring
and cleanup; the runner binds no CPU and measures no per-method CPU time, so we
make no claim about single-core cost. Because all four methods are
scored from the same two recorded responses in the same process, a per-method
timing would measure our replay loop rather than the rule, so we report none.

actually made, paid for in coverage where the surface cannot be read---not a
detection advantage over the differential baselines, and we claim none.

\subsection{Admissibility, and falsification}
\label{sec:admissibility}

Three properties must be kept apart. A surface is \emph{readable} when its answer
can be obtained from the vantage in question; \emph{sound} when its answer equals
the true authorization state; and \emph{admissible}, for promotion to
\texttt{violation-proven}, when (1)~it is readable \emph{and} (2)~one can give a
reason that it is not of the same kind as the defect under test---not itself a
reachability artifact whose blind spot correlates with the blindness tested.
Readability is what makes soundness usable, but it neither implies soundness nor
confers admissibility: the owner-vantage authorization-introspection surface is
readable on every cell in each system (\ResRsCellsSnapshot{} Gitea, \GgCells{}
Gogs, \GlCells{} GitLab), yet sound on Gitea alone---it agrees with effective
access on \GtOracleAgree{}, \GgOracleAgree{} and \GlOracleAgree{} of those
cells respectively. The
cross-vendor systems are therefore not cases of ``no oracle'': a readable
membership surface exists on both, but it is unsound for the authorization
channels exercised here, so it fails clause~(2). Clause~(2) is a per-system
judgement and not automatable from readability alone; the cross-vendor results
make it concrete (Gogs and GitLab fail it; their adjudicators convert the blind
spots into \GgFpVthree{} and \GlFpVthree{} false proofs). Where the condition
holds, the honest method consumes the oracle directly and drops the relation;
where it does not, the honest method abstains---as v3 does on the unreadable
stratum, where the aggregate table would have ranked it best, a ranking that
reflects abstention rather than improved detection.

\paragraph{An oracle-independence admission rule, applied before and after}
Clause~(2) needs an operational procedure, and we give one as part of this
revision. It is fixed as a standalone protocol---defect classes, four evidence
levels, an ordered \emph{admit}/\emph{reject}/\emph{unknown} rule, and what each
status licenses---before the multi-class and equal-budget runs of
Sections~\ref{sec:multiclass} and~\ref{sec:baselines}, and applied both to them
and, conservatively, to the archived experiments. It was not part of the original
submission and we do not backdate it: the pre-registered protocol pinned by
digest recorded the qualitative judgement
(\texttt{oracle\_independence=shared}; the oracle read for diagnosis
only) but did not contain the procedure below. The rule, per target defect class
(here: object-level authorization enforcement
of read and write requests): enumerate, at the level the product's own
documentation and observable behaviour support, the path that produces the
enforcement answer $y$ and the path that produces the oracle answer $o$;
\emph{admit} if neither path contains the other's decision point nor shares a
component whose failure could co-occur with the tested defect; \emph{reject} if
such sharing is found; \emph{unknown} if either path cannot be enumerated at
that level, in which case independence-based promotion is refused. Applied at
that level of evidence: on Gitea~\ResRsVersion{} the permission route the oracle
consults and the content routes are answered by the same server binary applying
the same permission model; on Gogs~\GgVersion{} the per-object route the oracle
consults enumerates direct collaborators only, and the permission suffix that
would expose the remaining channels does not exist (\GgChProbePermSuffix), so
the oracle path cannot even be completed; on GitLab~\GlVersion{} the
\texttt{members/all} route the oracle consults and the content routes are
answered by the same application consulting the same membership tables. On each
product the enumeration stops at the server boundary---below it we did not audit
internal source, so the finer-grained verdict would be \emph{unknown}---and the
conservative reading already available at the API level, one process and one
authorization model on both paths, is recorded as \emph{reject}. On the
controlled target, where we can read the source, the finer verdict is available
and is also \emph{reject}: its content decision calls the same function the
oracle answers from, so the shared decision point is visible at level~L2 rather
than inferred. The protocol and the per-system register ship with the artifact.
Three epistemic statuses must then be kept apart: the diagnostic $O$/$H$ readings in
the experiments (run under the recorded \texttt{shared} status); the
classification of every promoted verdict against the independent ledger $E$ and
the content observation $Y$ (gate correctness, which needs no oracle
independence); and independence-certified proofs of previously unknown defects,
which the rule refuses here. No recorded number changes under the rule; what
changes is the epistemic status of the promotion, which is exactly the status
the paper's claims already use.

Read together, the two findings bound what the technique's adjudication can yield
for the authorization proposition studied. If the reachability stand-in is the
only warrant and it is wrong, a legitimate access becomes a false proof
(Finding~1). If a readable, sound oracle is instead available, the relation adds
no adjudication over the direct consumer of that oracle and the outcome
(Finding~2). And where no readable, admissible oracle exists---the black-box
case---there is nothing for a sound method to consume, so it abstains rather than
proves. Across the statuses the oracle can take, the verdict the evaluated
relations can yield is thus a false proof, a result no better than the plain
request (and, where the oracle is readable, worse), or an abstention. None of
those is a proof of illegal access, because the relation's alarm is not evidence
of it: an alarm says that the relation failed, and an ordinary request that
succeeds says that the object was served. A genuine violation becomes reportable
only when an independent entitlement source says \emph{deny} and a content-level
effect observation says \emph{allow}; Section~\ref{sec:cvepair} reports that gate
producing one true positive on a vulnerable build and none on its fixed
successor. The relation contributes to neither side of that conjunction.

Each finding would fall as
follows: Finding~1, if the predicates were defined over live rather than
recorded reachability; Finding~2, by an authorization MR needing an observable
that a succeeding request plus a readable oracle cannot produce; Finding~3, by
a specificity in scenario units, or with the condition explicit, that still fails
to separate the configurations studied here.
