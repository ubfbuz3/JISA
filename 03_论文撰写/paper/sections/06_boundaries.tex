\section{Findings, admissibility, and falsification}
\label{sec:findings}

Each finding is a boundary on what the published relations can establish about
object-level authorization---\emph{temporal}, \emph{oracle} and
\emph{measurement}---carried by the studies of
Sections~\ref{sec:synthetic}--\ref{sec:realsystem}. The confirmations they rest on are
collected in Section~\ref{sec:support}.

\subsection{Finding 1 (RQ1): deterministic false proofs---the temporal boundary}
\label{sec:boundary1}

\emph{Taken as published---raw alarms, or alarms filtered by bookkeeping exceptions---the
adjudication reports a legitimate access whose grant the crawl record does not contain as
a finding of an established violation under the promotion policy $P$ of
Section~\ref{sec:stages}, in the direction forced by the two-valued predicate encoding.}
The two studies supply the grant differently: the constructed study seeds it outside the
framework's record, the temporal study makes it between the crawl and the request. On the
constructed system the rate is \ResObsOobVtwoFpRate{} (\ResObsOobVtwoFalseProof{} of
\ResObsOobFalseAlarm{} alarms) against \ResObsFwVtwoFalseProof{} for the same share
created in band; on Gitea, \ResRsMrBookkeepingFp{} false proofs on
\ResRsAuthorizedCells{} authorized cells (\ResRsMrBookkeepingFpRate{}) under the
crawl-time reading, against \ResRsMrBookkeepingAlarmsLive{} alarms live, with objects,
entitlements and predicates held fixed. The direction is forced: the predicate has two
representations, and a grant created outside the crawled interface has none but ``cannot
reach''. The predecessor attributes its residual false alarms to crawler
coverage~\cite{Mai2019Metamorphic}; we add that the failure is a function of \emph{when}
the model was read.

A finding about a pair is only as sharp as the half that is chosen.
\label{sec:policy}
Table~\ref{tab:promotion}
applies four promotion policies to the same \PolAlarms{} alarms over the \PolCells{} scored
transition cells. The alarm count is invariant---no policy changes what the relations
return, and none recovers a violation---while the number of claims, and therefore of

surface returns false denies, while on the other it withholds \PolGtPoracleWithheld{}
alarms. P3 promotes only where the ledger records a definite denial and returns zero
false proofs by consulting the reference---a policy that cannot be wrong and cannot be a
detector.

\subsection{Finding 2 (RQ2): nothing beyond an admissible oracle}
\label{sec:boundary2}

\emph{If the false proofs of Finding~1 are removed by consulting a readable
introspection surface, it is the oracle that carries the work: for the authorization
relations and scenarios evaluated here, once the oracle fully resolves the authorization
proposition under test, replacing the entire metamorphic step with a single ordinary
request---one that consumes the same readable oracle and access outcome---adds no
adjudication information.} The reason is partly algebraic: on the constructed grid the
agreement is an identity, because the comparator is built from the same quantities that
define the reference. The empirical burden is in the antecedent---that such an oracle is
readable, sound and independent.

\paragraph{What is construction here, and what is empirical}
\texttt{direct\_observe} claims a violation exactly when the oracle answers \emph{deny} and
the ordinary request succeeds; the reference label calls a cell a violation exactly when
the ledger answers \emph{deny} and the content-level effect answers \emph{allow}. Both
rules are built from the same two quantities, so on the domain where the oracle agrees
with the ledger and the request outcome with the content effect they \emph{cannot}
disagree: the agreement is an identity, a characterisation of two adjudication rules
rather than a measurement of one technique against another. What is empirical is the
domain---whether the oracle is readable, sound \emph{and} admissible on a given
deployment. Proposition~\ref{lem:oracle-suff} is stated conditionally for that reason.

\emph{The direct adjudicator.} Fix, before any run, the rule $D$ that issues
\texttt{violation-proven} for $(o,s,a)$ exactly when the oracle $O$ answers \emph{deny}
and the ordinary request $R$ succeeds. $D$ consumes $O$ and $R$ and nothing else; it is
the comparator of the ablation in Section~\ref{sec:synthetic-ablation}
(\texttt{direct\_observe}).

\begin{lemma}[absorption]
\label{lem:absorption}
Let a detector issue its verdict through a map $g(A,O,R)$, where $A$ is its alarm and
$O,R$ as above. If, on the domain of cells considered, (i)~$A$ is a function $a(M,R)$ of
the crawl-time model and the response only, and (ii)~$M$ is $O$-determined for the
authorization proposition under test---that is, $M$ carries no distinction the oracle does
not already make---then there is a map $g'$ with $g(a(M,R),O,R)=g'(O,R)$ on that domain.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By (i) the first argument of $g$ is a function of $(M,R)$; by (ii) $M$ is a function of
$O$ on the domain; substituting gives $A=a'(O,R)$, so $g$ factors through $(O,R)$ as
$g'(O,R):=g(a'(O,R),O,R)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[what the relation can add once the oracle is admitted]
\label{lem:oracle-suff}
For the two relations examined here, premise~(i) holds by source inspection: their
predicates read the crawl-time reachability model and the response, and nothing else
(Section~\ref{sec:impl}). Premise~(ii) is an empirical condition on the recorded domain,
and we do not assume it. Write $y$ for the response bit, $m$ for the model bit and $o$
for the oracle bit; the shipped alarm is $A=y\wedge\neg m$, the direct adjudicator is
$D=y\wedge\neg o$, and the promoted verdict of the state-calibrated method is
$H=A\wedge\neg o$, which abstains where $o$ is undefined. On the domain where $o$ is
defined:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $A=D$ if and only if $y=0$ or $m=o$; the two agree on every record whose
request fails, and part exactly on the rows with $y=1$ and $m\ne o$;
\item[(b)] $H\Rightarrow D$, because $A\Rightarrow y$ and the two share $\neg o$. The
metamorphic step therefore cannot confirm a record that the direct consumer of $(y,o)$
does not confirm; it can only abstain or repeat.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Part~(b) is algebra and needs no premise about the model. Part~(a) is informative only
where $m=o$, and that equality is measured: a sound oracle does not make the crawl-time
model sound. Row~7 of Table~\ref{tab:truth} holds \TvtModelWrong{} cells with $y=1$,
$m=0$ and $o=1$---the oracle answers correctly, the model is stale, and the relation
raises a false proof that $D$ does not. Premise~(ii) therefore fails on the recorded
domain: rows~7 and~8 share $O=\mathrm{allow}$ but carry different $M$. What the
proposition establishes is narrower and sufficient: once a definite oracle is available,
promotion through the relation is a \emph{subset} of direct adjudication.

The two shipped predicates read
\begin{align}
A_{002} &= \neg\mathrm{sup}(B,A)\;\wedge\;\neg m \nonumber\\
        &\quad\wedge\;\neg\mathrm{err}(o_1)\;\wedge\;q(o_1,o_2),\label{eq:a002}\\
A_{004} &= y\;\wedge\;\neg m,\label{eq:a004}
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{err}$ is the error predicate and $q$ normalised response identity. On the
domain evaluated here $\neg\mathrm{sup}=1$, $\mathrm{err}(o_1)=0$ and $q=y$ in all
\TvtMrTwo{} records on which the second clause was computed, so both collapse to
\begin{equation}
A \;=\; y \;\wedge\; \neg m .
\label{eq:aflat}
\end{equation}
Equations~\eqref{eq:a002}--\eqref{eq:aflat} reproduce the recorded alarm of all
\TvtRecords{} constructed records and all \TvtCells{} temporal cells with no disagreement
(\texttt{analysis/ingest\_truth.py}). The three records on which the state-calibrated
method and \texttt{direct\_observe} differ are the same S5 write cell under the three
exception settings, where the model lists the object for reading, the alarm stays silent,
and the oracle reports the write denied---a loss for the relation, not a gain.

\paragraph{Reading the table}
Table~\ref{tab:truth} evaluates both verdicts on every combination of the four variables,
split by the ledger's entitlement. $A$ and $D$ agree in \TvtAgree{} of the \TvtReadable{}

hold \TvtDisagree{} cells. The parting is two phenomena needing different remedies: in
the \TvtOracleWrong{} cells of row~6 the model is right and the membership surface answers
\emph{deny} on a cell both entitled and readable, so $D$ alone raises a false proof that a
better oracle would remove; in the \TvtModelWrong{} cells of row~7 the model is stale and
the oracle right, so the relation alone raises a false proof that a recrawl would remove.
Row~5 shows why agreement is not a certificate: in \TvtBothWrong{} cells both surfaces
answer \emph{deny}, the ledger says the subject is entitled, the request succeeds, and
both verdicts are false proofs. The rows without an oracle are not idle:
\TvtUnreadable{} cells return no definite answer, $D$ is undefined on all of them, and the
relation still alarms in \TvtUnreadAlarms{}. Nor is the model term free: the constant
variant finds \TvtBlindTP{} violations at \TvtBlindFP{} false proofs, the current-listing
variant \TvtConTP{} at \TvtConFP{}---\TvtModelSaveFP{} fewer false proofs for
\TvtModelCostTP{} fewer detections, over \TvtConAware{} records each.

The shipped code departs from Equations~\eqref{eq:a002}--\eqref{eq:aflat} in three
places, none of which changes the algebra: the model term is a store fixed at crawl time;
the error predicate is a status code, so a denial served with 200 makes $y$ wrong; and
response identity is not authorization identity. The proposition is falsifiable in one
way: exhibit an observable $Z$, \emph{other than the model and the response}, that a
relation consumes and that is decision-relevant. It concerns \emph{adjudication} only: a
relation may still improve input generation or search-space reduction.

\paragraph{Scope, quantified}
Section~\ref{sec:stratum} re-runs the comparison inside each stratum of effective oracle

and no fewer detections. Where it is not (\MiiNOff{} records) the antecedent fails, so the
family is compared against oracle-free references: it retains an advantage over a trivial
floor and over a single uninterpreted request (\MiiLiftVTwo{}$\times$
vs.~\MiiLiftObsSuccess{}$\times$ over the base rate), coextensive with what the

reproduces the zero-false-proof result (\ResRsDirectObserveFp{} vs.~\ResRsVThreeFp{}); the
control is \emph{constructed}, not discovered, so the tie is the empirical face of the
necessary condition.

\subsection{Finding 3 (RQ3): a pooled specificity cannot register a class it did not exercise}
\label{sec:resolution2}

\emph{The reported \ResLitReportedSpecPct{}\% is a specificity over follow-up inputs,
$\mathrm{TN}/(\mathrm{TN}+\mathrm{FP})$, not a false-alarm share of all generated inputs;
in that evaluation the MT failures were manually verified, so the labels are not the
crawler's own. Its denominator cannot, on its own, provide scenario-level resolution for a
failure confined to a class, and the inputs it counts are the ones the crawl could
enumerate.} With $\mathrm{FN}=0$ the figure is $1-\mathrm{FP}/(F-\mathrm{TP})$, so a
failure uniform across a class contributes only as many false positives as that class emits
negative inputs and is diluted by the rest of the run
(Proposition~\ref{prop:resolution}; Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution}). A class of legitimate
access the crawl never reaches contributes no negative inputs, so the ratio is silent
about it. Nor does the report fix the weights: if a failing class supplies a share $w$ of
the negative inputs the figure moves only between $1$ and $1-w$.

Fix a run $\omega$ and a method $D$; let $n_i(D,\omega)$ be the number of negative inputs
$D$ generates for scenario $i$ and $f_i(D,\omega)$ the false proofs among them, so
$S(D,\omega)=1-\sum_i f_i(D,\omega)\big/\sum_i n_i(D,\omega)$ where the denominator is
positive. The identity holds by counting on that run's records. For runs whose
negative-input total stays positive $S_k\to 1$ iff the pooled false-proof share tends to
zero, so a finite-sample $S<1$ says nothing about the limit: holding the false-proof count
at one while the total grows gives $S_k=1-1/k\to1$. Both cases coexist with an unexercised
failing class.

\subsection{Competing baselines: what a succeeding request cannot separate}
\label{sec:baselines}

We ran three conventional differential rules on the same \BlCells{} cells (six
configurations crossed with S1--S7), each consuming one request as the owner and one as the
actor (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}). \emph{B1 status-only} alarms whenever the replayed
request returns a non-error status. \emph{B2 equality replay} alarms only when the owner's
request succeeded and the actor received byte-identical output. \emph{B3} suppresses every
cell covered by the tester's own share log. The fourth row is MR-002 with the v3
adjudication.

The cells contain \BlBolaCells{} true violations, \BlLegitCells{} legitimate accesses
through a grant, and \BlDeniedCells{} denials. B1 and B2 claim on all 27 allowed cells:
\BlBOneTP{} true positives and \BlBOneFP{} false proofs, a precision of
\BlBTwoPrecision{}\%; the equality test adds nothing to the status test, because every
allowed access returns the owner's bytes. B3 removes the six in-band grants and still
issues \BlBThreeFP{} false proofs, all on S6. Ours issues \BlOursTP{} true positives and no
false proof (precision \BlOursPrecision{}\%), at the cost of \BlOursAbstain{} abstentions.
The baselines find all fifteen violations (\BlBaselineRecallAll{}\% total recall), whereas
ours confirms ten---those whose surface is readable---and declines the other five. Over all
\BlBolaCells{} violation cells total recall is \BlOursRecallAll{}\% and coverage falls to
\BlOursCoverage{}\%. A succeeding request is compatible with both a grant and a defect, so a
rule that treats it as proof must either ignore grants (B1, B2) or see only the grants it
created (B3). The out-of-band grant is the residual: on the Gitea snapshot the equality
rule fires on all \BlRsBTwoClaims{} legitimately allowed cells, and the in-band log still
leaves \BlRsBThreeClaims{}. The budget is matched at the probe pair and only
there---\BlProbeReq{} requests per cell for every method, \BlSharedProbes{} shared probes,
and \BlOursRequestsTotal{} requests for ours, \BlOursExtraTotal{} of them extra.
We report no per-method timing: every method is scored from the same recorded responses in
the same process.

\subsection{Admissibility, and falsification}
\label{sec:admissibility}

Three properties must be kept apart. A surface is \emph{readable} when its answer can be
obtained from the vantage in question; \emph{sound} when its answer equals the true
authorization state; and \emph{admissible}, for promotion to \texttt{violation-proven},
when (1)~it is readable \emph{and} (2)~one can give a reason that it is not of the same
kind as the defect under test---not itself a reachability artifact whose blind spot
correlates with the blindness tested. Readability neither implies soundness nor confers
admissibility: the owner-vantage surface is readable on every cell in each system
(\ResRsCellsSnapshot{} Gitea, \GgCells{} Gogs, \GlCells{} GitLab), yet sound on Gitea
alone---it agrees with effective access on \GtOracleAgree{}, \GgOracleAgree{} and
\GlOracleAgree{} of those cells. The cross-vendor systems are therefore not cases of ``no
oracle'': a readable membership surface exists on both, but it is unsound for the
authorization channels exercised here, so it fails clause~(2) (Gogs and GitLab convert the
blind spots into \GgFpVthree{} and \GlFpVthree{} false proofs). Where clause~(2) holds, an
analyst who already has an admissible oracle should consume it directly
(Proposition~\ref{lem:oracle-suff}(b)). Where it fails---the case on every system studied
here---dropping the relation for its direct counterpart is the wrong move: on Gogs
\GgVersion{} and GitLab \GlVersion{} the direct adjudicator produces
\GgFpDirectObserve{} and \GlFpDirectObserve{} false proofs against \GgFpVthree{} and
\GlFpVthree{} for the oracle-gated relation (Table~\ref{tab:gogs-cross}).

Clause~(2) needs an operational procedure---defect classes, four evidence levels, an ordered
\emph{admit}/\emph{reject}/\emph{unknown} rule---fixed before the multi-class and
equal-budget runs of Sections~\ref{sec:multiclass} and~\ref{sec:baselines}, and applied
both to them and, conservatively, to the archived experiments. It is newer than the frozen
temporal protocol and we do not backdate it. Per defect class the rule enumerates the path
producing the enforcement answer $y$ and the path producing the oracle answer $o$:
\emph{admit} if neither contains the other's decision point nor shares a component whose
failure could co-occur with the tested defect, \emph{reject} if such sharing is found,
\emph{unknown} if either path cannot be enumerated. On all three products the route the
oracle consults and the routes that decide the request are answered by the same process
under the same permission model (Gitea \ResRsVersion{}, Gogs \GgVersion{} with its
direct-collaborator-only per-object route, \GgChProbePermSuffix{} for the permission suffix
that would expose the rest, GitLab \GlVersion{} with \texttt{members/all}), so the verdict
is \emph{reject}---at the API level, and also on the controlled target where the source is
readable. Two revisions of the register, logged in place and without changing a verdict,
corrected five of those grounds. Three epistemic statuses stay apart throughout: the
diagnostic $O$/$H$ readings, the classification of every promoted verdict against $E$ and
$Y$, and independence-certified proofs of previously unknown defects, which the rule
refuses here.

Each finding would fall as follows: Finding~1, if the predicates were defined over live
rather than recorded reachability; Finding~2, by an authorization MR needing an observable
that a succeeding request plus a readable oracle cannot produce; Finding~3, by a specificity
in scenario units that still fails to separate the configurations studied here.
