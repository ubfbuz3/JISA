\noindent Dear Associate Editor and Reviewers,

Thank you for the detailed reports. The revision is substantive rather than
cosmetic, and we have kept the narrowing that both reports asked for: the title,
the abstract, the running head and every claim are now restricted to the two
reachability-gated relations that were actually examined. Three statements that
were previously argued in prose are now computed from records and reproduced by
script---the complete factorial design and its reconciliation, the truth table of
the two relations, and a competitive differential baseline. The baseline is
matched to ours at the level of the probe pair only: total request and
information budgets differ (111 requests against the baselines' 84 shared
probes), and the manuscript states the comparison in those terms rather than as
an equal-budget contest. One experiment that had not been run at all (a
competing differential
baseline) has now been run and reported. Where a request could not be met with
the evidence we have, we say so in the manuscript instead of softening the text;
five of the twenty-one items are met only in part, and Section~\ref{sec:partial}
of this letter lists them together with what is missing and why. One of the
five (R1.9) is reported as an open scope question for the editor rather than
as resolved.

References below are to the revised manuscript (30 pages, 24 tables). Table and
section numbers are stable; page numbers are given for the tables most often at
issue. Every number quoted in this letter was checked against the artefact that
generates it---the macros in the analysis output and the raw JSON records they
are computed from. This letter is prose, not a generated file, so the check is
a separate step rather than an automatic consequence of compiling it;
\texttt{check\_letter\_numbers.py} in the package re-runs that comparison.

\subsection{Characterization of authorization-state transitions}
\Concern{The three-phase protocol does not comprehensively model revocation,
inherited permissions, public visibility, role changes and concurrent updates;
the state-transition semantics, the timing of each observation and the
consistency assumptions behind the deterministic false proofs are requested, as
is whether the conclusions hold when authorization changes between request and
adjudication.}
\Response{Section~V-A now states the semantics that the experiment actually
exercises, and three scope limits are stated as part of the result rather than
buried in the discussion. The eight committed transitions are a designed set,
and we list them by the name the run record uses rather than by category:
direct grant, direct revoke, team-to-repository attach, team-member removal,
private-to-public visibility, public-to-private visibility, a read-to-write role
change, and a site-administrator grant. GitLab's set is the same shape with two
of its own: group share and group-member removal. There is no team-detach, no
member addition and no site-administrator revoke in the record; the two
visibility directions are separate events. Each cell is
serialised, no mutation is issued between a cell's request and its probe, so the
window between them is static \emph{by construction}; and a deployment in which a
grant or revoke lands inside that window is outside what these cells measure---for
such a case the cell's conclusion does not apply and the observation must be
repeated, which the manuscript says we do not claim to detect. Revocations behave
differently from grants, and the manuscript is explicit that they must not be
scored as misses: the stale model still lists \TmpBothOverRevoke{} cells whose
access was revoked (\TmpOverRevoke{} on Gitea, \TmpGOverRevoke{} on GitLab), and
in every one of them the request is refused, so no violation occurred and
nothing was missed---scoring them as false negatives would require
$E=\text{deny}$ and $Y=\text{allow}$ together, which never happens here
(Table~12, p.~15). On concurrency, the harness is single-threaded, no concurrent
writer ran, and $hv$ is declared to be a harness-maintained logical version
rather than an atomic server snapshot---which is what makes the still window
meaningful.}
\Action{Section~V-A (Table~12, p.~15); the same scope limits restated in
Section~VIII.}

\subsection{Operationalizing the admissibility condition}
\Concern{Clause~(2)---that the oracle is not affected by the same defect under
investigation---remains a qualitative judgement; a reproducible procedure for
determining admissibility \emph{before} examining outcomes is requested.}
\Response{We agree this is the weakest link. In this revision we give
clause~(2) an operational procedure---an \emph{admission rule}---and apply it
conservatively to the archived experiments
(Section~VI-E, ``An oracle-independence admission rule, applied
retrospectively''). We state its epistemic status plainly: the rule is
\emph{proposed in this revision}, not extracted from the pre-registered
protocol. The protocol pinned by digest did record the qualitative judgement
(\texttt{oracle\_independence=shared}; the oracle read for diagnosis only),
but it did not contain the procedure, and we do not backdate it. The rule
itself: fix the target defect class; enumerate, at the level the product's own
documentation and observable behaviour support, the path that produces the
enforcement answer and the path that produces the oracle answer; \emph{admit}
if neither path contains the other's decision point nor shares a component
whose failure could co-occur with the tested defect; \emph{reject} on such
sharing; \emph{unknown}---refusing independence-based promotion---where either
path cannot be enumerated at that level. Applying it: on Gitea the permission
route the oracle consults and the content routes are answered by the same
server binary applying the same permission model; on Gogs the per-object route
enumerates direct collaborators only and the permission suffix that would
expose the remaining channels does not exist (\GgChProbePermSuffix); on GitLab
the \texttt{members/all} route and the content routes are answered by the same
application consulting the same membership tables. The enumeration stops at
each server boundary---we did not audit internal source, so the finer-grained
verdict would be \emph{unknown}---and the conservative reading available at
the API level is recorded as \emph{reject} on all three products. Three
epistemic statuses are kept apart in the manuscript: the diagnostic
$O$/$H$ readings (run under the recorded \texttt{shared} status); the post-hoc
gate-correctness classification against the independent ledger and content
observation (which needs no oracle independence); and
independence-certified proofs of previously unknown defects, which the rule
refuses here. No recorded number changes under the rule.}
\Action{Section~VI-E (admission rule, per-product path evidence, and the three
epistemic statuses); the pre-registered protocol and the amendment timing note
remain in the reproducibility package as the earlier, qualitative record.}
\Remains{The rule is retrospective and its evidence base is API-observable
behaviour, not an internal-source audit; a finer-grained per-component
enumeration would be needed for anything beyond the conservative reject
recorded here. This is listed as a limitation in Section~VIII.}

\subsection{Proposition 2: separating the definitional from the empirical}
\Concern{The proposition risks being tautological; the formal identity should be
distinguished from the empirical premise that the evaluated relations consume no
additional decision-relevant information, with a truth-table derivation or
explicit functional representation.}
\Response{The result is now split in two. \textbf{Lemma~1} (absorption) is the
algebraic part: if a verdict is a function of an oracle answer and an ordinary
request, it cannot outperform the direct consumer of those same inputs. That part
is definitional and is labelled as such. \textbf{Proposition~2} is the empirical
part, and it is deliberately narrower than the lemma's domain: premise~(i) of
the lemma---that the alarm reads only the crawl-time model and the
response---holds for these two relations by source inspection, and on the domain
studied both alarms reduce to $y\wedge\neg m$ against $D=y\wedge\neg o$. What is
verified empirically is that predicate reduction. What holds algebraically is
$A=D$ if and only if $y=0$ or $m=o$, together with $H\Rightarrow D$.
Premise~(ii) of the lemma---that the model is $O$-determined---is \emph{not}
claimed: it fails exactly on the stale cells, which is row~7 of Table~18, and the
manuscript says so rather than presenting the relation as informationally
redundant in general. The explicit predicates arewritten out ($A_{002}=\neg sup(B,A)\wedge\neg m\wedge\neg err(o_1)\wedge
q(o_1,o_2)$ and $A_{004}=y\wedge\neg m$, against $D=y\wedge\neg o$), and on the
domain studied both reduce to $y\wedge\neg m$. The reduction was verified
mechanically against 576 constructed records and 712 temporal cells with zero
mismatches; the generating script exits rather than emitting the table if any
record disagrees. The equivalence is stated correctly as $A=D \Leftrightarrow
y=0 \vee m=o$: where $D$ is defined the two verdicts coincide in 530 of the 584
readable cells and part in the two rows with $Y{=}$allow and $M\neq O$---40 cells
where the model is right and the oracle wrong, and 14 where the model is stale
and the oracle right. Those two rows are the two failure mechanisms the
cross-vendor section keeps apart. The falsification condition is stated: produce
an observable, outside what a succeeding request plus a readable oracle can
determine, on which an authorization metamorphic relation still decides.}
\Action{Section~VI-B with Lemma~1, Proposition~2, Table~18 (p.~20) and the
corrected equivalence; the earlier claim of extensional equality is deleted.}

\subsection{Comparison against competitive black-box methods}
\Concern{\texttt{direct\_only} is deliberately unfair and \texttt{obs\_success} and
\texttt{always\_alarm} are intentionally weak; a differential, multi-account
authorization tester probing equivalent objects under distinct privilege levels
is requested, with matched query budgets, execution costs and detection
outcomes.}
\Response{This is the one comment that required a new experiment, and it has been
run. Section~VI-D and Table~19 (p.~22) report three conventional differential
rules on the same 42 controlled cells (six configurations crossed with S1--S7)
and, critically, on the \emph{same probe pair}: every method consumes one request
as the owner and one as the actor---\BlSharedProbes{} shared probes in all---and
ours issues one additional read of the
authorization surface on the 27 cells where a relation alarms and none where it
does not (0.64 per cell on average, \BlOursExtraTotal{} reads in total). The
budget is therefore matched at the level of the probe pair and only there: ours
issues \BlOursRequestsTotal{} requests against the baselines' \BlSharedProbes{},
and it is the only method that reads the target's authorization surface, so
total request count, visible information and privilege all differ. We report the
comparison as one between adjudication trade-offs at different information
costs, not as a matched-budget detection contest.
\emph{B1 status-only} alarms whenever the
replayed request returns a non-error. \emph{B2 equality replay} alarms only when
the owner succeeded and the actor received byte-identical output---the
differential rule with no authorization model. \emph{B3} adds the strongest
information a tester can hold without reading the target: it suppresses every
cell covered by the tester's own share log. On these cells B1 and B2 claim on all
27 allowed cells with 12 false proofs each (precision 55.56\%), B3 claims 21 with
6 false proofs (71.43\%), all six on the out-of-band channel, and ours claims 10
with no false proof but 17 abstentions (coverage 59.52\%). Recall is reported in
the table under both denominators, because the two differ exactly where ours
abstains: on the cells ours rules on, recall is 100\%; over all 15 violation
cells, including the 5 it abstains on, total recall is 66.67\%. The baselines
abstain on nothing, so both of their figures are 100\%. Two readings are
explicitly ruled out in the text: this is \emph{not} a detection gain, because
the baselines find all fifteen violations while ours confirms the ten whose
surface is readable and declines the other five; and it is \emph{not} a free
improvement, because coverage falls to 59.52\%. A corroborating observation on a
real system is reported with the same qualification: on the Gitea snapshot the
equality rule fires on all 25 legitimately allowed cells and the in-band log
still leaves 24, because the framework log does not observe 0.96 of the effective
access there---a property of the constructed topology, not an estimate of field
prevalence.}
\Action{Section~VI-D and Table~19 (p.~22); the runner
(\texttt{experiments/run\_baseline.py}) and its raw output are in the
reproducibility package, and both baseline tables are reproduced byte-identically
by \texttt{verify\_artifact.sh repro}.}
\Remains{No per-method wall-clock time. The complete comparison---both probes per
cell and the additional reads included---ran in \BlRunSeconds{}~s of wall-clock
harness time covering setup, probing, scoring and cleanup, and that figure is
recorded in the run output shipped with the artefact. The runner binds no CPU and
measures no per-method CPU time, so we make no claim about single-core cost. We
do not report a time per method because all four are scored from the
same two recorded responses in the same process, so any per-method number would
measure our replay loop rather than the rule. What the reviewer's question needs
beyond that---the cost of the same probes against a live target---is reported for
the real-system temporal loops only (Gitea 21.2~s, GitLab 7.3~s, of which the
probes are 20.7~s and 6.0~s), where the requests really do reach a server.
Beyond the wall-clock point, the budget condition of the request is met only at
the probe-pair level: the reviewer asked for matched query budgets, execution
costs and detection outcomes, and what is implemented matches the two probes
per cell while total request count, visible information and privilege differ
(111 against 84). We state this in the manuscript and claim no equal-budget
detection superiority; a full equal-total-budget design was not built.}

\subsection{Detection results across independently implemented defects}
\Concern{The three seeded instances share one fault pattern; the positive control
should be expanded to multiple independently implemented authorization defects,
including unauthorized reads, unauthorized modifications, inherited-permission
errors and privilege escalation, with per-defect recall and false-positive
rates.}
\Response{We did not implement multiple independent defects, and the manuscript
says so. What was added is a real vulnerable/fixed pair rather than another
seeded instance: a publicly fixed defect in Gitea (object-versus-path ownership
in the comment-permission check, fixed between 1.20.5 and 1.20.6), driven through
the same adjudication gate. The verdict uses the ledger's entitlement and the
content-level observation: on 1.20.5 the gate yields one true positive and no
false proof; on 1.20.6 it yields no alarm and no claim; the twelve control checks
(ten expected responses across the two builds and two recovery checks) all behave
as expected. The generating script is fail-closed in four layers, and each layer
is now checked rather than asserted: the required label set and its uniqueness
(one main probe plus five controls per build), the control responses, the
recovery outcome, and---the layer that matters most---a recomputation of $Y$ from
the owner's re-read of the comment body rather than from any cached field or
status code, followed by a re-run of the same \texttt{classify} and a
field-by-field comparison against the record. A regression test
(\texttt{test\_cve\_pair\_validation.py}, run by \texttt{reproduce.sh}) applies
seven perturbations, plus an unchanged-record control, to in-memory copies---a deleted control, a failed recovery, a
re-read body reverted to the original while the cached verdict still says true
positive, a control returning 403, duplicated and unregistered labels, and a
ledger conflict---and requires each to be rejected. The manuscript is explicit that this is a positive control of the
\emph{gate}---an independent entitlement deny together with a content-level
allow---and not evidence about the relations' recall on real defects, and the
conclusion states that a stronger claim needs a \emph{population} of real
defects, which a single pair cannot provide. The seeded control is also bounded

\subsection{Heterogeneous scenario weights}
\Concern{Proposition~1 assumes equal contributions per scenario; the result
should be extended to unequal request counts, correlated inputs and different
alarm counts per scenario, including the case where the number of generated
inputs depends on scenario complexity or detector behaviour.}
\Response{The fixed-weight case is derived (Proposition~1 and its corollary,
Section~IV-E) and the arbitrary-share case is bounded without any equal-weight
assumption: whatever the class shares are, a failing class contributing a share
$w$ of the negative inputs moves the figure only within $[1-w,1]$
(Section~VI-C). Our records are a fixed enumeration in which the input count
does not respond to the detector, so the endogenous case is not instantiated by
any experiment here; what this revision adds is a conditional derivation of
what an endogenous count can certify, stated below. Rather than assert a
generality we
cannot support, Section~VIII states the limitation: records within a design cell
are not independent, the records are a fixed enumeration, and we therefore report
counts and rates rather than inferential intervals, with no result resting on
statistical significance. In this revision we additionally give the endogenous
count its own conditional treatment (Section~VI, ``What an endogenous count
certifies, conditionally''): for a method $D$ and run $\omega$ with
$n_i(D,\omega)$ negative inputs and $f_i(D,\omega)$ false proofs per scenario,
the score $S=1-\sum_i f_i/\sum_i n_i$ is a counting identity per fixed run; for
a family of runs, $S_k\to 1$ if and only if
$\sum_i f_i/\sum_i n_i\to 0$ with the denominator positive. Two arithmetic
examples bound the interpretation: a fixed number of false proofs under a
growing input total gives $S_k=1-1/k\to 1$ (the failure persists, only its
weight vanishes), while false proofs growing as $\alpha k$ give $S_k=1-\alpha$
at every $k$ (no dilution toward~1). The derivation is conditional on the
run's scenario coverage---an unexercised failing class contributes nothing to
either sum---and we do not use it as a detection guarantee.}
\Action{Section~IV-E and Section~VI-C; the limitation stated in Section~VIII.}
\Remains{The endogenous derivation is per-run arithmetic plus an asymptotic
