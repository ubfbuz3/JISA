p12	A. EIGHT COMMITTED TRANSITIONS WITH A FROZEN
p12	MODEL, ON TWO PRODUCTS
p12	The two-snapshot comparison above cannot separate two
p12	explanations: the model went stale, or the crawl never listed
p12	the channel in the first place. The transition experiment
p12	separates them by construction. We ran eight authorisation
p12	transitions on one fresh Gitea 1.22.6 instance and repeated
p12	the same eight, under the same pre-registered protocol (hash
p12	d09d904707aa) and the same ledger rules, on an indepen-
p12	dent GitLab CE 17.11.7 instance. The mechanisms differ per
p12	product—attaching a repository to a team becomes sharing
p12	a project with a group; removing a team member becomes
p12	removing a group member—so the two runs share a protocol,
p12	not a topology, and we report them side by side rather than
p12	[short]	pooled.
p12	Each transition is a closed loop. We crawl every subject,
p12	serialise the per-subject reachable set, freeze it as M0 and
p12	record its digest; commit one authorisation transaction and
p12	increment the harness version hv only if the transaction re-
p12	turns 2xx; re-crawl with the same credentials to obtain M1;
p12	read the oracle from the owner vantage; issue the request and
p12	verify the effect by content; and classify against the ledger at
p12	decision time. All 16 transactions committed, all 16 frozen
p12	M0 digests were unchanged when re-checked immediately
p12	before probing, and all 16 cycles were version-consistent.
p12	Across 712 cross-credential cells (280 on Gitea plus 432 on
p12	GitLab), E and Y agree in 712 cases, so no cell is scored on
p12	a modelling discrepancy.
p12	The stale model produces 29 false proofs (13 on Gitea, 16
p12	on GitLab), and a recrawl with the same credential removes
p12	29 of them, leaving 0 still missed and 0 live false proofs (Ta-
p12	ble 12). The composition is informative rather than uniform,
p12	and the two products agree on its shape: one cell for the direct
p12	grant, one for the inherited-channel attachment, five and six
p12	when a repository becomes public, and six and eight when a
p12	user becomes site administrator. The last two are the cases a
p12	single-object probe is least likely to anticipate, because the
p12	granting event is not about the object at all.
p12	Revocations behave differently and must not be scored as
p12	misses. The old model still lists 17 cells whose access was
p12	revoked (7 on Gitea, 10 on GitLab). In every one of them the
p12	request is refused, so no violation occurred; treating them as
p12	false negatives would require E = deny and Y = allow,
p12	which never happens here.
p12	The multi-channel transition is where the products part
p12	company, and the difference is itself a result. On Gitea, after
p12	the team member is removed, acme/t1 remains readable
p12	through a direct collaboration while acme/t2 becomes un-
p12	readable, and the ledger records the remaining channel. On
p12	GitLab the same test has to be moved outside the group
p12	namespace: removing a member from a group also deletes
p12	that user’s project-level membership in the group’s projects—
p12	the project’s direct-member list went from one entry to
p12	none—so a direct grant placed inside the group would have
p12	measured that product rule instead of multi-channel access.
p12	We therefore placed the alternative channel on a personal
p12	project, and we record the cascade as a product-semantics
p12	observation rather than encoding it as a ledger rule (amend-
p12	ment A2 in the protocol record). This is the kind of non-local
p12	side effect that makes a hand-maintained expectation model
p12	wrong in a way no single probe can reveal.
p12	For the role change, the read cell is unchanged while the
p12	write probe goes from HTTP 403 with unchanged content
p12	to HTTP 201 with the owner re-reading the new bytes; the
p12	owner then deletes the probe branch and observes 404, and
p12	the repository’s README digest is unchanged after cleanup.
p12	The oracle column separates the products more sharply
p12	than the transition counts. On Gitea the owner vantage an-
p12	swers 224 of 280 cells (the remainder are anonymous, for
p12	which no oracle credential exists), but the subject’s own
p12	vantage answers only 7—all of them after the site-admin
p12	grant; outside it, a read collaborator receives HTTP 403 and
p12	an outsider HTTP 404. On GitLab the owner vantage answers
p12	360 of 432, and there the subject’s own credential answers
p12	360: GitLab’s member endpoint is readable by any project
p12	member. That readability is not soundness. The endpoint
p12	answers ‘‘is this principal a member’’, so it returns 404 for a
p12	public project a non-member may read and for a site adminis-
p12	trator who may read everything. Measured against the ledger,
p12	the Gitea oracle disagrees on 0 cells and the GitLab oracle
p12	on 53, all of the latter in the public (44) or site-administrator
p12	(9) channel. A readable oracle can therefore be systematically
p12	wrong in exactly the channels that are cheapest to overlook,
p12	which is why we never let it set E. The two calibration regimes
p12	record this rather than hide it: 6 known cells on Gitea, and
p12	on GitLab 4 gated cells plus two recorded diagnostics for the
p12	secondary vantage (amendment A1).
p12	A channel-by-channel view makes the mechanism explicit
p12	(Table 11), for Gitea and GitLab. Each cell is assigned to
p12	exactly one channel, and the rows sum to the cell count, so

p12	the ability to enumerate those grants through that route, which

p13	Section V-B, now located in a named channel rather than

p13	Two properties of the run are recorded because they are
p13	what an external check can verify. The protocol was frozen
p13	before the first Gitea transition, and its digest is carried in both
p13	run records. For the amendments we establish the ordering
p13	by content binding rather than by a self-reported clock: the
p13	archived pilot records only A1, and the reported GitLab run
p13	records the digests of both A1 and A2, so A2’s content was
p13	in place when that run executed. The pilot is archived with
p13	its cells excluded from every denominator. One recorded
p13	field does not support the stronger reading and we do not
p13	use it: A2’s written_at says 02:32, later than the run’s
p13	last probe at 02:26:14, so the file’s own timestamp cannot
p13	establish that it was written before the first transition. We
p13	retain the field as recorded and rely on the digest carried
p13	by the run; the discrepancy is documented with the artifact
p13	rather than corrected in place. Inside a run, every cell carries
p13	its event sequence number, the harness version before and
p13	after the transition, the M0 and M1 digests and wall-clock
p13	timestamps, so the ordering—crawl, freeze, mutate, recrawl,
p13	oracle, probe—can be re-checked line by line rather than
p13	trusted. Every observation in that loop is a GET; the only
p13	mutating calls are the transitions themselves, and each is
p13	recorded with its status code. Failed observations are not
p13	dropped silently: a transport error after two retries is recorded
p13	as such and excluded from the scored set, a mutation outside
p13	2xx excludes its whole transition, and an unreadable oracle is
p13	recorded as unknown with the cell still scored on E and Y.
p13	Three scope limits are part of the result. First, the verdict
p13	is anchored to the request outcome, and the transitions are
p13	serialised: no mutation is issued between a cell’s request
p13	and its probe, so the window between them is static by
p13	construction. A deployment in which a grant or revoke lands
p13	between the request and the adjudication is outside what these
p13	cells measure; for such a case the cell’s conclusion does not
p13	apply and the observation has to be repeated, and we do not
p13	claim that the harness would detect the interleaving. Second,
p13	0 residual is a count on these surfaces, not a completeness
p13	claim: the crawl sources themselves define what can be seen,
p13	deploy keys are outside the ledger, and we did not construct
p13	a channel that the recrawl is known to miss. Third, hv is
p13	a harness-maintained logical version, not an atomic server
p13	snapshot; the harness is single-threaded and no concurrent
p13	writer ran, which is what makes the still window meaningful.
p13	Third, the eight transitions are a designed set, not a sample of
p13	deployment changes, so none of the counts is a field rate.

p19	C. FINDING 3 (RQ3): A POOLED SPECIFICITY CANNOT
p19	REGISTER A CLASS IT DID NOT EXERCISE
p19	The reported 99.81% is a specificity over follow-up inputs,
p19	TN/(TN + FP), not a false-alarm share of all generated
p19	inputs; in that evaluation the MT failures were manually ver-
p19	ified, so the labels are not the crawler’s own. Its denominator
p19	cannot, on its own, provide scenario-level resolution for a
p19	failure confined to a class, and the inputs it counts are the
p19	ones the crawl could enumerate. Both halves are consistent
p19	with the reported value. The denominator: with FN = 0, the
p19	figure is 1 −FP/(F −TP), so a failure uniform across a
p19	class contributes only as many false positives as that class
p19	emits negative inputs and is diluted by the rest of the run
p19	(Proposition 1; Corollary 1: no fixed threshold can guarantee
p19	that such a class forces the metric below it). The coverage: if
p19	a class of legitimate access is never reached by the crawl, it
p19	contributes no negative inputs, so the ratio is silent about it
p19	even though the adjudication that consumes the crawl is not.
p19	Nor does the report fix the weights: if a failing class supplies a
p19	share w of the negative inputs, the figure moves only between
p19	1 and 1 −w, so telling a small-share failure apart from no
p19	failure requires an estimate of w that the published number
p19	does not carry.
p19	We are not saying the figure is false, that its labels are
p19	wrong, or that the false positives were misdiagnosed: the
p19	manual verification is exactly what makes the labels credible.
p19	The claim is narrower—that a pooled figure computed on the
p19	crawled input set does not by itself certify that a class outside
p19	that set was exercised, and that a class-uniform failure can be
p19	held above any fixed threshold by unaffected inputs.

Limits of Metamorphic Testing for Object-Level
Authorization: Temporal Reachability, Oracle
Admissibility, and Measurement Resolution
ABSTRACT Broken object-level authorization (BOLA) is the top-ranked API security risk, yet detecting it
is an oracle problem: vulnerable and correct systems answer identically. Metamorphic testing is a leading
technique for that problem in Web systems; for the relations we study, the predicates delegate the verdict to a
GUI-reachability model—a crawl-time snapshot of what the interface permits. We instantiate the published
relations as defined and drive constructed and real systems through crawl, out-of-band change, and execution.
Three findings follow. (1) Access granted out of band after the crawl is reported as a proven violation—a false
proof —in 8 of 8 constructed alarms, because an out-of-band grant fits only the wrong one of the predicate’s
two representations. (2) Repairing this with a readable, sound oracle that fully resolves the proposition leaves
the metamorphic step adding no observed adjudication benefit over the direct consumer of the same oracle
and outcome; the paired methods disagree on only 3 of 216 true-violation records, all favouring the plain
request. (3) The reported specificity of 99.81% is not conditioned on a check that the reachability model
equals the authorization state, and its denominator cannot register a scenario-uniform failure.
The findings come from a 576-record scenario/configuration study on a constructed system, together with a
controlled cell study on Gitea 1.22.6, extended, with a methodological variation, to Gogs 0.14.3 and GitLab
17.11.7; an endogenous control holds final visibility fixed, and the published relations reproduce the false

Proposition 2 (adjudication of the evaluated relations is or-
acle-determined). Let O be an authorization oracle that is
readable (its answer can be obtained from the vantage in
question) and sound (its answer equals the true permission),
and let R be the observed outcome of an ordinary request.
For the authorization relations and scenarios evaluated here—
whose predicates read the crawl-time reachability model M
and the response, and nothing else—once O is readable,
sound and complete for the proposition under test, M supplies
no decision-relevant authorization information that (O, R)
does not already determine: agreement with the true state
is redundant, and disagreement makes the verdict unsound
(Finding 1). The verdict of the metamorphic family is there-
fore a function f (O, R) of O and R alone, and any adjudicator
of that form is extensionally equal to the direct consumer
D := f (O, R)—the two are the same function applied to the
same arguments. A metamorphic step can be shown to be
necessary only by exhibiting an observable it consumes that
the direct consumer does not.

C. FINDING 3 (RQ3): THE REPORTED SPECIFICITY IS NOT
CONDITIONED ON THE CORRESPONDENCE IT ASSUMES
The reported 99.81% is a statement about a corpus of gen-
erated inputs; it is not conditioned on an explicit check that
the reachability model coincides with the true authorization
state, and its input-level denominator cannot provide sta-
ble scenario-level resolution for a scenario-uniform failure.
The omission: adjudication is a function of the reachability
model, so where model and true state diverge—the out-of-
band case of Finding 1—the figure measures agreement with
the tool’s own crawler; the authors concede the mapping
is ‘‘not always available’’ [2], but the number is reported
without the condition. The denominator: a failure uniform
across a scenario class contributes only as many false pos-
itives as that class emits inputs, and is diluted by the rest of
the run (Proposition 1: in our approximation the configuration
that misreports a whole class of legitimate access still scores
84.62%; Corollary 1: no fixed threshold can guarantee that
such a class forces the metric below it). We are not saying the
figure is false or the false positives misdiagnosed; the claim
is that such a metric does not itself validate the reachability-
to-authorization correspondence on which its interpretation
depends, and that a class-uniform failure can be held above
any fixed threshold by unaffected inputs.
